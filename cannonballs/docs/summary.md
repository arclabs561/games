How many cannonballs are in a pile, and which piles hold a square number of them? A square pile of 24 layers holds {{pile24.balls}} balls, which also make a flat square of {{pile24.root}} by {{pile24.root}}. No other square pile does, apart from the single ball. Watson proved this in 1918, and the search here finds no other below 10<sup>{{bounds.digits}}</sup> layers. Some oblong pile of short side a holds a square number of balls for 7 in 12 values of a, and never for a = 2 or 3. That rule is proved here.
