How many cannonballs are in a pile, and which piles hold a square number of them? A square pile of 24 layers holds {{pile24.balls}} balls, which also make a flat square of {{pile24.root}} by {{pile24.root}}. No other square pile does, apart from the single ball. Watson proved this in 1918; a search here below 10<sup>{{bounds.digits}}</sup> layers agrees, and serves as a check of the code. The single ball is also the only square pile that holds a cube number of balls, by a short argument from a theorem of Euler. Some oblong pile of short side a holds a square number of balls for 7/12 of all short sides in the long run, and never for a = 2 or 3. That rule is elementary, and is proved here.
