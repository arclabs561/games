| Square piles that are cubes | Only the single ball | Proved<!--raw-typst  (@prop-cubes)-->, using Euler's theorem that $x^3 + y^3 = 2z^3$ only when $x = y$ or $x = -y$; Bennett (2001) gives it too. A search agrees up to 10<sup>18</sup> layers |
| Oblong piles that are squares | Some length works for short side $a$ exactly when $a$ leaves remainder 1 on division by 3, or the multiple of 3 among $a$ and $a + 1$, divided by 3, is $9^k(3j + 2)$. | Oblong piles on an $a$ by $a + c$ base, of two or more layers, that are squares, for $c$ from 1 to 20 | Some exist for $c$ = {{oblong_shapes_with_square:and}}, and none for the other $c$; for $c = 2$ they have {{oblong_shapes.2:and}} layers (all lists are in `results.json`) | Computed exactly as the whole-number points of one elliptic curve for each $c$, with Sage (`curves.py`)<!--raw-typst  (@tab-shapes)-->; a search of every $a$ up to {{bounds.shapes}} agrees |
| Oblong piles on an $a$ by $2a - 1$ base that are squares | {{oblong_ridge:and}} layers | Cited: an answer to Puzzling Stack Exchange question 99084; recomputed the same way with Sage, and a search up to {{bounds.shapes}} layers agrees |
| Square piles that are triangles | {{coincidences.square pile / triangle:and}} layers | Cited: Finkelstein and London (1972)<!--raw-typst  (@tab-other)-->, OEIS A053611; reproduced by search up to {{bounds.coincide}} layers |
| Tetrahedral piles that are squares | {{coincidences.tetrahedral / square:and}} layers | Cited: Meyl (1878); reproduced by search up to {{bounds.coincide}} layers |
