Two friends play a game with no draws, such as a Peggle duel, and one wins k of n games: what is the chance that this player is the better one? With a uniform prior it equals the chance of at most k heads in n + 1 fair coin flips, so six wins in ten give {{coins.six_of_ten.0}}/{{coins.six_of_ten.1:d}}, about {{coins.six_of_ten_value:.3f}}. Playing on until the posterior gives one player a 95% chance of being better declares one of two equal players better with chance {{equal_verdict.posterior.100:.3f}} within 100 games; playing until the Bayes factor reaches 20 keeps that under 0.05 however long they play. Exact, by Bayes's billiard-table argument and a dynamic program over (wins, games).
