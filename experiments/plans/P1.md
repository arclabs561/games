# P1. Pokémon: does the type-chart equilibrium predict which types people play?

## Question

On the Pokémon Showdown ladder, do typings with more weight in the duel game's equilibrium get used more? And do they get used more than the number of Pokémon available with each typing would predict?

## Why it matters

`pokemon/README.md` solves the type chart as a game. In the duel over existing typings the equilibrium is unique, on about two dozen typings. The README never asks whether players behave that way. Real choices depend on base stats, abilities, moves, items and, in Generation 9, Terastallization, none of which the chart models. So the answer tells how much of type choice the chart alone explains.

- A clear positive result would make the equilibrium a useful summary of type value, beyond a curiosity.
- A null result, the more likely outcome, is still informative. It says that type matchups, as the chart encodes them, are not what drives use, and the README should say so next to the equilibrium tables.
- Generation 1 is the cleaner test. It has no abilities, items or Tera, and a different chart. A result there and not in Generation 9 would point at the mechanics the model leaves out.

## Data

**Source: Smogon's monthly usage statistics** (smogon.com/stats), the "chaos" JSON files. For each format, month and rating cutoff, a file lists every Pokémon used on the Showdown ladder, with its weighted share of teams and its raw count. Smogon computes them from every rated ladder battle on the official server (the counting scripts are public: Smogon-Usage-Stats on GitHub). Weighted stats count a battle by the chance that the player's rating is above the cutoff; cutoff 0 counts every battle equally. Files exist for every month since 2014.

**Alternatives considered:**

| Source | What | Terms | Why not the primary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metamon parsed and raw replays (Hugging Face `jakegrigsby/metamon-*`) | about 2.7 million public Showdown replays, usernames replaced by pseudonyms, Gen 1–4 and Gen 9 OU and some other tiers | parsed replays CC BY-NC 4.0; the raw-replay card states no license | only replays that players chose to upload, a selected subset; far larger than needed for team shares |
| PokéChamp dataset (`milkkarten/pokechamp`) | 2 million battles, 37 formats, 2024–2025 | no license stated on the card | same selection issue, and no license |

Replays would allow a bootstrap over battles. But usage shares from hundreds of thousands of battles a month have negligible sampling error, and the uncertainty that matters is between typings and between months. Usage statistics cover every rated battle, not a self-selected subset.

**Terms.** Smogon states no license for the statistics. None was found on the stats index or in the usage-statistics FAQ thread on its forums. The pkmn/smogon project, whose code is MIT, writes that "the aggregated stats information is freely available in the public domain". That is a third party's statement, not Smogon's. So: the raw files are not committed. A workflow downloads them and records each file's SHA-256. What is committed is a derived table (usage share by typing, per format and month) with credit to smogon.com/stats. The README will state that the license of the source is unknown.

**Formats, months and cutoffs, fixed now.**

- Primary: `gen9ou`, cutoff 1500, the 12 months 2025-11 to 2026-10, pooled with equal weight per month.
- Secondary: `gen1ou` with the Generation 1 chart and Generation 1 typings; cutoffs 0, 1695 and 1825 for both formats; each month separately.

**Typings.** Showdown names are mapped to the identifiers in `pokemon/pokeapi_forms.csv` by `pokedex.py`'s rules, with a short committed alias table for names that differ. The typing used is the form's typing in that generation (`pokedex.typing(form, g)`). Any name that cannot be mapped stops the run until the alias table covers it. As a check, the typings are compared with Showdown's own `pokedex.ts` (MIT) for every mapped form.

## Design

- **Usage share of a typing** in a month: the sum of the weighted team shares of the Pokémon with that typing, divided by 6, so that the shares sum to 1.
- **The equilibrium.** The duel game of `equilibrium.duel_matrix` over the typings that appear in the format in the pooled year, with the generation's chart. It is solved by `exact_equilibrium`. If it is unique (`is_unique`), its weights are used. If not, the midpoint of each typing's weight range (`weight_ranges`) is used, and the ends of the ranges give a sensitivity check.
- **Baseline: availability.** For each typing, the number of distinct Pokémon with that typing that appear in the format. A typing with more Pokémon gets more use for that reason alone.
- **Second baseline (secondary): stats.** For each typing, the highest base-stat total among those Pokémon.

## Primary metric and test

Δρ = ρ(equilibrium weight, usage share) − ρ(availability, usage share), with ρ the Spearman correlation over the typings in `gen9ou`. 95% interval by bootstrap over typings, resampling typings with their three values together (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The equilibrium is said to predict use beyond availability if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- ρ(equilibrium, usage) alone, with its interval, and the same for the stats baseline.
- **Support mass.** The share of usage on the equilibrium's support, against the share of available Pokémon whose typing is in the support.
- The same numbers for `gen1ou`, for the other cutoffs, and per month (12 values, to show stability).
- The attack game's defender mix over the format's typings (`attack_matrix`), compared with usage the same way.
- The ten most used typings with zero equilibrium weight, and the reason the equilibrium drops each: which typings beat it, from `payoffs_against_columns`.

## Sample size

The unit is the typing. `gen9ou` uses perhaps 80 to 120 typings in a year. With n typings, the standard error of a Spearman correlation is roughly 1/√(n − 1), about 0.1, so an interval is about ±0.2 wide. Only a large effect can be seen. That is a property of the question, not of the sample: there are only so many typings. `gen1ou` has far fewer, so it is reported without a test.

## Seeds

The equilibrium and the shares are exact given the data. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

CPU. The download (about 24 chaos files for the primary, about 100 with the secondary cutoffs, each a few megabytes to tens of megabytes) runs on GitHub Actions, because the machine that writes the README cannot reach smogon.com. The workflow on `compute/pokemon-p1` downloads, hashes, builds the per-typing table, and commits that table to the branch. The equilibrium over about 100 typings takes a minute or two here. Cost $0; cap one workflow run of under 30 minutes, rerun once at most.

## Entry points

To be written:

- `pokemon/usage.py`: `chaos_url(format, month, cutoff)`, `typing_shares(chaos_json, generation)`, the alias table, and `python -m pokemon.usage build --formats gen9ou gen1ou --months 2025-11..2026-10 --cutoffs 0 1500 1695 1825 --out pokemon/results/usage-typings.json`, run by the workflow.
- `python -m pokemon.p1 --usage pokemon/results/usage-typings.json --out pokemon/results/p1.json`: equilibria, correlations and bootstrap.
- Tests: `typing_shares` on a hand-written chaos file of three Pokémon; the alias table covers every name in the downloaded files; the bootstrap against exact enumeration on four typings.

## Outputs

- In git: `pokemon/results/usage-typings.json` (typing, format, month, cutoff, share, number of Pokémon, and the SHA-256 of each source file) and `pokemon/results/p1.json`. Also a README section with the answer.
- In the private dataset: nothing; the source files are re-downloadable and pinned by hash.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The months, formats and cutoff of the primary metric are fixed above and are not changed after the data is seen. If the interval for Δρ includes 0, the README states that the equilibrium does not predict use beyond the number of Pokémon available, with the interval and the support-mass comparison. If Smogon has not published a month by the time of the run, the twelve months end at the latest month published, and the plan's amendment section records the change before the download.

## Threats to validity

- **The model leaves out most of the game.** Stats, abilities, moves, items and Tera all shape use, so a null result is expected. Generation 1 is the partial control.
- **Tiers.** OU bans strong Pokémon, which removes some typings from use for reasons unrelated to the chart. The equilibrium is computed over the typings present, so a banned typing is out of both sides, but a typing present only through weak Pokémon still counts as available.
- **Single-typing Pokémon** count as one typing, and dual typings as their pair, as in the README's games. Forms such as Megas are not on the Generation 9 ladder; regional and other forms are mapped one by one.
- **Weighting.** Shares at cutoff 1500 weight strong players more. The cutoffs 0 and 1825 bracket the choice.

## Amendments

**2026-11-19, before any download.**

- **Generation 1 cutoffs.** Smogon publishes `gen1ou` at cutoffs 0, 1500, 1630 and 1760, not 1695 and 1825; only `gen9ou` (and `gen9doublesou`) has 1695 and 1825. This was read from the chaos index pages for 2025-11 and 2026-10. So `gen1ou` uses 0, 1500, 1630 and 1760, and its main cutoff is 1500, as for `gen9ou`. The `gen9ou` cutoffs are unchanged. A missing secondary file is recorded and skipped; a missing primary file stops the run.
- **Months.** 2026-10 is published, so the twelve months stand.
- **What "appears" means.** A chaos file lists only Pokémon above a small weighted usage (0.01% in Smogon's public counting scripts). A Pokémon is available if it is listed in any of the twelve files at the same cutoff. The sum of the listed shares, which the plan expects to be 6, is recorded for every file.
- **Names.** Cosmetic formes (Gastrodon-East) count as their base species, using Showdown's own marking of them. The check against Showdown's `pokedex.ts` covers every name in the files, in the format's generation, through Showdown's per-generation mods. The Arceus and Silvally formes are not mapped; neither is legal in these formats.
- **Entry points.** `pokemon.usage build` takes `--cutoffs gen9ou:0,1500,1695,1825 gen1ou:0,1500,1630,1760` in place of one list for both formats. The workflow on `compute/pokemon-p1` runs when its file is pushed, in place of by hand.
- **Exploratory, outside the plan.** Two comparisons are added and labeled exploratory, with no test: the log-ratio game over typings (k = 1 and 2), and each game over the 18 (or 15) single types, against usage by single type, where a Pokémon's share is split equally between its two types. They were asked for after the plan was merged. They do not change the primary metric.
