# H4. Hanabi: does a higher entropy bonus make independently trained policies compatible, in a two-color game?

## Question

In two-player Hanabi with two colors, do independent PPO policies trained with entropy coefficient 0.05 score more with partners from other training seeds than policies trained with 0.01? And how far below the open-game ceiling of each deal do they play?

## Why it matters

Forkel et al. (2025, arXiv:2511.22581, v5 of 2026-05-05) report that independent PPO with entropy coefficient 0.05 instead of the usual 0.01 lifts two-player cross-play between seeds from 15.45 to 24.45. Their policies then score about as much with strangers as with themselves. They also prove that, in the tabular case, a high enough entropy bonus makes every seed converge to the same policy. On the full game one seed takes them more than 40 hours on an L40S, about $72 at the Jobs price, and the claim rests on four seeds per setting. That is beyond this folder's budget.

A two-color game keeps the structure (hints, hidden own cards, three lives) with 20 cards and a top score of 10. `hanabi/` can compute the open-game ceiling of every deal of that game exactly, so each score can be compared with the most that deal allows.

- If the entropy effect appears at this scale, the folder has a cheap reproduction of a published result about zero-shot coordination, with exact ceilings. H3's language model then has a measured non-language baseline on the same game and deals.
- If it does not, the effect may need the full game's size, or other settings of Forkel et al. The report says which settings differed.

## Method, as of 2026-11-19

- **Environment.** JaxMARL's Hanabi (`jaxmarl` 0.2.0, 2026-09-10, Apache 2.0) with `num_agents=2`, `num_colors=2`, `num_ranks=5`, hands of 5, 8 hint tokens and 3 lives. Forkel et al. ran on JaxMARL too.
- **Algorithm.** JaxMARL's IPPO baseline with a recurrent network (`baselines/IPPO/ippo_rnn_hanabi.py`), set to the choices Forkel et al. found mattered: an LSTM actor-critic and λ of 0.9 for generalized advantage estimation. Other settings are the baseline's, recorded in the run's manifest.
- **The two arms** differ only in the entropy coefficient α: 0.01 and 0.05.
- **Greedy play** after training, as Forkel et al. evaluate: each policy plays its most likely action.

## Design

- **Agreement with `rules.py`.** Before any training, a test plays 1,000 two-color games with random legal actions in JaxMARL and replays every action list in `rules.py`. Scores must agree in every game. If they do not, the run stops until the cause is found and recorded in an amendment. Known candidates are the score on losing the third life and discards with all tokens on the table. `Rules` has switches for both.
- **Training.** 8 seeds per arm, trained side by side in one process (`jax.vmap` over seeds). Each seed trains for 2 × 10⁸ environment steps. Training deals are JaxMARL's own random deals.
- **Test deals.** 2,000 deals from `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=2, colors=2), seed)` for seeds 400,000 to 401,999, loaded into JaxMARL's state. No earlier run used them. For each deal, the open-game ceiling is computed by `upper_bound.verdict` and, for deals lost in the open game, `best_score`.
- **Pairings on the test deals**, each in both seat orders:
  - self-play: seed i with itself;
  - cross-play within an arm: seed i with seed j, i ≠ j, both from that arm (56 ordered pairs per arm);
  - cross-play between arms: every seed of one arm with every seed of the other (secondary).

## Primary metric and test

Mean greedy cross-play score within an arm, α = 0.05 minus α = 0.01, paired by deal. Each deal's cross-play score is the mean over that arm's 56 ordered seed pairs. 95% interval: a bootstrap that resamples deals (shared by both arms) and, within each arm, its 8 seeds, rebuilding the pairs from the resampled seeds (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The high entropy bonus is said to help cross-play if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- Self-play per arm, and the gap between self-play and cross-play, with intervals.
- The gap to the ceiling: per deal, the open-game ceiling minus the score, for self-play and cross-play.
- The share of perfect games, with Wilson intervals over deals.
- The 16 × 16 cross-play matrix of all seeds of both arms, as in Forkel et al.'s Figure 4.
- Non-greedy (sampled) self-play and cross-play.
- **Behavior:** per seed, the share of color hints among hints, the share of hinted cards played on the next turn, and the misplays per game. Seeds that agree in cross-play should agree in these too; seeds that do not may differ in which kind of hint means "play".
- For comparison, without new runs: Forkel et al.'s full-game numbers. They are a different game, so they are not paired with these.

## Sample size

With 2,000 deals and 8 seeds an arm, the uncertainty comes mostly from seeds. Forkel et al.'s two-player cross-play at α = 0.01 had a standard error of 1.89 points out of 25 over 4 seeds, and at α = 0.05 about 0.02. If the two-color game behaves alike, scaled to its top of 10, the low-entropy arm's spread dominates and gives a half-width near 1 point. A difference of 2 points out of 10 is then resolved. If both arms are compatible across seeds, the difference may be 0 with a narrow interval, which is also a result.

## Seeds

Training seeds 1 to 8 per arm; seed 0 is the pilot's. Test deal seeds 400,000 to 401,999. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Jobs, `l4x1` ($0.80 an hour), through `hfrun.py`. The throughput of JaxMARL's Hanabi with an LSTM on an L4 is not known here. Forkel et al.'s figure for the full game (more than 40 hours a seed on an L40S) bounds it from above: the two-color game has 20 cards instead of 50, shorter games and smaller observations. The plan assumes about 2 × 10⁵ steps a second for 8 seeds together, so 1.6 × 10⁹ steps per arm take about 2.5 hours.

| Job | Timeout | Expected cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pilot: throughput for 8 seeds, 2 × 10⁷ steps, and the agreement test | 30 min | $0.30 |
| Arm α = 0.01 | 3.5 h | $2.00 |
| Arm α = 0.05 | 3.5 h | $2.00 |
| Evaluation on 2,000 deals, all pairings | 30 min | $0.30 |

Expected about $4.60. Hard cap $6.40: the four timeouts add up to 8 hours. If the pilot's throughput would make 2 × 10⁸ steps per seed pass its timeout, the step count is cut to what fits before the confirmatory runs, and the amendment records the new count. The open-game ceilings for 2,000 small deals run here or on GitHub Actions, free. No Docker image: `jax[cuda12]` and `jaxmarl` install from wheels into a new `hanabi-jax` dependency group. uv resolves every group into one lock file; if this group conflicts with H3's `hanabi-gpu` group, the two are declared as conflicting in `pyproject.toml` (uv's `conflicts` setting).

## Entry points

To be written:

- `hanabi/jax_bridge.py`: `to_jaxmarl(rules, deck)` and `replay(actions, rules, deck)`, with the agreement test in `tests/test_hanabi_jax.py` (skipped where JAX is not installed).
- `hanabi/ippo.py`: a thin wrapper over JaxMARL's IPPO baseline, `python -m hanabi.ippo --ent-coef 0.05 --seeds 1-8 --steps 2e8 --out OUT`, saving each seed's parameters.
- `python -m hanabi.ippo evaluate --runs <folder> --deals 400000-401999 --out OUT`, in a job.
- `python -m hanabi.h4 --runs <folder> --out hanabi/results/h4.json`, run here: ceilings, pairing and bootstrap.

## Outputs

- In git: `hanabi/results/h4.json` (per deal: ceiling; per pairing and deal: score; per seed: behavior counts and training curve) and a README section.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/h4/`: the 16 policies' parameters and the training logs.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The agreement test must pass before training. Each arm runs once; a job that fails for reasons outside the code is rerun once. If the primary interval includes 0, the README reports that the high entropy bonus did not raise cross-play in the two-color game, with each arm's self-play and the cross-play matrix.

## Threats to validity

- **A smaller game.** With two colors, there are fewer ways to break symmetry (two color hints instead of five), which may make conventions agree more often at any α. The result is about this game.
- **Fewer steps than the paper.** The policies may be undertrained. The training curves show whether scores have flattened.
- **One reading of the baseline.** Settings other than α and λ are JaxMARL's defaults, not necessarily Forkel et al.'s, which their appendix lists.
- **Two engines.** JaxMARL plays the games and `rules.py` scores the ceilings. The agreement test covers random play, not every state a trained policy reaches.
