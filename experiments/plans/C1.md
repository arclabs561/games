# C1. Codenames: the human data split by game

## Question

Do the Codenames results that rest on human guesses hold when the Cultural Codes turns are split by game instead of by turn? The main one is that the linear map trained on Cultural Codes moves potion's top-1 match from 0.580 to 0.662. The order of the eight models is the other.

## Why it matters

`codenames/README.md` says the published splits "appear to be split by turn, not by game". A turn from a test game then has sibling turns in training: the same board, the same two players, often the same clue words. A model trained on those turns can learn the board and the players rather than how people associate words, so its test score is optimistic. Untrained models are hardly affected: they only borrow a temperature and a template from train and val.

- If the trained map's gain survives a game split, the README's section 4 stands, and C2 compares LoRA with a fair baseline.
- If the gain shrinks to within noise, section 4 is reported as an artifact of the split, and C2's comparison is redesigned around the new numbers.
- The order of untrained models changing would mean that the ranking in section 1 depends on which 305 turns happened to be in test.

C2 and C4 also need this split, which is why C1 comes first.

## What is known already (exploratory)

The CSV files carry no game id. A game can be rebuilt from the board and the two players: in Duet both players give clues, so a game is a 25-word starting board together with the unordered pair of player profiles, and every later turn's board is a subset of it. A check run while writing this plan (a 40-line script, not committed) used the published `generate_guess_task` files at commit `9bf4550` of SALT-NLP/codenames. It assigned all 7,703 rows to exactly one of 794 games. White et al. (2024) report 794 games for this dataset, so the reconstruction looks right. 165 of the games have turns in more than one split. 320 of the 373 test rows and 423 of the 455 validation rows come from games that also have training rows. White et al. write that the players in the validation and test sets do not appear in training. These files contradict that, unless their splits came from somewhere else; C1 records this either way.

These counts use rows before the filter in `norms.cultural_guesses` (which keeps 305 test turns), so they are approximate.

## Design

- **Data.** Cultural Codes `generate_guess_task`, train, val and test merged, then the filter of `norms.cultural_guesses`. The pinned commit and hashes in `fetch.py` stay.
- **Games.** `codenames/split.py` (to be written) rebuilds games as above. Every turn must land in exactly one game. The run stops if any turn is ambiguous or unassigned.
- **The new split.** A game goes to train, val or test by `zlib.crc32` of its key (the sorted board words and the two profile strings) modulo 10: 0 to 7 train, 8 val, 9 test. No seed is involved, and nobody chooses which games are in test. Expected size: about 79 test games and 700 to 800 test turns.
- **Arms**, all on the new test turns:
  - untrained potion, with τ and template refitted on the new train and val;
  - potion with the linear map of `train.py`, trained on the new train split only (`eat=False`, as in the README's "Cultural Codes train" row);
  - the same for Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (query template);
  - every untrained model in `study.calibrate`, refitted the same way.
- **Leakage, measured directly (secondary).** Each test game's turns are split in half by the hash of the turn's text: "held" and "sibling". Arm A trains the potion map on the train games. Arm B trains on the train games with the same number of random turns swapped out for the sibling turns. Both are scored on the held turns only. B minus A is the gain from seeing other turns of the same game, with the training-set size fixed.
- **Player overlap (secondary).** Players are identified by their profile strings. Count the test games whose players also play in train games, and report the primary metric on the test games with no such player.

## Primary metric and test

The difference in top-1 match (the person's word is the model's most similar board word) between the trained potion map and untrained potion, on the new test turns. Paired by turn. 95% interval by bootstrap over test games (10,000 resamples, seed 0), because turns within a game are correlated. The gain is said to hold if the interval excludes 0. The point estimate is compared with the published 0.082.

## Secondary metrics

- Top-1 of every untrained model on the new test set, with intervals by bootstrap over games. Kendall's τ between this order and the published order, with a bootstrap interval.
- Mean log-likelihood under the fitted Luce guesser.
- The same primary difference for Qwen3.
- The leakage estimate B − A, with a game-bootstrap interval.
- The fitted τ and chosen template per model, to see whether they move.

## Sample size

With about 750 test turns, a top-1 near 0.6 and 20% of turns where the two arms disagree, the paired standard error is about √(0.2 / 750) ≈ 0.016. Clustering in games of about ten turns, with a modest within-game correlation, widens it by a factor of about 1.2 to 1.5. That gives a half-width of 0.04 to 0.05. A true gain of 0.08 would be detected; a gain of 0.03 would not be told apart from 0, and the report will say so. The test split is fixed at 10% to match the published proportion; a larger test split would cost training data, the factor under study.

## Seeds

The split has no seed. `train.py`'s seed picks the negatives and the training order. The trained maps use seeds 1 to 5 (seed 0 made the README's numbers), and the primary metric uses each turn's hit rate averaged over the five. The bootstrap seed is 0.

## Compute

CPU, this machine. `study.calibrate` takes about a minute per split, and `train.py` 1 to 60 seconds per run. The Hugging Face models' vectors come from the branch `compute/codenames-embed`. They cover all words in all three published splits, so no new vectors are needed. Expected cost $0; cap: 2 CPU hours, no paid jobs.

## Entry points

To be written:

- `codenames/split.py`: `games()`, `game_split(key)`, and `python -m codenames.split --check`, which prints the counts above and fails on any ambiguous turn.
- `norms.cultural_guesses(split, scheme="published")`, with `scheme="game"` reading the new split. The default keeps every existing result unchanged.
- `--scheme game` on `codenames.study calibrate`, `codenames.train` and `codenames.evaluate`, writing `results/calibrate-game.json` and `results/train-*-game.json`.
- `codenames/stats.py`: paired and cluster bootstrap. Tests compare it with exact enumeration of every resample on inputs of three or four units.
- `python -m codenames.c1 --out codenames/results/c1.json`: the primary and secondary metrics from the files above.

## Outputs

- In git: `codenames/results/c1.json`, `calibrate-game.json` and `train-*-game.json`; the game id of each turn as `results/cultural-games.csv`, if the dataset's license allows it (none is stated, so by default only the code that rebuilds it is committed); and a README section with the new table next to the old one.
- In the private dataset: nothing; all inputs are public and all outputs are small.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The test turns are scored once, after `split.py --check` passes and the arms are fixed. If the primary interval includes 0, the README says that the gain of the linear map is not distinguishable from 0 under a game split, gives the interval, and moves the old number into a note on the leaky split. If the reconstruction cannot assign every turn, C1 stops and reports how many turns it could not place. It does not fall back to a heuristic split.

## Threats to validity

- **The reconstruction is inferred.** Two games could share a board and identical player profiles. The match with White et al.'s 794 games suggests this is rare. The check prints any board used by more than one pair.
- **All of this data has been seen.** The published test turns are now mostly in train, and the authors of section 1 chose the templates while looking at test scores. The new test set is not fresh in that sense. Only the split is new.
- **Profiles are not people.** Two players with the same demographics and leanings would look like one player in the overlap check.
- **Small test set.** About 79 games. The interval is the honest measure of what that supports.
