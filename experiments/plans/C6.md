# C6. Codenames: a language model picks among legal clues that an embedding giver proposes

## Question

When a hosted language model chooses one clue from a list of legal (clue, count) pairs proposed by an embedding giver, do guessers it never met find more of its words than they find of the embedding giver's own first choice?

## Why it matters

In C4 every language-model giver lost to the EmbeddingGemma 2 giver, by 0.29 to 0.89 words per turn with seven embedding guessers it had never met (`codenames/README.md`, "Language models"). Two things were mixed in that loss. About half of the clues from Llama 3.3 and DeepSeek-V3.2 broke the clue rule, and the legal ones were also worse: 0.68 to 0.90 below the embedding giver on the boards where the clue was legal. This plan removes the first cause by construction. Every candidate is legal and has a vector for every guesser, so only the choice is left.

- If the language model's choice beats the embedding giver's first choice, a model's knowledge of words helps when its rule-breaking is taken away. C7 then trains a small model to make the same choice.
- If it does not, C4's loss was not only illegal clues: given the same options, the language model chooses no better than the embedding score. C7 still runs, since its question is whether training helps, but its prior is lower.
- The best choice in the list, known only to a cheat, is an exact upper bound. How much of the gap up to it the model closes says how much room there was.

## Design

- **Boards.** 200 test boards from board seed 6004 and 50 validation boards from seed 6005, dealt by `game.py` from `words.txt`. No earlier run used either seed. Boards with seeds from 6100 up serve the prompt search below and are never scored.
- **Candidates.** For each board, `rl.actions` in the EmbeddingGemma 2 space (bare template, τ from `results/calibrate-game.json`) with a shortlist of 30 clues from `words.clue_words()`. That gives 120 actions: 30 clues times counts 1 to 4. A test checks that every clue is legal on its board (`words.legal`) and has a vector in all eight spaces.
- **Prompt.** `llm.giver_prompt` followed by the 30 clues, numbered, and the instruction to answer `CLUE NUMBER` with a clue from the list and a number from 1 to 4. The order of the list is the shortlist's order, which is fixed by `rl.actions`. A reply that names no listed clue, or a count outside 1 to 4, gets the embedding giver's first choice, and the count of such replies is reported.
- **Choosers.** Six hosted models through Hugging Face Inference Providers, ordered and fixed now:
  1. gpt-oss-120b, reasoning effort low, replies capped at 1,024 tokens (as in C4);
  2. Llama 3.3 70B Instruct;
  3. Qwen3-32B with thinking off;
  4. DeepSeek-V3.2;
  5. Qwen3.8-27B (Qwen, 2026-08-05, Apache 2.0), thinking off;
  6. Gemma 4 31B instruction-tuned (Google, Apache 2.0), thinking off.

  The first four are C4's. The last two are the newest dense open models that more than one provider served on 2026-11-19, according to their pages on the Hub. Temperature 0 and seed 0, one provider per model, recorded per call.
- **Baselines on the same boards and candidates.**
  - *First choice:* the action with the highest expected value under the EmbeddingGemma 2 Luce guesser, the giver that proposed the list.
  - *Uniform:* the mean expected value over all 120 actions. This is exact; nothing is sampled.
  - *Oracle:* the action with the highest mean expected value under the scoring guessers themselves. No giver can know this. It bounds what any choice from the list can reach.
- **Scoring guessers.** Every clue is scored by exact expected turn value (`luce.py`, the README's costs):
  - the seven calibrated embedding guessers other than EmbeddingGemma 2, as in C4;
  - the potion guesser with the linear map that C1 trained on human guesses (`train.py`, game split). There is one Luce guesser per training seed, 1 to 5, and their values are averaged. This is the closest guesser to people that the folder has: it matches the person's pick in 0.632 of C1's test turns.
  - the EAT association guesser (secondary).

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, tested with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05. The chooser for both is the one of the six with the highest mean value over the seven embedding guessers on the 50 validation boards, chosen before any test board is sent.

- **H-strangers.** Mean expected turn value over the seven embedding guessers, the chosen model's pick minus the first choice, paired by board.
- **H-human.** Expected turn value under the human-fitted potion guesser, the same difference.

95% intervals by bootstrap over the 200 test boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The p-value for Holm is the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0, doubled. A hypothesis holds if its adjusted p-value is below 0.05 and the difference is positive.

## Secondary metrics

- All six models on both measures, with intervals and without significance claims.
- The share of the gap closed: (pick − first choice) / (oracle − first choice), with an interval from the same resamples.
- The rank of the model's pick among the 120 actions under the EmbeddingGemma 2 score, and the share of boards where the pick is the first choice.
- **Language-model guessers**, as in C4: Llama 3.3, DeepSeek-V3.2 and gpt-oss-120b guess on the chosen model's clues and on the first choice's, one sampled ranking per clue. A model does not guess on its own clues.
- **Prompt evolution (exploratory, after the primary run).** GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.19457; the `gepa` package, MIT, version 0.1.4 of 2026-07-15) rewrites the chosen model's prompt on 200 boards from seed 6100 up. Its score is the mean expected value under the four guessers that C7 trains against: wordllama, granite, bge and harrier-oss-v1-270m. The best prompt is scored once on the test boards with the three guessers that C7 holds out and the potion map. This is the no-training baseline that C7 has to beat. It runs after the primary test is scored, so it cannot affect it.
- **Behavior:** mean count, the rarity of the chosen clues, and the number of distinct clues over the 200 boards.
- The repeat check of C4: 20 validation prompts sent twice more to each model.

## Sample size

C4's difference between two givers over seven guessers had a half-width of about 0.15 words per turn on 200 boards, but most of that came from fallback clues and illegal clues. Here both arms choose from the same 120 actions, so the boards' difficulty cancels in the pairing more fully. A half-width of 0.05 to 0.10 is expected, which resolves a difference of 0.1 words per turn. The gap between first choice and oracle on these boards is not yet known. If it is under 0.1, no choice from the list could show an effect, and the report will say so.

## Seeds

Board seeds 6004 (test), 6005 (validation) and 6100 onward (prompt evolution). Model calls use seed 0 where the provider accepts it. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers, from a `cpu-basic` job through `hfrun.py`, with the cost meter of `codenames/hosted.py`. Calls:

| Part | Calls |
|---|---:|
| Validation, 6 models × 50 boards | 300 |
| Test, 6 models × 200 boards | 1,200 |
| Language-model guessers, 3 × 200 boards × 2 givers | 1,200 |
| Repeat check, 6 × 20 × 2 | 240 |
| Prompt evolution, at most 300 scored prompts and 60 reflection calls | 360 |

C4's calls cost $0.00002 to $0.00006 each, with prompts about half as long. Allowing four times that gives at most $0.80; the CPU time adds about $0.03. Expected $0.40 to $0.80; hard cap $1.50, enforced by the cost meter, in the order of the table. No GPU and no Docker image.

## Entry points

To be written:

- `codenames/rerank.py`: `candidates(board, space, tau, k=30)` (wrapping `rl.actions`), `rerank_prompt(board, cands)`, `read_choice(reply, cands)`, and `python -m codenames.rerank choose --models <list> --seed 6004 --n 200 --out OUT`, run in the job.
- `python -m codenames.rerank evolve --model <chosen> --seeds 6100-6299 --budget 300 --out OUT` for the prompt search.
- `python -m codenames.rerank score --runs <folder> --out codenames/results/c6.json`, run here. It needs the potion maps of C1, which `train.py` rebuilds in seconds.
- Tests: `read_choice` on replies with and without listed clues; the uniform and oracle baselines against a loop over all 120 actions.

## Outputs

- In git: `codenames/results/c6.json` (per board: candidates, each model's pick, every guesser's value for the pick and for the baselines; model ids, providers, dates and costs) and a README section.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/c6/`: every prompt and reply, and the evolved prompt.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The chooser is selected on validation boards, and the test boards are sent once. If H-strangers fails, the README states that a language model choosing from legal candidates did not beat the embedding giver's own choice with strangers, and gives the share of the oracle gap closed. If a model is not served when the run starts, it is dropped, not replaced. The amendment says so before the run.

## Threats to validity

- **The proposer shapes the result.** The list comes from EmbeddingGemma 2, so every candidate is a clue that an embedding model finds good. No one can choose a clue that only a language model would think of. This is the design's purpose, and also its limit.
- **The scoring guessers are models.** The potion map is fitted to people's guesses but is still a vector model. No person guesses here.
- **Model drift and nondeterminism**, as in C4. The repeat check measures the second.
- **Prompt.** One fixed prompt for the primary test. The evolved prompt is reported separately and labeled exploratory.

## Amendments

### 2026-11-19, before any run

- **Approval and budget.** The plan is approved with a hard cap of $1.50 for everything, the pilot included. Each job gets its own cap on the cost meter (below), and the caps add up to less than $1.50 with the CPU time.
- **Providers.** All six models are served. Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B are pinned to deepinfra, with `chat_template_kwargs: {enable_thinking: false}`. The first four keep C4's providers and settings. Llama 3.3, Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B have replies capped at 64 tokens, as C4's givers were.
- **Pilot.** Before validation, every model chooses on 5 boards of seed 6999, a seed no plan uses, to check providers, parsing, tokens and cost. Nothing from the pilot is scored or reported as a result. If it shows a fault in the reading of replies or in the request settings, the fix is made before the validation run and stated here.
- **Where the actions come from.** `codenames/actions.py` computes the 120 actions (`legal_actions`, wrapping `rl.actions` with the whole clue list), for C6 and for C7. The job has no word vectors, so the lists for seeds 6999 (5 boards), 6005 (50) and 6004 (200) are computed here and committed as `codenames/results/c6-actions.txt` (SHA-256 `a95f9d1a3745ba2c231de8b7098d7aaf6258aab15ef7bd0835b5aae8131a8257`). The job reads that file. The module is `codenames/rerank.py` with `choose` and `crossguess` subcommands; scoring is in `codenames/c6score.py`.
- **Prompt.** The text of `llm.giver_prompt`, then "Choose your clue from this list:", the 30 clues as `1. clue` lines, and "Answer on one line as: CLUE NUMBER, with a clue from the list and a number from 1 to 4."
- **Reading a reply.** The first word in the reply that is a listed clue and is followed by a number (`([A-Za-z]+)\W+(\d+)`, case ignored) gives the action. If that number is outside 1 to 4, the reply has no action ("count out of range"). A reply with no listed clue followed by a number is "unlisted". Gemma 4's empty thought channel is removed first. A reply without an action gets the first choice.
- **Ties and oracles.** The first choice is the first action in list order with the highest EmbeddingGemma 2 value. There are two oracles: the action with the highest mean value over the seven strangers, used for H-strangers, and the action with the highest mean value over the five potion maps, used for H-human.
- **The potion maps.** They are retrained with `train.py`'s settings for seeds 1 to 5. Scoring checks that each rebuilt map reproduces the per-turn hits that C1 recorded in `results/train-potion-game.json`. Each map's Luce temperature is the one C1 fitted on the training turns, 0.05.
- **Language-model guessers.** They hear the chosen model's pick (with the first choice where its reply had none) and the first choice. The comparison uses only the guessers that heard both.
- **Prompt evolution.** The `gepa` package is not in the `codenames` dependency group, and this run does not change the dependencies. Whether and how the exploratory GEPA part runs is decided after the primary test is scored, in a later amendment. It cannot affect the primary test.
- **Job caps.** Pilot $0.05, validation with the repeat check $0.20, test $0.50, language-model guessers $0.30.

### 2026-11-19, after the pilot and before validation

The pilot (job `6aff14db4fdc5ab3c559dd95`, $0.002) found two faults in the reading of replies. Nothing from it is scored.

- Qwen3-32B answers in the form `CLUE: **store** NUMBER: **4**`, which the pattern above does not read. Qwen3.8-27B, with thinking off, still reasons in its reply on some boards, ending with its answer; at 64 tokens 2 of its 5 replies were cut off in the middle of that reasoning.
- **New reading rule.** The action is read from the last line of the reply that names a listed clue with a number after it: that clue, and the first number after it on the line. A count outside 1 to 4 is still "count out of range", and a reply without such a line is still "unlisted". A reply that the provider stopped at the token cap (`finish_reason` "length") has no action ("truncated"), since its answer was not reached. Each of these gets the first choice, as before.
- The token caps stay as stated, 1,024 for gpt-oss-120b and 64 for the rest. Raising Qwen3.8-27B's cap would let it reason before answering, which the plan's "thinking off" excludes, and at its listed output price of $1.875 per million tokens would put the cap at risk. Its truncated replies are counted and reported.
- In the pilot, 3 of gpt-oss-120b's 5 calls were refused by the meter, because the six models ran side by side and each call reserves $0.002 against a cap of $0.05. The later jobs' caps leave room for every reservation.
