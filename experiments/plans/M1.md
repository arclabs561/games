# M1. Mastermind: do Fallout terminals of common words change the table?

## Question

The Fallout table in `mastermind/README.md` draws each screen from ENABLE, a list with many words a game would not show (JAUK, WHIT). On screens drawn only from words that are common in English, what share of passwords can the best play crack within four attempts, and how far do minimax, expected size, the "common" rule and random guessing fall below it? Does the hardest row, 20-word Very Easy screens at 92.5% ± 0.4 for the best play, move?

## Why it matters

The README's open questions ask exactly this. The game's own word lists are not available, so no list is the game's; a frequency cut is a closer model of the words a writer would pick. Common short words share more letters with each other than obscure ones, and likeness is the only clue the terminal gives, so the change could go either way.

- If the best play's share moves by more than the detectable effect, the README's headline depends on the word list, and the table gains a column.
- If it does not move, the ENABLE table stands for common words too, at this resolution, which is worth saying because ENABLE is the weakest assumption in the Fallout study.

No model is learned and nothing is paid. The plan exists because the study is a simulated comparison with a choice (the frequency cut) that must be fixed before the result is seen, and because the data's license must be settled first.

## Data

- **ENABLE** (`boggle/enable1.txt`), public domain, as now.
- **Word frequency:** `wordfreq` 3.1.1 (already in the `boggle` group), `zipf_frequency(word, "en")`. Its code is under the Apache 2.0 license and its data files "may be redistributed under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license"; derived work must credit the SUBTLEX authors, and the project says its data is a snapshot through about 2021 and will not be updated (github.com/rspeer/wordfreq, read 2026-11-20). The package is used at run time. No frequency, and no filtered word list, is committed: a committed list derived from the data would carry the share-alike terms into this repository, and aggregates do not need it.
- **Not used:** Google Books Ngram exports (CC BY 3.0, storage.googleapis.com/books/ngrams/books/datasetsv3.html, read 2026-11-20). They would be a second, independent frequency source, but the English 1-gram files are many gigabytes. Left for a later plan if the result here is not null.

**The common list, fixed now:** ENABLE words of 4 to 15 letters with Zipf frequency at least 3.0. Pool sizes by length, computed before this plan from ENABLE and wordfreq 3.1.1 (exact counts; no screen was drawn):

## Design

- Everything as in the README's study (`fallout3.py --study`): word length uniform over the level's lengths, the password uniform over the screen, every strategy played against every password on each screen (so each screen's numbers are exact), "best possible" by exhaustive search on each screen, random guessing computed exactly.
- **One factor:** the list the screen's words are drawn from, ENABLE or common.
- **Screens:** 5,000 per row and list, with new seeds: 31 for ENABLE and 32 for the common list. The README's seed-3 screens are not reused, so the ENABLE arm is also a replicate of the published table.
- **Rows:** the README's six (Very Easy with 5 and 20 words, Easy, Average and Hard with 20, Very Hard with 13), plus the 10- and 15-word rows that `--study` prints.

## Primary metric and test

- **H-common.** On 20-word Very Easy screens, the best play's share of passwords cracked within four attempts, common minus ENABLE. The screens of the two lists are drawn independently, so the difference is of two independent means; 95% interval by bootstrap over screens within each list (10,000 resamples, seed 0).
- **Decision.** If the interval excludes 0, the README's headline row gets the common-word value beside ENABLE's and says which way the list moves it. If it holds 0, the README says that common words did not change the hardest row, with the detectable effect.

## Secondary metrics

- Every strategy in every row, both lists, with normal 95% intervals as the README reports them now, and the differences.
- The thresholds 2.5 and 3.5 for the 20-word Very Easy row, as a sensitivity check.
- Mean pairwise likeness of a screen, by list and length, and the share of screens on which no strategy can always win.
- The hardest common-word screen found (its words are common English words and may be printed).
- The ENABLE arm against the README's published values, row by row: a replicate check of the simulation.

## Minimum detectable effect

The README's 92.5 ± 0.4 on 1,000 screens is a normal 95% half-width, so a screen's best-play share has a standard deviation of 0.4 / 1.96 × √1000 ≈ 6.5 points. For two independent lists of n = 5,000 screens, 2.80 × 6.5 × √(2 / 5000) ≈ 0.36 points. The effect worth finding is 1 point. The plan is powered for it.

## Seeds

Screens: seed 31 (ENABLE) and seed 32 (common). Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

CPU only. The README's study takes about 4 minutes for 1,000 screens a row with every strategy; 5,000 screens a row, two lists, plus the three Very Easy sensitivity runs, is about 45 minutes on one core (estimated by scaling). It runs on one GitHub Actions job, or here with `nice` if the shared machine is free.

**Expected $0. Paid cap $0.** Actions cap: one job of at most 2 hours, rerun once at most.

## Entry points

`fallout3.py` exists. To be written:

- `--words enable|common` and `--zipf T` for `fallout3.py --study`, with `--screens N` and `--seed S`. wordfreq joins the `mastermind` dependency group (the test that ties packages to folders requires it).
- `python -m mastermind.m1 --out mastermind/results/m1.json`, which runs both arms and the bootstrap.
- Tests: the common list is a subset of ENABLE; its size per length at Zipf 3.0 matches the table above for wordfreq 3.1.1; the study with `--words enable --seed 3 --screens 1000` reproduces the README's table.

## Outputs

- In git: `mastermind/results/m1.json` (every row, both lists, the pool sizes, the wordfreq version) and a column or short table in the README's Fallout section, with wordfreq's license and the SUBTLEX credit in the README's data table.
- Nothing derived per word from wordfreq is committed.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- The run happens once at a pushed commit. If the replicate check of the ENABLE arm differs from the README's table by more than its intervals allow on any row, the code is checked before any common-word number is read.
- **A null result** is stated with the detectable effect of 0.36 points.

## Threats to validity

- **No list is the game's.** A frequency cut is a guess at how a writer picks words.
- **Zipf frequency is a snapshot of usage to about 2021**, from web and subtitle text, not of the late 2000s when Fallout 3 was written.
- **Duds may not be random.** If the game picks duds to share letters with the password, neither list models it; the README's open question on that stays open.
