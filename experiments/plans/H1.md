# H1. Hanabi: how many deals reach 25 with every card visible

## Question

What share of standard Hanabi deals can reach 25 in the open game, where every player sees every card and the deck order, for two to five players? Two parts: settle the deals the current solver leaves undecided, and check every verdict with a second, independent solver.

## Why it matters

The share bounds every agent's rate of perfect games, and the mean best score bounds every agent's mean (`hanabi/README.md`). The README gives it from 2,000 seeded deals per player count. Its intervals are 0.973 to 0.986 for two players and 0.993 to 1 for five. The four- and five-player shares rest on one search, written once.

- A second solver that agrees on every decided deal makes the verdicts trustworthy. A disagreement means a bug in one of them, which would change the README's numbers.
- Settling the eleven undecided deals removes the gap between the "won" and "won plus undecided" ends of each range.
- 20,000 deals per count narrow the sampling interval by a factor of about three. The two-player bound on the mean score, 24.977 ± 0.008, is what the "share of the gap that no agent can close" column rests on.

## Design

- **Deals.** `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=p), seed)` for seeds 0 to 19,999 and p = 2, 3, 4, 5, with the official rules (`Rules()` defaults). Seeds 0 to 1,999 are the README's sample and are reported separately as well as pooled.
- **Solver A** is `solver.winnable`, as it is, with a budget of 20,000 nodes, then 300,000 for deals left undecided, then 5,000,000.
- **Solver B** (to be written) shares no code with `solver.py` except `rules.py`. It is a time-indexed constraint model of the open game solved with OR-Tools CP-SAT. Each turn chooses an action, piles and token counts are integer variables, and the target is a score of 25. It decides a deal by finding a winning line or by proving the model infeasible. If CP-SAT cannot handle full deals within a minute each on average, Solver B becomes a depth-first search over `rules.step` with a transposition table and only one bound, the time bound, written from the README's description without reading `solver.py`.
- **Checks.**
  - Every win from either solver is replayed by `solver.replay` through the rules engine and must reach 25.
  - Every deal both solvers decide must get the same verdict. Any disagreement stops the analysis until it is explained.
  - Solver B is also run on every deal of the small decks in the README's table, and must reproduce each exact count.

## Primary metric

For each player count: the winnable share over seeds 0 to 19,999, with a 95% Wilson interval. Deals still undecided after both solvers are reported as a range, the lower end counting them as lost and the upper as won, and the interval covers both ends, as the README does now. Alongside: the number of deals on which the two solvers agree, out of the number both decide. The target is agreement on all of them.

There is no hypothesis test. This is a measurement, and the question is how precise it is.

## Secondary metrics

- The certificate that first proves each lost deal (time, storage, pooled game, search only), as the README tabulates now.
- The best score on each lost deal (`upper_bound.best_score`) and the bound on the mean score, with a 95% interval.
- Node counts and times per deal for both solvers, and the deals that need the largest budgets.
- Whether any four- or five-player deal is lost.

## Sample size

At a share near 0.98, as for two players, 20,000 deals give a Wilson half-width of about 0.002. At 0.9995, as for four players, it is about 0.0004. The README's bound on the mean score has a half-width of 0.008 at 2,000 deals, and 20,000 deals take it to about 0.0025.

## Seeds

Deal seeds 0 to 19,999 for each player count. Solvers A and B are deterministic. CP-SAT runs with one worker and a fixed random seed (0), so its search is repeatable.

## Compute and cost

CPU. Solver A classifies 2,000 deals in 1 to 2 minutes here, so 80,000 deals take one to two hours, plus the reruns at higher budgets. Solver B's speed is unknown until it is written. Everything runs on GitHub Actions as a matrix of 40 jobs (four player counts times ten blocks of 2,000 seeds), on the branch `compute/hanabi-h1`. Undecided deals at the largest budget run as separate jobs with a 6-hour limit. Cost $0; cap 60 runner jobs and one rerun of the matrix.

## Entry points

`upper_bound.py --sample P N --start S --budget B` exists. To be added:

- `--json OUT`: per-deal verdict, certificate, best score, nodes and seconds.
- `hanabi/solver_cp.py` (Solver B) with `winnable_cp(rules, deck, time_limit)`, which returns "won" with a line, "lost", or "undecided". OR-Tools joins a new `hanabi` dependency group.
- `python -m hanabi.upper_bound --sample 2 2000 --start 4000 --solver both --json OUT/p2-4000.json`, one job per block.
- `python -m hanabi.h1 --runs <folder> --out hanabi/results/h1.json`: merges blocks, checks agreement, and computes the shares and intervals.

## Outputs

- In git: `hanabi/results/h1.json`, with one row per deal (seed, players, verdict A, verdict B, certificate, best score), about 3 MB. Also the updated tables in `hanabi/README.md`.
- In the private dataset: nothing. The winning lines are reproducible from the code and are not stored.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The matrix runs once. Deals undecided after 5,000,000 nodes and Solver B's time limit stay undecided and are reported as such, with their seeds. A disagreement between solvers is not a null result. It is a bug report, and no share is updated until it is resolved. If Solver B cannot be built to decide full deals, H1 reports Solver A on 20,000 deals and states that the second check covers only the small decks.

## Threats to validity

- **Shared rules.** Both solvers use `rules.py`. A rules bug would fool both. The rules engine has its own tests, and the information strategy matches HanSim game by game, which checks the rules from outside.
- **Seeded deals are not every deal.** `random.Random(seed)` shuffles are a sample of deals, assumed uniform; the share is a sampling estimate, not an exact count.
- **Open game only.** The share bounds real play from above. It does not say how close a strategy that does not see the deck can get.
