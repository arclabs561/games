# H3. Hanabi: a small language model trained by self-play against the rules engine

## Question

In two-player Hanabi with two colors, does Qwen3.5-2B, trained by self-play reinforcement learning with the game's score as its reward, score more with partners it never trained with than the untrained model does? The partners are copies trained from other seeds and a rule-based player written for this plan.

## Why it matters

H2 found hosted models far below the ceiling: gpt-oss-120b 18.6 points below 25 and DeepSeek-V3.2 25 below, in two-player self-play. Ramesh et al. (2026, arXiv:2601.18077) fine-tuned Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with GRPO on HanabiRewards, single moves rated by o3, and gained 7.5 points on average in their Sherlock setting. Their reward is another model's judgment, and their training ran on a node of 8 A100s. Yuan et al. (MARSHAL, arXiv:2510.15414, ICLR 2026) trained Qwen3-4B by self-play on Mini Hanabi with the game's own reward. They add two changes to GRPO: an advantage per turn, from the return still to come, and separate normalization for each seat.

Neither paper scores its model with partners from independent training runs or with a player that is not a language model. Coordination with strangers is what Hanabi is used to measure (Hu et al., 2020; Dizdarević et al., 2025).

- If training raises the score with other seeds and with the rule-based player, a small model learns play that strangers can follow. H4's policies on the same deals say how far that is from a non-language agent.
- If it raises self-play only, the model learned private conventions, the failure that other-play addresses, and the report says so.
- If it raises nothing, the setting (2B model, this budget) is too small, and the README says that the Ramesh et al. and MARSHAL gains were not reproduced here.

## Model and method, as of 2026-11-19

- **Model.** Qwen3.5-2B, thinking off, LoRA rank 32 on every linear projection of the language model. C7 gives the reasons for this model. Gemma 4 E2B is the fallback if Qwen3.5-2B fails in the pilot.
- **Game.** `Rules(players=2, colors=2)`: 20 cards, hands of 5, 8 hint tokens, 3 lives, top score 10, the official rules otherwise. The game is short enough to train on (about 20 turns), and every deal's open-game ceiling can be computed exactly. H4 uses the same game.
- **Observation.** `llm.observation(..., deduction=True)` (H2's prompt with the possible values of each own card), and the reply ends with a move as in H2. Replies are capped at 128 tokens. A reply with no legal move gets H2's fallback move and a reward of −1 for that turn.
- **Reward.** +1 for each card played, shared by both seats. If the third life is lost, the game scores 0, so that turn's reward is minus the score so far. The return from a turn is the sum of the rewards still to come, and the game's total return is its score.
- **Algorithm.** MARSHAL's advantage: for each seat separately, the return from each turn minus the mean of those returns over the 8 games of a group. A group is 8 games on the same deal. The loss is PPO's clipped surrogate, with clip-higher bounds 0.2 and 0.28 and a token-mean average, as in DAPO, and no KL term. TRL's `GRPOTrainer` groups samples by prompt, which does not fit returns that run over a whole game, so the loop is written here (`hanabi/selfplay.py`, about 200 lines) with PEFT and PyTorch. A test checks its advantages against a hand computation on a game of three turns.
- **Considered and not chosen.**
  - Single-move RL on HanabiRewards (21,464 Sherlock rows with move ratings; its card on the Hub gives the MIT license). Its rewards are o3's judgments, not the game's. Its states come from five-color games in another engine's prompt format, and the Hub's viewer fails on the files, so their contents were not checked. It is left for a later plan.
  - The hat strategy as a partner. `information.py` plays it for three to five players only, and its partners read every hint as a code, which H2 dropped for that reason.
  - GDPO. The format penalty is part of the one return, so there is a single reward to normalize.

## Design

- **Training.** 120 steps. A step plays 4 deals × 8 games in self-play: both seats are the same model with the same adapter, sampling at temperature 1. Training deals come from seeds 100,000 onward, fresh for each step. Learning rate 2e-5, fixed now.
- **Partners at test**, never used in training:
  - *Other seeds:* the adapter of seed i with the adapter of seed j, i ≠ j.
  - *Rule-based player* (`hanabi/bots.py`, written before any training and fixed by this plan): play a card whose possible values are all playable; otherwise, with a hint token, give the hint that marks a playable card of the partner and the fewest other cards; otherwise discard a card known to be useless; otherwise discard the oldest card with no hint; otherwise give any legal hint.
- **Test deals.** 200 deals, seeds 400,000 to 400,199 of `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=2, colors=2), seed)`, the first 200 of H4's test deals. Greedy decoding. Every pairing plays each deal in both seat orders.
- **Arms:** the trained model (seeds 1, 2 and 3) and the untrained model.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05:

- **H-seeds.** Mean score with an adapter from another seed, minus the untrained model's self-play score, paired by deal. Each deal's trained score is the mean over the 6 ordered seed pairs.
- **H-bot.** Mean score with the rule-based player, trained minus untrained, paired by deal. Each deal's trained score is the mean over the 3 seeds.

95% intervals: a bootstrap that resamples deals and, for the trained arm, its 3 seeds (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The p-value for Holm is the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0, doubled.

## Secondary metrics

- Self-play of each seed, and the gap between self-play and cross-play.
- The gap to each deal's open-game ceiling for every pairing.
- The rule-based player in self-play, and H4's IPPO policies on the same 200 deals, both as reference points.
- Lives lost, fallback moves and truncated replies per game.
- **Behavior:** for each seed, the shares of plays, discards, color hints and rank hints. Also the share of hinted cards that the partner plays on its next turn, the move the rule-based player expects. Seeds whose conventions differ should show it here.
- **Reward against score.** The engine's score of each logged game is compared with the summed rewards, to catch a reward that drifts from the game.
- **Transfer (exploratory).** Each trained seed in self-play on 30 standard five-color deals, seeds 0 to 29, H2's deals, against the untrained model.

## Sample size

Scores on the two-color game run from 0 to 10, and a small model loses all three lives often. With a standard deviation of 3 to 4 points over deals, 200 paired deals give a deal-level half-width near 0.3 to 0.5. Three seeds add seed variance, and the report gives each seed's numbers. A gain of 1 point or more out of 10 should be resolved.

## Seeds

Pilot: seed 0, 40 steps, exploratory. Confirmatory: training seeds 1, 2 and 3. Training deals from seed 100,000; test deals 400,000 to 400,199. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Jobs, `l40sx1` ($1.80 an hour, 48 GB), through `hfrun.py`. A step plays 32 games of about 20 turns, about 640 replies of at most 128 tokens from prompts of about 450 tokens, and trains on all of them: about 370,000 tokens through a 2B model with LoRA. That is roughly 30 to 45 seconds on an L40S, so 120 steps take 1 to 1.5 hours.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The pilot must show that the mean score rises and fallbacks fall within 40 steps. If not, the training runs do not start, and the README reports that training did not take at this setting. If both primary hypotheses fail, the README gives the differences and states that self-play training did not raise the score with strangers. If H-seeds fails and self-play rose, it reports the gap between self-play and cross-play as the result.

## Threats to validity

- **A smaller game.** Two colors make fewer conventions possible, which may help cross-play. Five-color transfer is exploratory only.
- **The rule-based player is ours.** It was written for this plan, before training. A different rule set would give a different number.
- **Self-play with shared weights** trains both seats as one policy. Seeds still train apart, which is what cross-play tests.
- **Small budget.** 120 steps of 32 games is about 3,800 games per seed. MARSHAL's training was larger, and the report does not claim to reproduce its numbers.

## Amendment, 2026-11-20, before any run: H4's result, the methods amendments, and the seed count

Written before the pilot. No H3 job has run and no H3 code has been written.

- **H4 has reported.** Its two-color game, deals and ceilings are the ones H3 uses, and its policies give H3's secondary reference on the same 200 test deals (`hanabi/results/h4.md`). H4's policies were scored on deals 400,000 to 401,999; H3's test deals are the first 200 of those. They have not been played by any language model, so they are fresh for H3's arms; the H4 comparison on them is labeled as using H4's test deals a second time.
- **Minimum detectable effect** (METHOD.md, 2026-11-20). H3's primary intervals resample deals and seeds. As a planning value, H4's cross-play interval at α = 0.01 had a half-width of 0.22 points with 8 seeds; a language model's seeds may vary more, so the plan doubles it. With 3 seeds, the half-width is about 0.44 × √(8/3) ≈ 0.72, and the detectable effect 2.80 / 1.96 × 0.72 ≈ 1.0 point of 10. With 5 seeds it is about 0.80. The plan's stated effect worth finding is 1 point. With 3 seeds the plan is powered only at that size, and is underpowered if the seed spread is larger than assumed.
- **Seeds.** METHOD.md makes five training seeds the default where five fit the approved cap. Five seeds cost two more training runs (2 × $2.70 at the plan's estimate) and a larger evaluation (20 ordered seed pairs instead of 6): about $16.80 expected, with a hard cap of $19.80 (11 hours of `l40sx1` timeouts). The plan as merged costs about $10.40 with a cap of $13.50. Either is above this month's ceiling of about $8 for the repository, so H3 is not proposed for this month. When it is approved, the approval names the seed count: five seeds under a cap of $19.80, or three under $13.50 with the reason (budget) stated in the report, and three remains the floor.
- **Model partners are a proxy.** The trained seeds and the rule-based player are model partners. The README will state H3's result as a proxy result.
- **Equivalence and nulls.** A difference whose interval holds 0 is reported as "no detectable difference" with its interval. The plan sets no equivalence margin, so it makes no claim of equivalence.
- **Reply caps.** The pilot reports the share of replies that reach the 128-token cap. Above 5%, the cap is raised to 256 before the training runs, by this rule.
