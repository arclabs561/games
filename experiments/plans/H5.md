# H5. Hanabi: H2's three-player part, with the reply cap fixed

## Question

In three-player Hanabi, how many points below the information strategy, and below each deal's open-game ceiling, does gpt-oss-120b score in self-play, when its replies are no longer cut off by the token cap?

## Why it matters

H2 planned this comparison as a secondary part and did not run it: the budget ran out (H2's second amendment). H2's two-player numbers for gpt-oss-120b are partly limited by the harness: 24% of its turns reached the 4,096-token cap before writing a move and got a fallback discard (`hanabi/results/h2-harness.md`). A methods review of the folder adds that H2 scores a game 0 when the third life is lost, the official rule, while Ramesh et al. (arXiv:2601.18077) record the score at the moment of failure, so the two are not on one scale.

Three players is where the folder has a non-language strategy to compare with on the same deals: `information.py` plays the hat-guessing strategy for three to five players, and at three players it scores 22.254 ± 0.032 on 10,000 seeded deals (`results/information.md`; simulated with HanSim's discard rule, which the README finds changes nothing measurable; a generalization made here). A deal-by-deal comparison separates points the deal rules out, points a known strategy recovers, and points the model loses.

- If the model's gap to the information strategy is much larger than the strategy's gap to the ceiling, the model's loss is play, not the deal.
- If the fixed cap removes the fallbacks and the score stays far below the strategy, H2's two-player gap was not mainly the harness. If the score rises far above H2's two-player 6.37, the cap was a large part of it. (The player counts differ, so this is read with care.)

## Design

- **Deals.** Three players, seeds 0 to 29 of `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=3), seed)`. No model has played them. Every arm plays the same deals in the same order.
- **Model.** gpt-oss-120b (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`), pinned with the router's suffix to Novita (`openai/gpt-oss-120b:novita`), the provider of H2's gpt-oss arms, reasoning effort medium, temperature 0, seed 0, all three seats the same model. Substitute if Novita does not serve it at the pilot: DeepInfra, by an amendment before any test game.
- **Observation.** `llm.observation(..., deduction=True)`, as in H2's primary arm, and H2's reply format and fallback (the first legal discard, or the first legal move when all eight tokens are on the table).
- **Reply cap.** 16,384 tokens (`--max-tokens`, the module's default since `h2-harness.md`). The pilot decides between two settings by a rule fixed now: if at most 5% of the pilot's replies at medium effort reach the cap, the test uses medium and 16,384. Otherwise the test uses reasoning effort low with 16,384, and if that also cuts off more than 5%, the plan stops and an amendment comes before any test game. Cut-off replies and fallbacks are counted and reported.
- **Per deal, computed here without model calls:** the open-game ceiling (`upper_bound.verdict`, then `best_score` for a lost deal, and Solver B as the check), and the information strategy's score (`information.play_info_game`, official rules), which is deterministic for a deal.

## Primary metric and test

- **Gap to the information strategy.** For each deal, the strategy's score minus the model's score, official scoring (0 when the third life is lost). The mean over the 30 deals, with a 95% interval by bootstrap over deals (10,000 resamples, seed 0).

There is no null to reject in the usual sense: the strategy is expected to lead by many points. The result is the size of the gap and its interval. The decision the plan makes from it: if the interval's upper end is below 3 points, the README says that the model plays within 3 points of a fixed convention at three players; if its lower end is above 10, that the model is far below it with the cap fixed.

## Secondary metrics

- The gap to the open-game ceiling, per deal and on average.
- Both scorings: official (0 on the third life) and the score at the moment of failure, as Ramesh et al. record it. Comparisons with their numbers use theirs only.
- Mean score, perfect games, games that lose all three lives (Wilson intervals), turns per game, fallbacks per game, replies cut off at the cap, median reply tokens, and the median latency per call.
- **Behavior,** from replaying every game in `rules.py`: plays, misplays, discards and hints per seat and game; the share of hints followed by a play of a touched card; lives lost to fallback moves and to the model's own moves, as in `h2-harness.md`.
- **Repeat check,** per METHOD.md: 20 prompts from the pilot and 20 from the first test games sent twice more; the share whose move changed, with the providers that served them.
- **For comparison, without new runs:** WTFWThat's published three-player score (hanabi.rs README, 20,000 seeds: 24.7946) and the information strategy's 22.254, both from other deals or many deals, labeled as such.

## Minimum detectable effect

The model's score in H2's two-player primary arm had a standard deviation of 5.48 over 30 deals (`results/h2.json`; computed here). The information strategy's score varies less. With a standard deviation of about 6 for the paired difference, 30 deals give a half-width of 1.96 × 6 / √30 ≈ 2.1 points and a detectable difference of 2.80 × 6 / √30 ≈ 3.1 points. The question is the size of a gap that published results put above 10 points, so 30 deals place it to within about 2 points either way. They do not rank models, and no second model is run.

## Seeds

Deal seeds 0 to 29 (three players). Pilot deals 1000 and 1001 (three players), exploratory and not scored. Model calls at temperature 0 and seed 0. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers from a `cpu-basic` job through `experiments/hfrun.py`, group `hanabi`, with the meter of `hanabi/hosted.py`, which reserves room for a full reply at the cap before each call. Prices: Novita's listed $0.05 and $0.25 per million input and output tokens (H2's meter); the job reads the listing at start.

- In H2's two-player primary arm, gpt-oss-120b used 62.5 turns a game, about 825 prompt and 2,773 completion tokens a call (some cut off at 4,096), and $0.046 a game.
- A three-player prompt shows two other hands, so this plan assumes 1,100 prompt tokens. With the cap at 16,384, uncut replies run longer than H2's censored mean; the plan assumes 3,800 completion tokens. A call then costs 1,100 × $0.05/10⁶ + 3,800 × $0.25/10⁶ ≈ $0.0010, and a game of about 65 turns about $0.065.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pilot: 2 games at medium effort, then 2 at low if the cap rule says so; repeat check on 20 prompts | 2 to 4 | $0.13 to $0.25 | $0.40 | 2 h |
| Test: 30 games, 8 at a time, and the repeat check on 20 prompts | 30 | $1.95 | $3.20 | 6 h |
| Ceilings and the information strategy on 30 deals | | here | | |

**Expected about $2.10 to $2.30. Hard cap $3.60**, the sum of the two job caps. If the test's cap stops it, the games finished form a complete prefix of deals in order, and the report says where it stopped. At about 35 seconds a call (H2's medium-effort replies) and 8 games at a time, 30 games take about 2.5 to 3.5 hours, inside the timeout; the job stops starting games at 5 hours (`--stop-after 300`) and stops games in progress at 5.5 hours.

## Entry points

`hanabi.h2 play` already takes `--players 3`, `--max-tokens` and `--reasoning-effort`, pins providers and logs who served each call. To be written:

- `python -m hanabi.h5 report --runs <folder> --out hanabi/results/h5.json`: ceilings, the information strategy per deal, both scorings, the bootstrap and the behavior counts, reusing `h2.py`'s replay.
- A test that `play_info_game` on three-player seeds 0 to 29 is deterministic and that the replay reproduces each recorded score, on a stub model.

The jobs:

- pilot: `python -m hanabi.h2 play --model openai/gpt-oss-120b --provider novita --players 3 --seeds 1000-1001 --deduction on --max-tokens 16384 --reasoning-effort medium --cap 0.20 --label pilot --out OUT`
- test: the same with `--seeds 0-29 --cap 3.20 --label confirmatory`, and the effort the pilot's rule selects.

## Outputs

- In git: `hanabi/results/h5.json` (per deal: ceiling, strategy score, model score under both scorings, fallbacks, cut-off replies, tokens, cost), the run summaries in `results/h5-runs/`, and a README section beside H2's.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/h5/`: every prompt and reply.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- Each game is played once. A game that fails for reasons outside the model (provider errors after the retries) is replayed from the start once; otherwise it is reported as missing.
- If the pilot's cap rule fails at both efforts, no test game is played and the README reports the pilot's cut-off shares.
- **A null-like result.** If the gap's interval holds values from 3 to 10, the README reports it as it is, with the detectable effect, and says that 30 deals did not place the gap more finely.

## Threats to validity

- **Self-play of one model.** This is not play with strangers or people; per METHOD.md, it is a proxy.
- **The information strategy is a generalization made here** for three players, not the published five-player version.
- **One prompt,** fixed and not tuned, as in H2.
- **Provider nondeterminism and drift,** measured by the repeat check and logged per call.
