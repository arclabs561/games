# C13. Codenames: C3's trained givers and the count-1 rule, scored by language-model guessers

## Question

C3 found that a giver trained against a population of embedding guessers scores 0.31 words per turn (0.26 to 0.36) more with a held-out embedding guesser than one trained against itself. A count-1 rule with no training recovers about 97% of that gain under the same guessers (exploratory, `codenames.cautious`, on the branch `fix/codenames`). With guessers outside the embedding family, hosted language models of two other families, (a) does the population giver still beat the self-play giver, and (b) does it beat the count-1 rule?

## Why it matters

C3's result is the README's one positive giver result, and every guesser that scored it is an embedding model. C11 found that C10's population pick lost its whole lead (0.22 under potion) under gpt-oss-120b and Llama 3.3, and that "what changes is the count": the population arm asks for fewer words. A methods review of C3 reran two tasks and found the count-1 rule as good as the trained policy. Both point to the same reading: population training learns caution, and caution may be what strangers of any family reward. Language-model guessers test whether the caution transfers outside the family, and whether training adds anything to it.

- If population beats self-play under language models, C3 holds outside the embedding family.
- If population does not beat count-1, the README says that what C3's training learned is caution, which a rule with no training gives.
- If neither beats count-1 and population does not beat self-play, C3 is a result about embedding guessers, as C10's lead was.

## Arms

All from committed weights and code; nothing is trained.

- **Givers:** potion and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, C3's two givers.
- **Task per giver:** C3's headline tasks, potion with bge held out and Qwen3 with EmbeddingGemma 2 held out. The language-model guessers are outside every task's population, so one task per giver suffices.
- **Per giver:** *start* (maximize expected words, no training), *self-play* (5 training seeds), *population* (5 seeds), and *count-1* (`codenames.cautious`: the giver's own likeliest single hit, count 1; deterministic). The policies' θ come from `codenames/results/c3.json`, and `diagnose_c3` regenerates their picks, as it already checks against C3's committed values.
- **Clues as the policies choose them,** from C3's candidate list (`words.legal`). The strict rulebook check (`legality.strict_legal`) flags 16.6% of potion's population picks and 3.5% of its self-play picks on C3's boards (`results/diagnostics/legality.md`); the primary keeps the picks as they are, and a secondary restricts to boards where every arm's pick is strictly legal.

## Guessers

gpt-oss-120b pinned to DeepInfra (reasoning effort low, 1,024 tokens) and Llama 3.3 70B Instruct pinned to Novita (256 tokens), temperature 0, seed 0, C4's guesser prompt with the action's count, one reply per distinct (board, clue, count), played out by `c4.play_turn`, as in C11. Exact values under the held-out embedding guesser and EAT come free on the same boards.

## Boards

Test: 400 fresh boards of seed 13004. Pilot: 10 boards of seed 13999, not scored. No earlier plan uses seeds from 13000 to 13999.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05. The value of an arm on a board is its played-out turn value, averaged over the two guessers and, for trained arms, over the five seeds.

- **H-self.** Population minus self-play, averaged over the two givers.
- **H-rule.** Population minus count-1, averaged over the two givers.

95% intervals by the stratified bootstrap of METHOD.md: boards resampled (shared across arms and givers), and within each giver its five seeds resampled, with population and self-play of one seed resampled as a pair (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The p-value for Holm is twice the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0. Each is read in both directions; an interval holding 0 is "no detectable difference" with the interval, not a tie, unless it lies inside the margin below.

**Equivalence margin.** δ = 0.10 words per turn for H-rule: if its whole interval lies inside −0.10 to 0.10, the report says that population training and the count-1 rule are equivalent within 0.10 under these guessers.

## Secondary metrics

- Each giver, each guesser and each arm separately; start against all arms.
- The same contrasts under the held-out embedding guesser (exact) on these boards, as the in-family reference.
- Mean count, words found and assassin rate per arm and guesser.
- Boards where every arm's pick passes `strict_legal`.
- Repeat check: 20 prompts per guesser sent twice more at the start; changed values, empty and cut-off replies, median latency.

## Minimum detectable effect

C11 measured a standard deviation of 1.10 for the mean over two language-model guessers of a played-out difference between two giver arms. Averaging five seeds lowers it somewhat; the plan keeps 1.10. With 400 boards, 3.08 × 1.10 / √400 ≈ 0.17 at Holm's first level and 2.80 × 1.10 / √400 ≈ 0.15 at 5%. The effect worth finding is half of C3's 0.31. The plan is powered for about that and not for smaller effects.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers from `cpu-basic` jobs through `experiments/hfrun.py`, with the meter of `codenames/hosted.py`. Costs per call measured in C11: $0.000044 for gpt-oss-120b, $0.000035 for Llama 3.3.

- Distinct actions per board: up to 12 per giver (start, count-1, five self-play seeds, five population seeds); trained seeds often agree, and the plan assumes 6 per giver, 12 per board. The pilot measures it.
- 400 boards × 12 × ($0.000044 + $0.000035) ≈ $0.38. Pilot and repeat check about $0.02.

| Job | Cap | Timeout |

## Entry points

- Needs `codenames/cautious.py` from the branch `fix/codenames`, merged first.
- To be written: `python -m codenames.c13 lists --seed 13004 --n 400 --out codenames/results/c13-picks.txt` (picks of every arm, from C3's θ, run here), `c13 guess` and `c13 repeat` (reusing `c11 guess`), and `c13 score --out codenames/results/c13.json`. Tests: the regenerated picks equal C3's committed values on C3's first three test boards; the stratified bootstrap pairs population and self-play by seed.

## Outputs

In git: `codenames/results/c13.json`, `c13-picks.txt`, `c13-runs.json`, and a paragraph in the C3 section of the README. In the private dataset, under `runs/c13/`: every prompt and reply.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The pilot checks providers, reading and cost; nothing from it is scored. The test is sent once; a failed job is rerun once from the start. If H-self fails, the README says that C3's lead over self-play was not detected with language-model guessers, with the interval and the detectable effect, and the C3 claim is scoped to embedding guessers.

## Threats to validity

- Language models are model partners: per METHOD.md, a proxy for people.
- One task per giver: the policies of other tasks may differ.
- One reply per prompt from nondeterministic providers.
