# C7. Codenames: reinforcement learning of a small language model that chooses among legal clues

## Question

A current small language model is trained by group-relative policy optimization to choose among the same legal clues as C6. Its reward is computed exactly from a population of embedding guessers. Do held-out guessers understand its clues better than those of the seven-feature policy of `rl.py`? Both are trained against the same population, on the same candidates and boards.

## Why it matters

C5 was closed unrun because C4's language-model givers lost to the embedding giver, half of it through illegal clues. C6 removes that failure: every candidate is legal and has a vector for every guesser. What is left is a contextual bandit with 120 actions and an exact reward. `rl.py` already learns such a policy over seven hand-made features, and C3 measures it across seeds. A language model reads the words themselves, so it could learn something the features miss, such as a second sense of a board word. It could also overfit its training guessers in ways the features cannot.

- If the language model beats the feature policy with held-out guessers, a trained small model adds something the embedding numbers do not hold. A larger model or a richer prompt (counts above 4, a clue outside the list) becomes the next step.
- If they are level, the cheap policy is enough for this task, and the README says that the language model's training cost bought nothing with these partners.
- If the language model wins with its training guessers and loses with held-out ones, the gap is the other-play effect (Hu et al., 2020) in a new form, and the README reports it as such.

## Model and method, as of 2026-11-19

- **Model: Qwen3.5-2B** (`Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B`, instruction-tuned, created 2026-02-28, Apache 2.0). It is the newest dense Qwen model under 9B: Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 (April and August 2026) come in 27B and larger sizes only. Its card lists 2B parameters in the language model, 24 layers that mix Gated DeltaNet and gated attention, and a vision encoder that this plan does not use. TRL's GRPO documentation lists it among the models tested for agent training, and vLLM 0.34.0 registers its architecture. Thinking is turned off in the chat template.
- **Fallback: Gemma 4 E2B** (`google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, created 2026-03-02, Apache 2.0 for the whole Gemma 4 family). It is used only if Qwen3.5-2B fails to train in the pilot, and the amendment records why.
- **Considered and not chosen.** Two candidates are older: SmolLM3-3B (2025-07, Apache 2.0) and Phi-4-mini-reasoning (2025-04, MIT). The newest small Llama is still Llama 3.2 (2024). NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano-4B (2026-03) is a hybrid Mamba model under NVIDIA's own license. OLMo 3 7B (Apache 2.0) is larger than the task needs.
- **Algorithm.** `trl.GRPOTrainer` from TRL 1.18.1 (2026-11-17), with its default loss, `loss_type="dapo"`. That loss averages over all tokens in the batch, which removes the length bias that Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) found in the original loss. `epsilon_high=0.28` is DAPO's clip-higher setting (Yu et al., 2025). The KL weight is 0, TRL's default, as in DAPO and in Deshpande et al. (2026). Rewards are scaled within each group, TRL's default.
- **Not used, and why.** GSPO's sequence-level ratio (Zheng et al., 2025) was designed for mixture-of-experts models, and a reply here is a few tokens, so token and sequence ratios nearly coincide. GDPO (arXiv:2601.05242) normalizes several rewards separately; this plan has one reward. On-policy distillation needs a teacher that gives better clues, and C4 found none among the hosted models.

## Design

- **Candidates and prompt.** Exactly C6's: the 120 actions of `rl.actions` in the EmbeddingGemma 2 space, and C6's fixed prompt. So the untrained model is C6's prompt applied to a 2B model.
- **Reward.** The exact expected turn value (`luce.py`, the README's costs) of the chosen action, averaged over the four training guessers. A reply that names no listed clue, or a count outside 1 to 4, scores −1. The reward is computed in the job from vectors; it costs no model calls. A test checks it against `rl.rewards` on fixed boards.
- **Training guessers:** wordllama, granite, bge-small and harrier-oss-v1-270m.
- **Held out from training:** potion, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B and harrier-oss-v1-0.6b. Also held out are the potion map that C1 trained on human guesses (its base model is held out too) and the EAT association guesser. EmbeddingGemma 2 proposes the candidates and is not a scoring guesser.
- **Arms**, one factor apart:
  1. *Language model:* Qwen3.5-2B with LoRA (rank 32, α 64, on every linear projection of the language model), trained with the settings below;
  2. *Feature policy:* `rl.py` with EmbeddingGemma 2 as giver, the same four training guessers and the same candidates, at its default settings (400 Adam steps of 16 boards, learning rate 0.05).
- **Fixed settings** for arm 1, with no search: 300 steps of 16 boards, 8 samples per board, learning rate 2e-5, temperature 1 while training, at most 16 new tokens, truncated replies masked from the loss (`mask_truncated_completions`). Training boards come from board seed 7000 onward, fresh for each step. Generation uses transformers inside the trainer; a reply of a few tokens does not need vLLM.
- **Test boards:** C6's 200 boards of seed 6004, with greedy decoding.
- **Further baselines on the test boards:** the untrained Qwen3.5-2B, C6's embedding first choice, and C6's chosen hosted model. Their values come from C6's run where C6 scored them.

## Primary metric and test

Mean expected turn value over the three held-out embedding guessers, arm 1 minus arm 2, paired by test board. Three seeds for arm 1; seed i of arm 1 is paired with seed i of arm 2. The 95% interval is a bootstrap that resamples boards (shared across seed pairs) and seed pairs, 10,000 times with seed 0. The language model is said to beat the feature policy if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- The same difference under the human-fitted potion map, and under EAT.
- Each arm's value with its four training guessers, the self-play side, and the gap between that and the held-out value.
- Arm 1 against the untrained model, C6's first choice and C6's chosen hosted model.
- Arm 2's seeds 4 and 5, which are cheap, to show the feature policy's own spread.
- The share of replies that score −1, the mean reward and the policy's entropy, every 20 steps.
- **Behavior:** the share of test boards where the pick is the embedding first choice; the mean count; the clues' rarity; the number of distinct clues. A policy that learns one safe clue type would show here before it shows in the reward.

## Sample size

C6's argument gives a half-width near 0.05 to 0.10 for a paired difference over 200 boards. Three seed pairs add seed variance, which the pilot estimates. A difference near 0.15 can be detected if seeds vary about as much as boards, and a smaller one cannot, as in C5. Three seeds is METHOD.md's minimum for expensive runs, and the report gives every seed's difference.

## Seeds

Pilot: seed 0, 100 steps, exploratory. Confirmatory: training seeds 1, 2 and 3 for arm 1, and 1 to 5 for arm 2. Training boards from seed 7000; test boards seed 6004. Bootstrap seed 0.

A step generates 128 replies of at most 16 tokens from prompts of about 400 tokens, and trains on them: about 50,000 tokens through a 2B model with LoRA, roughly 10 to 20 seconds on an L4. 300 steps then take 1 to 1.5 hours, and evaluation on 200 boards a minute.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The pilot must show that mean reward rises and the share of −1 replies falls within 100 steps. If it does not, the confirmatory runs do not start, the README reports that training did not take at this setting, and the fallback model is tried only after an amendment that the owner approves. If the primary interval includes 0, the README reports that the trained language model did not beat the feature policy with held-out guessers, with each seed's result. A run that times out is rerun once.

## Threats to validity

- **One small model, one setting.** A null result says nothing about larger models or other learning rates.
- **The list caps both arms.** Both choose from the same 120 actions, so neither can do better than C6's oracle.
- **The partners are vector models.** The held-out guessers are the same kind as the training ones. The potion map is fitted to people, which is the nearest this plan comes to them.
- **Contamination.** Qwen3.5 may have seen Codenames text. It has not seen these boards, which are new.

## Amendment, 2026-11-19

Recorded before the pilot. None of these changes the primary metric or its test.

- **Entry points.** The job is `python -m codenames.rlvr --seed S --steps N --eval test --table URL --out OUT`. The rewards of all 120 actions on every training board are computed beforehand on CPU, from the vectors and by `rl.rewards`, by `python -m codenames.c7 table`, and the job reads them from that table. This gives the same values and spares the GPU job the vectors. A test checks the table against `rl.rewards` and against `players.expected_value` one action at a time. The table is committed to the branch `compute/codenames-c7`, and the job checks its SHA-256.
- **Arm 2** is `python -m codenames.c7 feature --seed S`: `rl.fit` at its defaults (400 Adam steps of 16 boards, learning rate 0.05) on the same 4,800 training boards and candidates as arm 1, with rl.py's seven features from the EmbeddingGemma 2 space. `rl.py`'s own command line deals its own boards and limits clues to EAT cues, so `--population` was not added to it. Arm 2 takes seconds and runs on the local machine, not on GitHub Actions.
- **Training boards.** Step s (from 0) uses the 16 boards dealt from seed 7000 + s, the same for every training seed. The training seed sets the LoRA initialization and the sampling.
- **The pilot is scored on C6's 50 validation boards** (seed 6005), not on the test boards, so that the test boards are used once (METHOD.md). It runs at seed 0 for 100 steps; no confirmatory seed is 0.
- **Replies on the test boards that name no listed clue** get the embedding giver's first choice, C6's rule. Their number is reported, and so is the primary difference with such replies valued at −1 by every guesser.
- **Settings the plan left open**, all TRL or PEFT defaults: the learning rate decays linearly to 0 with no warmup; LoRA dropout is 0; 128 replies a step in 4 micro-batches of 32. With one gradient step per batch of samples (TRL's default `num_iterations=1`), the probability ratio is 1 at every token, so the clip bounds, `epsilon_high=0.28` included, are never reached. The loss is then the token-averaged policy gradient with advantages scaled within each group. The setting is kept as planned.
- **Model loading.** Qwen3.5-2B is loaded as a causal language model, so the vision tower is not loaded, and `all-linear` LoRA covers every linear layer of the language model and nothing else. The job fails if any language-model weight is missing.
- **Kernels.** `flash-linear-attention` 0.6.0 joins the `codenames-gpu` group, so Gated DeltaNet runs with its Triton kernels on the GPU. The CPU test of the loop (a random Qwen3.5 model of 68,000 trainable parameters) uses transformers' PyTorch path for the same layers.
- **Budget.** The owner approved a hard cap of $7 for C7 in total.
