# H2. Hanabi: hosted language models against the ceiling

## Question

How many points below the open-game ceiling do hosted language models score in two-player Hanabi, deal by deal? And, for three players, how far below the information strategy?

## Why it matters

`hanabi/README.md` puts published agents against the ceiling: self-play RL agents trail it by 0.4 to 0.9 points with two players, and o3 by about 9. The language-model numbers come from other papers, with their own rules and deals. Scoring models on the README's seeded deals, with the solver's verdict for each deal, separates two things: points lost because a deal cannot be won, and points lost to play.

- A gap to the ceiling much larger than the gap to the information strategy would show the models losing to play rather than to the deal. The ceiling result says that almost every deal is winnable.
- If deduction help (the possible values of each own card) closes most of the gap, the models' weakness is bookkeeping, not strategy, as Ramesh et al. suggest with their Watson and Sherlock settings.

## Scope

The README reports the information strategy for three to five players only (`information.py`). With two players the scheme has only 2(P − 1) = 2 hint values and a single partner to sum over, so the hat trick does little. The comparison with it is therefore made at three players, as a secondary part. Two players is the primary setting because the published language-model results and AH2AC2 use it.

The README's plan item 3, pairing a model with partners that play the information strategy, is dropped. Those partners read every hint as a code, and a model that does not know the code would make them misplay. The result would measure the code, not the model.

## Models

Two models, from the C4 list, chosen now: gpt-oss-120b (reasoning effort medium) and DeepSeek-V3 (the newest V3 release served). Substitutes in order: Qwen3 32B with thinking on, then Llama 3.3 70B. All seats in a game are the same model. Temperature 0 and a fixed seed where accepted; ids, providers, dates and parameters are logged per call.

## Design

- **Deals.** Two players, seeds 0 to 29 of `rules.seeded_deck`. The order is fixed: seed 0 first. Every model and setting plays the same deals.
- **Settings.** `observation(..., deduction=False)` and `deduction=True`. One factor apart.
- **Fallback.** As in `play_text_game`: the first legal discard, else the first legal move. Fallbacks are counted.
- **Per deal**, from H1's results or computed here: the solver's verdict and the best score reachable in the open game (25 for a won deal).
- **Three players, secondary.** Seeds 0 to 19 with deduction on, one model (the better of the two on the primary metric), against `information.play_info_game` on the same deals.

## Primary metric and test

For each model, with deduction on: the mean over the 30 deals of (best open-game score − model's score). 95% interval by bootstrap over deals (10,000 resamples, seed 0). There is no null hypothesis to reject: the ceiling is above any real strategy, so the gap is positive for every model. The number reported is its size and its interval.

## Secondary metrics

- The same gap with deduction off, and the paired difference between the two settings, bootstrap over deals.
- Mean score, perfect-game share, games that lose all three lives, and unusable replies per game.
- The three-player gap to the information strategy on the same deals, paired by deal.
- For comparison, without new runs: the gaps of the agents in the README's table and of the information strategy at three players.

## Sample size

Language-model scores vary widely between games. The papers the README cites report means of 15 to 18 points for the best models, and a game that loses all its lives scores 0. With a standard deviation of 5 to 8 points over deals, 30 deals give a half-width of 2 to 3 points. That is enough to place the gap (about 7 to 10 points, if the published numbers carry over) and to see a deduction effect of 3 points or more. It is not enough to rank two models that differ by a point, and the report will not rank them.

## Seeds

Deal seeds 0 to 29 (two players) and 0 to 19 (three players). Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers, from a `cpu-basic` job through `hfrun.py`. A two-player game takes about 60 to 75 turns. A prompt is 1,500 to 1,900 characters, about 400 to 500 tokens, measured by playing seeded deals with a stub. Replies are capped at 512 tokens with reasoning on. That makes about 60,000 tokens a game. Primary: 2 models × 2 settings × 30 games = 120 games, about 7 million tokens. Secondary: 20 games, about 1.5 million. At listed prices for these models that is $2 to $5. Hard cap $4, enforced by a cost meter in the job, as in C4. Games run in the fixed order, model by model, deduction on before off, and a stop at the cap leaves a complete prefix of deals for each setting it reached. The report says where it stopped. This plan needs approval before its jobs.

## Entry points

`llm.observation`, `llm.parse_action` and `llm.play_text_game` exist. To be written:

- A `complete` function for Inference Providers, shared with Codenames. It lives in each package (`hanabi/hosted.py`), since each game is its own package; the shared part is small.
- `python -m hanabi.h2 play --model <id> --players 2 --seeds 0-29 --deduction on --out OUT`: one JSON line per game, with the score, lives lost, fallbacks, the action list and token counts.
- `python -m hanabi.h2 report --runs <folder> --verdicts hanabi/results/h1.json --out hanabi/results/h2.json`.

## Outputs

- In git: `hanabi/results/h2.json`, with per-game scores, settings, costs and model ids, and a "Language-model players" section in the README in place of the plan there.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/h2/`: every prompt and reply.

## Stopping rules and a null result

Each game is played once. A game that fails for reasons outside the model (provider errors after three retries) is replayed from the start once and is otherwise reported as missing. If deduction makes no difference within the interval, that is reported as the result.

## Threats to validity

- **Prompt.** The observation text is fixed and was not tuned. Better prompting or a conventions guide, such as the H-group conventions that AH2AC2's DeepSeek-R1 entry used, could close part of the gap.
- **Same model in every seat.** This is self-play for language models. It is not ad-hoc play with strangers, which AH2AC2 measures.
- **Drift and nondeterminism**, as in C4.
- **Few deals.** 30 deals give a coarse interval, the price of staying inside the budget.

## Amendments

### 2026-11-19 (UTC), before the confirmatory runs

A pilot of 12 two-player games on seeds 1000 to 1003, which the confirmatory run does not use, showed that the plan cannot be run as written. Its numbers are exploratory and are not part of the result.

- **Reply cap: 4,096 tokens instead of 512.** With 512, the reply budget is spent on reasoning before the move is written. Of the pilot's calls at 512, 28 of 36 for gpt-oss-120b and 23 of 29 for DeepSeek-V3.2 stopped at the cap. Each such reply gets the fallback, and the fallback is a blind play whenever all 8 tokens are on the table, so all 8 games lost their three lives within 3 to 15 turns and scored 0. The result would measure the cap, not the play. At 4,096, gpt-oss-120b still stopped at the cap on 42 of 149 calls (median reply about 3,000 tokens) and DeepSeek-V3.2 on none of 46 (median about 1,100). A higher cap does not fit the budget. Truncated replies are counted and reported with the fallbacks.
- **Models and providers, fixed now.** gpt-oss-120b (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`, reasoning effort medium) through Novita, and DeepSeek-V3.2 (`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2`, the newest V3 release served, in its default non-thinking mode) through DeepInfra, the only provider serving it with pay-as-you-go access. Each model keeps one provider for all its games.
- **Cost meter.** The usage objects report token counts but no cost, so the meter prices each call at the provider's listed price (router.huggingface.co/v1/models, fetched at the start of each run) times the reported tokens. This is not the bill.
- **Order.** At 4,096 tokens a gpt-oss-120b game costs about $0.057 (two pilot games), against the plan's estimate of 60,000 tokens a game, so the plan's order (model by model, deduction on before off) would spend the cap before the second model's primary arm. The order is now: the primary arms first (deduction on, both models, all 30 deals), then deduction off for DeepSeek-V3.2, then deduction off for gpt-oss-120b, then the three-player games with the better model. Each run has its own cap, the caps and the pilot add up to at most $4, and a run stopped by its cap keeps a complete prefix of deals. The report says which arms were cut and where.
- **Time limits.** Each job stops starting games after a set time and stops games in progress a little later, so that its outputs are stored. A game stopped this way is reported as stopped, not as a result.
