# P2. Pokémon: does the full-battle equilibrium survive a policy that switches?

## Question

The folder's full-battle team game (63 teams, a round robin in Pokémon Showdown) was played with one fixed policy: use the most damaging move, never switch by choice. If both sides instead use a policy that switches out of bad matchups, ported from poke-env's `SimpleHeuristicsPlayer`, is the published equilibrium still hard to exploit, or does some team beat it by more than battle noise explains?

## Why it matters

The README's full-battle result is an equilibrium on 15 teams, the heaviest at 26.8% (20.4% to 32.2%), with the six most used Pokémon at 13.7% (`pokemon/results/team-study.md`; simulated, 20 battles per pair, seed 0). A methods review of the folder points out that this is an equilibrium of a meta-game under one weak policy, and that empirical game theory treats the policy as part of the strategy. It asks for "one stronger off-the-shelf policy" before anything is said about teams. The README's open questions ask for a policy that switches by choice.

- If the published equilibrium is about as hard to exploit under the switching policy as under a fresh replicate of the old one, the team result is not an artifact of never switching, at this resolution.
- If some team exploits it under the switching policy by more than noise allows, the team result depends on the policy, and the README names the policy in every equilibrium claim and reports the new equilibrium beside the old.

## Policies

Both use the folder's sets (one ability and four damaging moves per Pokémon, `teams_moves.csv`) and its battle stream (`teams_showdown.js`, Showdown at the pinned commit `5b88c476`), which seeds every battle.

- **D, the published policy** (`teams_showdown.py` as it is): lead with the best level-4 matchup against the opposing six; each turn, the move with the highest expected damage (`teams_model.expected_damage`); never switch by choice; after a faint, the bench Pokémon with the best level-4 matchup.
- **S, switching.** D with one change, the voluntary switch of poke-env's `SimpleHeuristicsPlayer` (poke-env 0.17.0, MIT; `src/poke_env/player/baselines.py`, read 2026-11-20), ported into the stream harness:
  - matchup(a, b) = (the best multiplier of a's types against b) − (the best multiplier of b's types against a), +0.1 if a's base Speed is higher and −0.1 if lower, +0.4 × a's HP fraction − 0.4 × b's HP fraction (poke-env's `_estimate_matchup`, with its constants 0.1 and 0.4);
  - switch out when some bench Pokémon has matchup > 0 against the opponent's active Pokémon and either the active one has matchup < −2 or its Defense or Special Defense stage is −3 or lower, or its stronger attacking stat's stage is −3 or lower (poke-env's `_should_switch_out`, threshold −2);
  - switch in the bench Pokémon with the highest matchup.
  The move choice is D's, not poke-env's base-power estimate, so that the two policies differ in switching only. Entry-hazard rules are left out: no set here has a hazard or a removal move.
- **Turn limit.** A battle that reaches 300 turns is stopped and counted as a tie. Two switching policies can trade switches; the report gives the share of battles stopped this way.

poke-env itself is not used: it plays through a server that chooses each battle's seed, so battles could not be rerun. Its logic is ported, with its license notice in the module.

## Design

- **Teams:** the 63 teams of the published round robin (`teams-battles.json`), unchanged.
- **Matrices,** each a full round robin of 1,953 pairs, 40 battles per pair, sides alternating, seeds from labels that no earlier run used:
  - **M_D′**, policy D on both sides, label `p2-replicate`: a fresh replicate of the published game, the noise baseline.
  - **M_S**, policy S on both sides, label `p2-switch`.
- **Strength check:** each of the 63 teams against itself, S on one side and D on the other, 40 battles each with sides alternating, label `p2-strength` (2,520 battles).
- **The published equilibrium σ_D** is the one in `teams-battles.json`'s matrix (20 battles per pair, seed 0), fixed now. It is not recomputed.
- **Payoffs:** M[i][j] = (wins − losses) / battles for team i against team j, in [−1, 1], as in the published game.

## Primary metric and test

- **Exploitability.** ε_X(σ) = max over teams j of Σ_i σ_i M_X[j][i], the best payoff any of the 63 teams gets against the mixture σ.
- **H-survive.** Δ = ε_S(σ_D) − ε_D′(σ_D). Battle noise alone makes ε positive in any replicate (computed for this plan from the published matrix, by redrawing each pair's 20 battles from its observed shares: a fresh replicate gives ε(σ_D) of 0.110 on average, with a standard deviation of 0.035, over 2,000 redrawn matrices, seed 0; exploratory, and to be redone by `pokemon.p2` with its code committed). Both new matrices have 40 battles per pair, so their noise is the same, and Δ removes it.
- 95% interval by bootstrap over battles within each pair, both matrices resampled independently (10,000 resamples, seed 0).
- **Decision.** If the interval lies above 0, the published equilibrium does not survive the switching policy, and the README says so with the team that exploits it most. If it holds 0, the equilibrium survives at this resolution, with the detectable effect. Below 0 would mean that switching makes σ_D harder to exploit, and is reported as such.

## Secondary metrics

- The equilibrium of M_S (LP for the support, then exact fractions on the support and every inequality checked, as the folder does), its support size and weights with bootstrap intervals, the overlap of its support with σ_D's (Jaccard), and the total-variation distance between the two mixtures.
- The six-most-used team's weight under S.
- The share of M_S that one ranking explains (the Hodge split of `hodge.py`), against 28.2% for the published matrix, and Kendall's τ between the two rankings of the 63 teams.
- **Strength check:** S's share of the mirror battles against D, with a Wilson interval. If S does not win more than half, the report says that the switching policy is not stronger than D in these battles, beside the primary.
- Per battle: turns, voluntary switches per side, ties at the turn limit, choice errors and damage checks against level 4's formula (as `teams_showdown.py` already counts).
- M_D′ against the published matrix: the replicate's own equilibrium and ε_D′, as a check of the noise model.

## Minimum detectable effect

At 40 battles per pair, the replicate computation above scales to a standard deviation of about 0.035 / √2 ≈ 0.025 for each ε, so Δ has a standard deviation near 0.035, and the detectable effect is 2.80 × 0.035 ≈ 0.10 payoff units. A team that exploits σ_D by 0.10 wins about 55% against it, net of ties. The effect worth finding is 0.20 (60%). The plan is powered for it. The bootstrap's own spread replaces the planning value in the report.

## Seeds

Battle seeds from the labels `p2-replicate`, `p2-switch` and `p2-strength` through `teams_showdown.seed`, so every battle can be rerun. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/experiment.yml`), free for this public repository. The two matrices and the strength check are about 159,000 battles. The published run's speed was not recorded; at a planning speed of 10 battles a second per runner, the work splits into 20 jobs of about 15 minutes each. The pilot measures the speed and the tie rate.

## Entry points

`teams_showdown.py`, `teams.py`, `hodge.py` and the published matrix exist. To be written:

- `--policy damage|switch` and `--turn-limit 300` in `teams_showdown.py play`, with `matchup` and `should_switch` as small functions, poke-env's MIT notice in the module, and tests on hand-made states (a −2.5 matchup with a positive bench option switches; a −2.5 matchup with no positive bench option does not; a −3 Defense stage switches).
- `python -m pokemon.p2 --matrices <files> --out pokemon/results/p2.json`: exploitability, the bootstrap, the new equilibrium and the secondary numbers.

## Outputs

- In git: `pokemon/results/p2.json` and the two matrices as `teams-battles-p2-*.json` (counts per pair, as the published one), and a paragraph in `team-study.md` and the README's full-battle row.
- No replay data of any kind is used or committed.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- The pilot must show no choice errors and full agreement of the damage checks, as the published run did. If either fails, the harness is fixed and the pilot rerun before any matrix.
- If more than 5% of the pilot's S battles reach the turn limit, the switch rule gets the guard "never switch twice in a row", by an amendment before the matrices.
- **A null result.** If H-survive's interval holds 0, the README says that the full-battle equilibrium was not measurably easier to exploit when both sides switch by poke-env's rule, with the interval and the detectable effect of 0.10, and still names the policy in the claim.

## What is not used, and why

- **Metamon's parsed replays** (`jakegrigsby/metamon-parsed-replays`, tagged cc-by-nc-4.0, read 2026-11-20) are not used. The Metamon policy checkpoints (`jakegrigsby/metamon`, tagged apache-2.0) are trained on that dataset; to keep any non-commercial data out of the work, they are not used either.
- **Learned policies** are out of scope: a policy trained here would need its own plan under METHOD.md.

## Threats to validity

- **One heuristic.** A stronger policy than D is not the strongest policy. The strength check says whether S is even stronger.
- **Damage-only sets.** Setup, recovery, hazards, items and Terastallization, which the README lists as missing, are still missing; switching matters more with them.
- **The same 63 teams.** A team outside them could exploit either mixture.
- **Symmetric policies.** Both sides use the same policy; a team game where policies differ by side is another question.
