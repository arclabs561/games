# C9. Codenames: a small language model trained to guess as people do

## Questions

1. Does Qwen3.5, fine-tuned on C1's human training turns and then trained by reinforcement learning with the person's pick as a verifiable reward, pick the person's word more often than gpt-oss-120b untrained?
2. Does the reinforcement-learning stage add to the fine-tuning stage?

## Why it matters

gpt-oss-120b matches the person on 65.0% of C1's 551 test turns (C4). It is a 120B model behind an API, and every call costs money and drifts with the provider. A small model that matched it could run inside every job, as a scoring guesser for C6 and C7 and as a partner. C2 and C8 ask the same question of embedding models, which rank words by one similarity. A language model reads the clue and the board together, so it can weigh the other words on the board, which a similarity score cannot.

- If the trained model passes gpt-oss-120b, the folder gains a cheap human model, and C6's and C7's clues are scored by it in an amendment to their reports.
- If fine-tuning alone gets as far as fine-tuning plus RL, the second stage is not worth its cost for this task, and the README says so. The comparison is one of the questions the owner asked: does a newer method help on a small labeled set?
- If neither stage passes the LoRA embedding model of C2 (0.626), a 2B to 4B language model is not a better human model than a 0.6B encoder at this data size.

## Model and methods, as of 2026-11-19

- **Model.** Qwen3.5-2B or Qwen3.5-4B (created 2026-02-27 and 2026-02-28, Apache 2.0), instruction-tuned, thinking off. Which of the two is fixed by a rule on validation turns before any training (below). The reasons for Qwen3.5 over other small models are in C7. Gemma 4 E2B or E4B is the fallback, used only if Qwen3.5 fails to train in the pilot.
- **Stage 1, supervised fine-tuning.** `trl.SFTTrainer` (TRL 1.18.1) with LoRA (rank 32, α 64, every linear projection of the language model). The target is the person's word alone; the loss covers the answer only.
- **Stage 2, RL with a verifiable reward.** `trl.GRPOTrainer` from the stage 1 adapter, with C7's settings: `loss_type="dapo"`, `epsilon_high=0.28`, no KL term, rewards scaled within each group, 8 samples per prompt. The reward is 1 if the sampled word is the person's and 0 otherwise. A reply that names no board word scores 0.
- **Stage 2 with the teacher's label (secondary arm).** The same, but the reward is 1 if the sampled word is gpt-oss-120b's first pick for that turn. This is the black-box, outcome-based form of on-policy distillation in the taxonomy of Song and Zheng (2026): the student samples, and a teacher judges its own sample. The teacher's picks come from C8's labeling run, so this arm costs no new calls.
- **Considered and not chosen.** Agarwal et al. (2024) and TRL's `DistillationConfig` grade each token of the student's sample with the teacher's probabilities. That form of distillation needs the teacher's probabilities on the student's samples, and Inference Providers do not return them. Serving gpt-oss-120b in the job would need an 80 GB GPU for the whole run, several times this plan's cost.

## Design

- **Data.** C1's game split: 4,820 train turns, 717 val turns, 551 test turns in 68 games. The prompt is `llm.guesser_prompt` with count 1, as in C4, and the guess is the first board word of the reply.
- **Choice of model size** (before any training): untrained Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B are scored on the 717 val turns. The 4B is used only if its val top-1 is at least 0.03 above the 2B's; otherwise the 2B is used. The gain must pay for about twice the GPU time.
- **Arms**, each with three seeds:
  1. *SFT:* stage 1, two epochs.
  2. *SFT + RL:* arm 1's adapter of the same seed, then stage 2 for one pass over the train turns (300 steps of 16 turns).
  3. *SFT + RL with the teacher's label* (secondary): as arm 2 with the teacher's reward.
- **Learning rate** for stage 1: one choice among 5e-5, 1e-4 and 2e-4, by val log-likelihood of the person's word at seed 1. Stage 2 uses 2e-5, fixed now, as in C7.
- **Scoring.** Greedy decoding. For log-likelihood, each of the board's words is scored as the reply, and the person's word gets its share of the 25 (or fewer) probabilities. This gives a number comparable with C1's and C2's Luce guessers.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05:

- **H-teacher.** Top-1 on C1's test turns, arm 2 minus gpt-oss-120b (C4's per-turn hits), paired by turn.
- **H-RL.** Top-1, arm 2 minus arm 1, paired by turn and seed.

Each arm's per-turn hit is averaged over its three seeds. 95% intervals and p-values come from C1's bootstrap over test games (10,000 resamples, seed 0).

## Secondary metrics

- Arm 2 minus C2's LoRA encoder and minus C1's potion map; arm 3 minus arm 2.
- Log-likelihood on test for every arm; top-1 on val after each stage.
- Each seed's top-1, and the range.
- The share of replies that name no board word, before and after each stage.
- **As a guesser for C6's clues** (exploratory): expected value of C6's picks and first choices under the trained model's word probabilities, played out as a Luce-like guesser.

## Sample size

C1's test set resolves a paired difference of about 4 points between two strong guessers. To pass gpt-oss-120b by a resolved margin, the trained model needs about 0.69 or more. That is 6 points above the best trained guesser so far, and the plan expects H-teacher to fail more likely than not. H-RL can resolve a gain of 3 to 4 points, since the two arms share a seed's errors.

## Seeds

Stage 1 seeds 1, 2 and 3; stage 2 starts from the adapter of the same seed. Seed 0 is the pilot's. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Jobs through `hfrun.py`: `l4x1` ($0.80 an hour) for the 2B model, `l40sx1` ($1.80 an hour, 48 GB) for the 4B.

| Job | Timeout | 2B on L4, expected hours | 4B on L40S, expected hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Size choice and learning rate: two models on 717 val turns, then 3 SFT runs scored on val | 1 h | 0.8 | 0.8 |
| Arms 1 and 2, one job per seed: SFT, test scoring, RL of 300 steps, test scoring | 1.75 h each | 3 × 1.4 | 3 × 1.3 |
| Arm 3, one job per seed, from the arm 1 adapter | 1.5 h each | 3 × 1.2 | not run |
| Total | | 8.6 h, $6.90 | 4.7 h, $8.50 |

An RL step generates 128 replies of at most 8 tokens from prompts of about 200 tokens, as in C7, so 300 steps take 1 to 1.5 hours on an L4. The teacher's labels come from C8 at no new cost. With the 4B model, arm 3 is dropped to keep the cap; the README then says that the teacher's reward was tried with neither size.

Expected $7 to $8.50. Hard cap $12. The timeouts above add up to 10.75 hours ($8.60) on the L4 and 6.25 hours ($11.25) on the L40S, so the cap holds even if every job runs to its timeout. A rerun after a failure needs an amendment. No Docker image. This plan runs after C8 and needs approval before its jobs.

## Stopping rules and a null result

Val turns choose the size and the learning rate, and the test turns are scored once per arm. If stage 1 does not raise val top-1 above the untrained model's, stage 2 does not start. If H-teacher fails, the README gives the difference and states that the trained small model did not pass the hosted one. If H-RL fails, it states that RL added nothing measurable to fine-tuning.

## Threats to validity

- **Contamination**, as in C4 and C8. Qwen3.5 and gpt-oss-120b may both have seen Cultural Codes.
- **Few labels.** 4,820 turns is a small set for a 2B model. Two epochs of SFT may overfit; the val check after stage 1 catches a collapse but not a mild one.
- **The reward is the label.** RL with a binary reward for the person's pick sharpens the model toward its mode. A higher top-1 can come with a worse log-likelihood, and the report gives both.
