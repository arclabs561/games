# C14. Codenames: C6 again, with reply caps that cut off fewer than 5% of replies

## Question

When a hosted language model chooses one action from the 120 legal (clue, count) pairs that the embedding giver proposes, and its replies are no longer cut off or empty, does its choice beat the embedding giver's first choice with guessers it never met?

## Why it matters

C6 found that no hosted model chose better than the first choice: the selected model, Qwen3.8-27B, trailed by 0.06 (−0.12 to −0.005) with embedding strangers. But 84 of its 200 test replies were cut off at the 64-token cap and 30 more named no listed clue, so its action was the fallback (the first choice itself) on 58% of boards; and gpt-oss-120b returned empty replies on 40 of 200 test boards and none on validation (`codenames/results/c6.md`, `llm-providers.md`). The README now reports C6 as decided by the cap. METHOD.md's amendment of 2026-11-20 says that above 5% cut off, a plan raises the cap before the confirmatory run. This plan runs C6's question under that rule.

- If a model's choice beats the first choice once it answers on nearly every board, C6's null was the harness.
- If it still does not, the null holds with replies that are complete.

## Design

As C6, with these changes, each fixed now:

- **Choosers:** Qwen3.8-27B (thinking off) and gpt-oss-120b (reasoning effort low), the two that C6's harness most constrained, both pinned to DeepInfra with the router's suffix. No model selection: both are primary.
- **Reply caps by rule.** On the validation boards, Qwen3.8-27B starts at 256 tokens; if more than 5% of its replies reach the cap, the test uses 1,024. gpt-oss-120b starts at 1,024 and moves to 2,048 by the same rule. An empty reply is sent once more at the end of its job; if more than 5% of a model's validation replies are still empty after that, the plan stops and an amendment comes before any test call.
- **Candidates from strictly legal clues.** The 30-clue shortlist comes from the clue words that `legality.strict_legal` allows on the board, as in C11's amendment, since C6's first choice was a form of a board word on 10.8% of boards. The first choice F is recomputed on that list.
- **List order** as in C6 (the shortlist's order). The report gives the share of picks at each list position, since C7's trained model learned to name the first item.
- **Boards:** test 300 of seed 14004, validation 50 of seed 14005, pilot 10 of seed 14999. No earlier plan uses seeds from 14000 to 14999.
- The prompt, the reading of replies and the fallback (F) are C6's.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, one per chooser, with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05: the chooser's action minus F, mean exact turn value over the seven embedding strangers, paired by test board. 95% bootstrap interval over boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0). Read in both directions. Equivalence margin δ = 0.05: an interval inside −0.05 to 0.05 is reported as equivalent within 0.05; otherwise a null is "no detectable difference" with its interval.

## Secondary metrics

- The oracle (best of the 120 for the strangers) minus F, and the share of the gap each chooser closes.
- Per chooser and split: replies cut off, empty, naming no listed clue, and fallbacks; median latency; repeat check of 20 prompts at the start of validation and of test.
- The conditional difference on boards where the chooser named a listed action.
- Played-out values under Llama 3.3 (Novita) and DeepSeek-V3.2 (DeepInfra) as language-model guessers on each chooser's pick and F, one reply each.
- Pick position in the list, and the share of picks with count 1.

## Minimum detectable effect

C6's selected-model interval (−0.12 to −0.005 on 200 boards) gives a standard deviation near 0.41 for the paired difference, with the fallback on 58% of boards contributing 0. With complete replies the difference is nonzero on more boards; the plan assumes 0.6. With 300 boards, 3.08 × 0.6 / √300 ≈ 0.11 at Holm's first level. The effect worth finding is 0.1, about a quarter of the oracle's 0.444 lead in C6. Powered for about that.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers from `cpu-basic` jobs through `experiments/hfrun.py`. C6's test calls cost about $0.00007 each at a 64-token cap; with the larger caps the plan assumes $0.0002 for both choosers.

| Part | Calls | Cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Choosers: 360 boards × 2 models, with resends | about 760 | $0.15 |
| Repeat checks, two splits × 2 models × 40 sends | 160 | $0.03 |
| Llama 3.3 and DeepSeek-V3.2 guessing each pick and F, 300 boards × about 2.5 distinct actions | about 1,500 | $0.09 |

**Expected about $0.30. Hard cap $0.55.** Job caps: pilot $0.02, validation $0.08, test choosers $0.25, test guessers $0.15, each with a 4-hour timeout or less; they add up to $0.50.

## Entry points

`rerank.py`, `actions.py`, `legality.py` and `hosted.py` exist. To be written: `--max-tokens` and `--strict` for `rerank choose`, the cap rule in the validation job's summary, and `python -m codenames.c14 score --out codenames/results/c14.json`. Tests: every candidate passes `strict_legal`; the fallback is F; a cut-off reply is counted as such and not parsed as a pick.

## Outputs

In git: `codenames/results/c14.json`, `c14-picks.txt`, `c14-runs.json`, and a paragraph in the README's chooser section beside C6. In the private dataset, under `runs/c14/`: every prompt and reply.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The test is sent once; a failed job is rerun once from the start. A null is stated with the reply counts that show the harness no longer decided it.

## Threats to validity

Embedding strangers share F's family; the language-model guessers are a secondary check and also a proxy. The strict candidate list changes the candidates as well as the cap, so C14 is compared with C6 as a rerun of the question, not of every number.
