# C12. Codenames: language-model givers with the whole clue rule, the costs, a retry and more reasoning

## Question

In C4, hosted language models gave clues worth 0.29 to 0.89 words per turn less than the embedding giver to guessers they had never met. Their prompt did not state the whole clue rule or what a wrong pick costs, an illegal clue got no second try, and reasoning was off or low. With a prompt that states the rulebook's clue rule and this folder's exact scoring, one retry after an illegal clue, and medium reasoning, does gpt-oss-120b still give clues worth less than the embedding giver's (a) to embedding guessers it never met, and (b) to language-model guessers of other families?

## Why it matters

The README says that language models as givers lose 0.29 to 0.89 words per turn. A methods review of the folder calls that claim too broad: the embedding giver maximizes the exact expected value under a guesser of the same family as every scorer, while the language models got a short prompt, no rule statement, no retry and little reasoning, and about half of two models' clues fell back to a default. The README's open questions ask the same thing.

Stephenson, Sidji and Ronval (arXiv:2412.11373, v2 of 2025-04-21) report the opposite direction: LLM agents "are more generalisable to a wider range of teammates than prior techniques". Read closely (arXiv HTML, read 2026-11-20), their setting differs from C4's in four ways:

- Their codemaster prompt states that a clue must "Not derive, or be derived from, one of the words on the board", and a clue that breaks the format or that rule is sent back for another answer.
- Their cross-play pairs are LLM with LLM and word-vector bot with word-vector bot. They say they could not produce reliable results with an LLM and a word-vector agent on one team, so they report no LLM giver with an embedding guesser, the pairing of C4's primary test.
- Their score is the number of turns to finish a single-team game, 25 for a loss, over 100 seeds, not the value of one turn.
- Their word-vector bots are Word2Vec and GloVe with fixed thresholds, not a calibrated expected-value giver.

So the two results need not conflict: C4 measured LLM givers with embedding strangers, and they measured LLM givers with LLM partners against word-vector pairs. This plan measures both on the same boards, with a harness like theirs.

- If the full harness closes the gap with embedding strangers, C4's result was the harness, and the README's claim is withdrawn.
- If the gap stays with embedding strangers but the LLM giver leads with LLM guessers, the two findings are about different partners, and the README says that.
- If the LLM giver trails under both kinds of guesser, the claim holds for this model with a fair harness, and the README scopes it to gpt-oss-120b.

## Arms

All arms give one clue per board. Everything not named is as in C4.

- **E, the embedding giver.** EmbeddingGemma 2's expected-value giver (`players.expected_giver`) over the clue list of `words.clue_words()`, now restricted to clues that `legality.strict_legal` allows, with counts as in C4. The strict filter applies to every arm, so no arm can name a form of a board word.
- **A0, C4's harness.** gpt-oss-120b with `llm.giver_prompt`, reasoning effort low, a 1,024-token cap, no retry (C4 and its amendment).
- **A2, the rule, the costs and a retry.** A new prompt (`llm.giver_prompt_full`, fixed before the pilot and printed in the report) that states:
  - the clue is one English word, not a board word, not a form of one (plural, tense, derived word, spelling variant), not part of a compound board word or containing one, as the rulebook says ("You can't say any form of a visible word on the table"; "You can't say part of a compound word on the table", English rules of July 2015);
  - the guesser picks up to the number, one at a time, and the turn ends at the first word that is not the team's;
  - the turn's value: +1 per team word found, −1 for an opponent word, 0 for a neutral word, and minus every team word still unfound for the assassin;
  - that a guesser here never picks more than 4 words, so a number above 4 is scored as 4;
  - the reply format, `CLUE NUMBER`.
  If the clue fails `strict_legal`, one retry message names the board word it relates to and the relation (from `legality.flags`) and asks for another clue. Reasoning effort low, cap 1,024.
- **A3, the full harness (primary).** A2 with reasoning effort medium and a 4,096-token cap.
- A reply with no strictly legal clue after its tries gets the fallback: E's best strictly legal clue for one word, as C4's fallback but strict. Its rate is reported per arm.

A2 and A0 differ in the prompt and the retry, and A3 and A2 in reasoning, so the steps are reported as secondary decompositions. The primary compares A3 with E. gpt-oss-120b is fixed now, not chosen on validation: in C4 it named a legal clue on 98% of boards, the only model of the four to do so, and it is the only one with a reasoning setting on its pinned provider.

**Part B, optional, with its own cap.** DeepSeek-V3.2, C4's selected giver, in arms A0 and A2 (it has no reasoning setting in its default mode), on the same boards. It is secondary. It runs only if approved with its own cap.

## Guessers

- **Embedding strangers:** the seven calibrated Luce guessers other than EmbeddingGemma 2, as in C4. Exact expected values, no sampling noise.
- **Language-model guessers of other families,** one reply per (board, clue, count), played out by `c4.play_turn` as in C11: Llama 3.3 70B Instruct pinned to novita (replies capped at 256 tokens) and DeepSeek-V3.2 pinned to deepinfra (cap 256), both at temperature 0 and seed 0, with C4's guesser prompt. Neither shares a family with gpt-oss-120b or with the embedding models.
- **The giver's own model as guesser** (secondary): gpt-oss-120b guessing on its own clues, reasoning low, cap 1,024. Needed for the retention ratio below, the measure closest to Stephenson et al.'s.

## Boards

Test: 500 boards of seed 12004. Validation: 50 boards of seed 12005, used for the prompt check, the repeat check and the cap rule, and never scored. Pilot: 10 boards of seed 12999. No earlier plan uses a seed from 12000 to 12999.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05, both paired by test board:

- **H-strangers.** Mean exact turn value over the seven embedding strangers, A3 minus E.
- **H-models.** Mean played-out turn value over the two language-model guessers, A3 minus E. A board enters only if both guessers answered both prompts; dropped boards are counted.

95% intervals by bootstrap over boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0, `c4score.bootstrap`). The p-value for Holm is twice the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0. Each hypothesis is read in both directions: a difference with an interval above 0 says the full-harness giver is better with that kind of guesser, below 0 that it is worse, and an interval holding 0 is unresolved, reported with its detectable effect.

## Secondary metrics

- A0 − E under both kinds of guesser, on these boards, beside C4's −0.508 for gpt-oss-120b (C4's boards and filter differ).
- The decomposition A2 − A0 and A3 − A2, under both kinds of guesser.
- **Retention**, Stephenson et al.'s question in this folder's terms: for A3, the value under the two language-model strangers divided by the value under gpt-oss-120b as its own guesser; for E, the value under the seven embedding strangers divided by the value under EmbeddingGemma 2's own guesser (exact). Ratios of means, with bootstrap intervals over boards. If A3 keeps a larger share of its own-partner value than E does, that matches their finding, whatever H-strangers says.
- Per arm: the share of first replies that fail `strict_legal`, the share fixed by the retry, the fallback rate, the mean count, words found, the assassin rate, and replies cut off at the cap.
- Part B, if run: DeepSeek-V3.2's A0 and A2 under both kinds of guesser.
- Values of failed replies set to 0 instead of the fallback, as a sensitivity check.

## Minimum detectable effect

From C4's H-give interval (−0.460 to −0.166 on 200 boards), the paired difference has a standard deviation near 1.06. With legal clues on most boards, more of each board's difference comes from the model's own clue, so the plan uses 1.2. With n = 500, 2.80 × 1.2 / √500 = 0.150, and 3.08 × 1.2 / √500 = 0.165 at Holm's first level. For H-models, C11 measured 1.10 for the mean of two models' played-out differences between two giver arms; with arms that differ more, the plan uses 1.5: 3.08 × 1.5 / √500 = 0.207. The effect worth finding is half of C4's selected-model gap, 0.15. H-strangers is powered for about that; H-models only for effects near 0.2, and the report says so. Both are recomputed from the test's standard deviations.

## Pinned providers, caps and checks

Per METHOD.md's amendment of 2026-11-20: each call names its provider with the router's suffix (`openai/gpt-oss-120b:deepinfra`, `meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct:novita`, `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2:deepinfra`), the serving provider, model id and response id of each reply are logged, a reply from another provider is sent again and counted, and each job saves `quantizations.json` and `served.json`.

- **Cap rule.** If more than 5% of A3's validation replies reach 4,096 tokens, the test uses 8,192, by this rule and without a further amendment. If more than 5% of A0's or A2's replies reach 1,024, the same rule raises them to 2,048. Cut-off replies are counted and reported per arm and split.
- **Repeat check:** 20 giver prompts per arm and 20 guesser prompts per guesser, sent twice more at the start of validation and again at the start of test. Reported: the share whose clue (or played-out value) changed, per split, with empty replies, cut-off replies and median latency. If these differ between validation and test, the test result is also reported on the boards with no such reply, as exploratory.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- The pilot checks parsing, the retry path, the providers and the cost per call. Nothing from it is scored.
- The test is sent once. A job that fails is rerun once from the start, and only the rerun is scored; the calls the failed job sent are recorded.
- **A null result.** If H-strangers's interval holds 0, the README says that with the whole rule, the costs, a retry and medium reasoning, gpt-oss-120b's clues were not distinguishable from the embedding giver's for embedding strangers, with the difference, interval and detectable effect, and it withdraws "lose 0.29 to 0.89" as a general claim. If both intervals lie below 0, it keeps the claim, scoped to this model and harness, and says that Stephenson et al. measured other partners.

## Threats to validity

- **The embedding strangers share E's family.** The language-model guessers are there for this reason, but they are models too: per METHOD.md, a result with model partners is a proxy until checked with people.
- **One sample per reply** at temperature 0 from a nondeterministic provider. The repeat check measures how much it moves.
- **The prompt is fixed before the pilot and not tuned.** A better prompt may exist. This tests a fair harness, not the best one.
- **Contamination.** Codenames strategy text is likely in the models' training data. That favors neither arm in a known direction.
