# R1. Chess: does Maia-3 help predict a Lichess puzzle's rating?

## Question

A Lichess puzzle's rating is the Glicko-2 rating at which solvers succeed about half the time. Maia-3 gives, for any position and rating, the chance that a player at that rating plays each move. Does adding Maia-3's chance of finding each solution move, across ratings, to a feature model lower the error of a predicted puzzle rating on held-out puzzles? And how well does Maia-3 do with no fitting at all, by reading off the rating at which it solves the puzzle half the time?

## Why it matters

`chess_ratings/` uses Maia-3 to rate players from their moves. A puzzle is the reverse problem: the moves are fixed and the rating is unknown. Two published competitions asked this question.

- The IEEE BigData 2024 Cup (KnowledgePit) scored predictions of Lichess puzzle ratings by mean squared error, with a preliminary score on part of the test set and a final score on the rest. The winning entry (Woodruff, Filatov and Cognetta, 2024) used truncated Maia and Leela networks as board embedders. One participant paper (arXiv:2410.11078) reports a preliminary MSE of 75,995 and a final MSE of 158,292, so the final test set behaved differently from the preliminary one.
- In the 2025 follow-up at FedCSIS, Björkqvist fine-tuned Maia-2 end to end with hand-made and engine features and placed first, with a final MSE of 52,311. His paper notes that the test puzzles had fewer solving attempts than the training puzzles.

Both used Maia-1 or Maia-2 as a learned embedder. Neither used Maia-3, and neither scored the simplest reading of a human-move model: the rating at which the model's player would solve the puzzle half the time.

- If Maia-3's features lower the error, a model of human moves carries information about puzzle difficulty beyond the board's surface features, and the folder can say how much.
- If they do not, the result says that a move model trained on games does not transfer to puzzles at this resolution, with the detectable effect.
- The zero-fit reading is a result either way: how far a model of games is from puzzle ratings without any puzzle data.

**What could not be checked.** The scouting notes for this plan said the 2024 Cup used 3,888,765 puzzles, that Björkqvist placed third with Maia-1 features and LightGBM, and that the best final MSE was 104,541. The papers read for this plan do not show these numbers. The one Björkqvist paper found is the 2025 paper above (Maia-2, first place). The report will cite only what was read.

## Data

- **Puzzles.** The Lichess puzzle database, as the Hugging Face dataset `Lichess/chess-puzzles` (6,157,341 rows; card updated 2026-10-07; tagged cc0-1.0). database.lichess.org says that its exports are released under CC0 and lists 6,193,530 puzzles with the fields PuzzleId, FEN, Moves, Rating, RatingDeviation, Popularity, NbPlays, Themes, GameUrl, OpeningTags (both read 2026-11-20). The snapshot used is the dataset revision at the pilot's start, recorded by its commit hash.
- **The competitions' own files are not used.** KnowledgePit's rules forbid passing the competition data to anyone outside the competition. Their numbers are quoted from the papers, as context, and are not paired with these.
- **Which puzzles qualify.** NbPlays at least 500 and RatingDeviation at most 100, so that the target is settled. The counts that qualify are reported. If fewer than 135,000 qualify, the split sizes below shrink in proportion, by an amendment written before any Maia-3 query.
- **Split.** Puzzles are ordered by md5(`chess-puzzles-r1-2026-11-20` || PuzzleId). The first 100,000 qualifying puzzles train, the next 10,000 validate, the next 20,000 are the test set. A second test set, *few plays*, takes the first 5,000 puzzles in the same order with NbPlays from 50 to 200 and no condition on RD. It imitates the shift that Björkqvist describes. Nothing in it is used for fitting.
- **Inputs a model may use.** The FEN and the Moves field only. Themes, Popularity, NbPlays, OpeningTags and RatingDeviation are left out of every model: they are produced by solvers and would leak the answer (a theme such as `mateIn1` is assigned from the solution and from the solvers' results).

## Predictors

The first move in Moves is the opponent's move that sets the puzzle; the solver's moves are the 2nd, 4th, and so on.

- **The Maia-3 curve.** For each rating R on the grid 400, 500, …, 3000 (27 points, as in `results/calibration-plan.md`), and each solver move k, p_k(R) is Maia3-5M's probability of the solution move with both players given R. History is the puzzle's own moves only (the FEN carries no earlier positions), as the authors' UCI engine does by default. The chance to solve is P(R) = ∏_k p_k(R), over at most the first 8 solver moves. Lichess also accepts any mating move on the last move of a mate puzzle; this curve counts only the listed move, and the report gives the share of test puzzles where this applies.
- **Z, zero fit.** R½, the rating where P(R) crosses 1/2, by linear interpolation of log(P / (1 − P)) between grid points; 400 if P(400) > 1/2, 3000 if P(3000) < 1/2.
- **Z+, one-dimensional fit.** Isotonic regression of the puzzle rating on R½, fitted on the training puzzles.
- **B, board baseline.** LightGBM on features of the board and the solution without Maia-3: number of solver moves; pieces of each kind for each side; material balance; side to move; whether each of the first three solver moves is a capture, a check, a promotion or castling; legal moves in the puzzle position; whether the puzzle ends in mate.
- **B+M.** B's features plus log P(R) at the 27 grid points, log p_k(1500) for the first three solver moves (missing when absent), and R½.

LightGBM settings: learning rate 0.05, num_leaves 31 or 127 (chosen on the validation set by MSE), at most 5,000 trees with early stopping after 100 rounds on the validation set, seed 0. B and B+M are tuned the same way.

## Primary metric and test

One primary hypothesis:

- **H-M.** Test-set MSE of B+M minus MSE of B, paired by puzzle: the mean of (error of B+M)² − (error of B)². 95% interval by bootstrap over test puzzles (10,000 resamples, seed 0). Maia-3 is said to add predictive value if the interval lies below 0, and the report states the relative change in MSE.

## Secondary metrics

Reported with intervals and without significance claims:

- MSE, RMSE, MAE and Spearman's ρ of Z, Z+, B and B+M on the test set and on the few-plays set.
- The mean guess (the training mean rating) as a floor.
- Bias of Z by band of the puzzle rating (400 to 1000, …, 2600 and up). The player-rating study found Maia-3 rating weak players too high; whether the same shift appears in puzzles is one thing this shows.
- The share of test puzzles whose curve never crosses 1/2 on the grid.
- Error by number of solver moves (1, 2, 3, 4 or more).
- For context only, without pairing: the competition MSEs above, labeled as from other test sets.

## Minimum detectable effect

The standard deviation of the per-puzzle difference in squared error is not known before the pilot. As a planning value, if B's RMSE is near 450 points (MSE near 200,000), the squared errors of two models that share most of their errors differ with a standard deviation of roughly 250,000. With n = 20,000 test puzzles, 2.80 × 250,000 / √20,000 ≈ 4,950, about 2.5% of B's MSE. The effect worth finding is a 5% reduction. The pilot measures the standard deviation on the validation set, and if the detectable effect is above 5% of B's validation MSE, an amendment adds test puzzles from the same ordered list before any test puzzle is scored.

## Seeds

Split salt `chess-puzzles-r1-2026-11-20`. LightGBM seed 0. Bootstrap seed 0. Maia-3 is deterministic given the checkpoint (`checkpoint_sha256` is logged); the job records the device.

## Compute and cost

Maia3-5M on Hugging Face Jobs (`t4-small`, $0.40 an hour) or on GitHub Actions runners (free), through `experiments/hfrun.py` or `experiment.yml`.

- **Queries.** Each puzzle needs (solver moves) × 27 queries. Assuming 2.5 solver moves on average (to be measured in the pilot), 135,000 puzzles need about 9.1 million queries.
- **Speed.** In the calibration study, Maia3-5M on one T4 answered about 1,750 queries a second; cpu-upgrade fell from about 230 to 20 to 50 a second during the day (`chess_ratings/README.md`). On a T4, 9.1 million queries take about 5,200 seconds, 1.45 hours, about $0.58, plus setup.
- **Actions.** The pilot also measures one Actions runner's speed. If 20 runner jobs at that speed finish the queries in under 5 hours each, the main run goes to Actions at no cost; otherwise to one T4.
- **LightGBM** on 100,000 rows of about 50 features runs on a `cpu-basic` job or an Actions runner in minutes.

| Job | Hardware | Timeout | Expected cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Pilot: 500 validation puzzles, speed on T4 and on one Actions runner, solver-move count | T4 and Actions | 30 min | $0.10 |
| Curves for 135,000 puzzles | Actions (20 jobs) or T4 | 5 h a job, or 2 h | $0 or $0.70 |
| Features, LightGBM, scoring | `cpu-basic` or Actions | 1 h | $0.01 |

**Expected $0.10 to $0.85. Hard cap $1.00**, set by the timeouts: the T4 run stops at 2 hours ($0.80), and if it has not finished, the puzzles done form a complete prefix of each split in hash order, and an amendment comes before more is spent.

## Entry points

To be written:

## Outputs

- In git: `chess_ratings/results/puzzles.json` (every number quoted, the puzzle counts, the dataset revision, the hyperparameters chosen) and a README section. Per-puzzle rows are CC0 and may be committed, but only aggregates are, to keep the repository small.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/r1/`: the curves and the fitted models.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- The pilot checks that Maia-3's probabilities match the authors' UCI engine on three puzzle positions, as the folder already does for games, and that the solver-move count fits the cost. Nothing from the pilot is scored.
- The test sets are scored once, after the models are fitted and frozen.
- **A null result.** If H-M's interval holds 0, the README states that Maia-3's solution-move probabilities did not lower the error of a feature model on held-out Lichess puzzles, gives the difference with its interval and the detectable effect, and gives Z's error as the zero-fit reading.

## Threats to validity

- **Puzzle raters are not blitz players.** A puzzle rating comes from solvers' puzzle ratings, which are on their own scale. Maia-3 conditions on game ratings. Z+ and B+M can learn the map; Z cannot.
- **Position overlap.** Puzzles come from Lichess games, some from Maia-3's training months (January 2023 to July 2025). The model saw the games' moves, not the puzzle solutions; where a solution was also the move played in the game, Maia-3 may have seen it.
- **One snapshot.** Ratings of puzzles keep moving. The NbPlays rule limits this, and the few-plays set measures the shift.
- **Not the competitions' test sets.** The comparison with published MSEs is context, not a ranking.
