# C10. Codenames: a hosted language model as the listener inside an embedding giver

## Question

An embedding giver proposes legal clues, and a hosted language model guesses on each of them. The giver then gives the clue that is worth the most under the language model's guesses. Do guessers that the giver never met find more of its words than they find of the embedding giver's own first choice? And does the language model add value when its guesses are averaged with a population of embedding guessers?

## Why it matters

The language models in this folder are better at guessing than at giving.

- In C4, gpt-oss-120b matched the person's word on 0.650 of C1's test turns, against 0.548 for EmbeddingGemma 2 (difference 0.102, interval 0.071 to 0.135). As a giver it lost to the EmbeddingGemma 2 giver by 0.508 words per turn with the strangers (`codenames/README.md`, "Language models").
- In C6, six models chose among the 120 legal actions that EmbeddingGemma 2 proposes. None beat the proposer's own first choice. With the strangers, the selected model, Qwen3.8-27B, trailed it by 0.059 (−0.116 to −0.005). gpt-oss-120b trailed it by 0.047 (−0.119 to 0.025). The best action in each list is worth 0.444 (0.387 to 0.504) more than the first choice, so the lists hold better clues than the first choice.

Sieker and Zarrieß (2026) report the same split for pragmatics in general: many models do much better as pragmatic listeners than as pragmatic speakers. White, Pandey and Pan (2024) build a Codenames Duet giver on the rational speech act model, in which a pragmatic speaker picks the clue that a literal listener is most likely to read as meant. Their literal listeners are word-embedding models fitted to groups of players, and a prompted language model is one of the guessers they test against. C10 puts the language model in the listener's place and keeps the embedding model as the proposer. C3 found that a giver trained against a population of six guessers did better with a seventh it never met, by 0.31 (0.26 to 0.36) words per turn. That is the reason to also average the language model with a population.

- If the language-model listener beats the first choice, a language model's skill at guessing can be turned into better clues without asking it to speak. A cheap listener then becomes the target: C8, which distills gpt-oss-120b's guesses into an embedding model, would give one at no cost per call.
- If it does not, the language model's advantage in guessing does not help these partners, even when the model is asked only to guess. C6 and C10 together would then say that the first choice is hard to improve on with hosted models alone.
- If the listener helps only when it is averaged with the population, it is useful as one more guesser of another kind, and the population is the main source of the gain.

## Design

- **Boards.** 300 test boards from board seed 9004 and 100 validation boards from seed 9005, dealt by `game.py` from `words.txt`, as `c4.boards` does. Five boards of seed 9999 serve the pilot and are never scored. No earlier plan uses a seed from 9000 to 9999. C6's test boards (seed 6004) are not used again.
- **Candidates.** C6's action list, from `codenames/actions.py` unchanged: 30 legal clues on each board, proposed by EmbeddingGemma 2, each with counts 1 to 4. A test checks that every clue is legal and has a vector in all eight spaces.
- **Prefilter.** A clue's proposer value is the highest of its four actions' values under the EmbeddingGemma 2 Luce guesser. The K clues with the highest proposer values are kept, with ties broken by the order of `actions.py`. Their 4K actions are the candidates of every arm but one (C6's chooser, below). The first choice is always among them. The ordered lists of the top 20 clues are computed here and committed, because the job has no word vectors.
- **The listener.** gpt-oss-120b through deepinfra, reasoning effort low, replies capped at 1,024 tokens, temperature 0, seed 0: C4's model and settings. It was chosen in advance, as the best guesser of C4's test. It hears C4's guesser prompt, `llm.guesser_prompt(clue, 1, words)`, with count 1 as in C4, once for each kept clue. Its reply is read with `llm.parse_ranking`.
- **The tail rule.** In the C4 replies read while writing this plan, gpt-oss-120b lists a few words in an order of its own and then the rest of the board in the prompt's order. That tail repeats the prompt, and the plan treats it as saying nothing about the clue. How often it occurs is reported. The *informative prefix* of a reply is the shortest start of its ranking after which the remaining words follow the prompt's order. Words in the prefix get ranks 0, 1, 2 and so on, and every word after it gets the same rank, the length of the prefix. A reply that names no board word gives every word the same rank. A test checks the rule on replies made by hand.
- **The listener's guesser.** A Luce guesser with weight exp(−rank / T) on each unrevealed word, scored exactly by `luce.turn_odds` with the costs of `players.costs_for`. Its value for an action (clue, count) is the expected turn value, so the listener values all four counts of a clue from one reply. The temperature T is fitted once by maximum likelihood of the person's word on C1's 717 validation turns. The data are C4's stored gpt-oss-120b replies on those turns (`runs/c4/guess-val-gpt-oss-120b/` in the private dataset), read with the same tail rule. The fit searches 400 values of T spaced evenly in log T from 0.05 to 20. It needs no new calls and uses no board of this plan. C1's test turns are not used.
- **The embedding population, as in C3.** For each of the seven strangers in turn (the held-out guesser s), the population is the other six. The population value of an action is the mean of their exact expected values. Each held-out choice is a *task*, as in C3, and every task costs no model calls.
- **Arms**, on the same boards:
  - *F, first choice:* the action with the highest value under EmbeddingGemma 2, as in C6.
  - *L, listener:* the action among the 4K with the highest value under the listener's guesser.
  - *P, population:* in task s, the action among the 4K with the highest population value.
  - *LP, listener and population:* in task s, the action among the 4K with the highest value of w times the listener's value plus (1 − w) times the population value. With w = 1/7 the listener counts as a seventh member of the population.
  - *C, C6's chooser:* Qwen3.8-27B with C6's prompt, settings and reading rule, over all 30 clues. A reply without an action gets the first choice, as in C6.
  - *S, the listener as speaker:* gpt-oss-120b with C6's prompt over the same K clues as L, with C6's settings and reading rule. A reply without an action gets the first choice. S and L differ only in the model's role.
  - *Bounds:* the best of the 4K actions for the strangers' mean (the oracle at K), and the mean over the 4K (uniform). Both are exact.
- **Ties.** An argmax takes the first action in the order of the prefilter, clue by clue, counts 1 to 4.
- **Scoring guessers**, by exact expected turn value as in C6:
  - the seven calibrated embedding guessers other than EmbeddingGemma 2 (the strangers);
  - the five potion maps of C1 (`train.py`, game split, seeds 1 to 5), rebuilt and checked as in C6 (the human-fitted guesser);
  - the EAT association guesser (secondary).
- **How an arm is scored.** For the four arms with one pick per board, the stranger value is the mean over the seven strangers of the pick's value. For P and LP, it is the mean over the seven tasks of the held-out stranger's value for that task's pick, as in C3. The human-fitted value of P and LP is scored on the pick of the task that holds out potion, so that the potion maps' base model is never in the population. That task's pick is also the one that the EAT guesser and the language-model guessers hear.

## Choices made on validation

The listener guesses on all 20 prefiltered clues of each validation board, which covers every K below. Two choices are made in this order and committed in `codenames/results/c10-val.json` before any test call:

1. **K**, from 10, 15 and 20: the K with the highest mean stranger value of L on the 100 validation boards. Ties go to the smaller K.
2. **w**, from 1/7, 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4, at that K: the w with the highest stranger value of LP. Ties go to the smaller w.

T is fixed before validation, by the fit above. Nothing else is chosen.

## Primary metrics and tests

Four primary hypotheses, in two pairs, tested together with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05. All four use the selected K and w and are paired by test board.

- **H-listen-strangers.** Stranger value, L minus F.
- **H-listen-human.** Human-fitted value, L minus F.
- **H-add-strangers.** Stranger value, LP minus P.
- **H-add-human.** Human-fitted value, LP minus P, on the task that holds out potion.

95% intervals by bootstrap over the 300 test boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0), from `c4score.bootstrap`. The p-value for Holm is the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0, doubled. A hypothesis holds if Holm rejects it and the difference is positive.

The first pair asks whether the listener alone improves on the embedding giver's choice. The second asks whether the listener adds to a population that already transfers to strangers, so it compares two arms that differ only in the listener's term.

## Secondary metrics

Reported with intervals and without significance claims:

- **Against the language models as choosers.** L minus C, and L minus S. The second is the listener and speaker comparison of Sieker and Zarrieß for this game: one model, the same candidates, two roles.
- **The whole giver.** LP minus F, P minus F and LP minus C.
- **Room.** The share of the gap to the oracle at K that L closes: (L − F) / (oracle − F), with an interval from the same resamples.
- **The EAT guesser**, on F, L, C, S, and the potion task's picks of P and LP.
- **Language-model guessers.** Llama 3.3 70B and DeepSeek-V3.2 guess once on each distinct (board, clue, count) that one of the six arms gives, with the pick's count, through `c4.crossguess`. Each turn is played out from the reply's order, as in C4 and C6. Both come from families other than the listener's. gpt-oss-120b is not a scoring guesser, because a model agrees with its own guesses by construction.
- **The listener's own value** of each arm's pick, the self-play side. It is reported as a check on the search and is not evidence.
- **Sensitivity to T.** L is recomputed with T halved and with T doubled, which needs no calls.
- **The listener's fit.** The fitted T is reported, with the log-likelihood per turn of the person's word on C1's validation turns. The same log-likelihood is given for EmbeddingGemma 2's Luce guesser on those turns. So is the mean length of the informative prefix, on those turns and on the test boards.
- **Validation curves.** L for each K, and LP for each w, on the validation boards.
- **Behavior**, for each arm:
  - the share of boards where the pick is the first choice;
  - the mean count;
  - the median rank of the pick under EmbeddingGemma 2;
  - the number of distinct clues.
- **Repeat check.** All 20 listener prompts of each of the first 10 validation boards, sent twice more. Reported: how often the informative prefix changed, and how often L's pick changed at K = 20.
- **Replies.** The number of listener replies that name no board word, and of chooser replies without an action, per arm.

## Sample size

In C6, the difference between a chooser and the first choice had a half-width of about 0.056 with the strangers and 0.108 with the human-fitted guesser, on 200 boards. With 300 boards, the same spread gives half-widths near 0.046 and 0.088. Then a difference of 0.05 words per turn is resolved with the strangers, and one of 0.09 with the human-fitted guesser. Smaller ones are reported as unresolved. LP and P share the population term, so their difference should vary less from board to board than L and F do. Holm over four tests asks the smallest p-value to be below 0.0125. On C6's boards the oracle was 0.444 above the first choice, so there is room for an effect of this size. The gap at each K is measured on the validation boards before the test, and the report states it.

## Seeds

Board seeds 9004 (test, 300 boards), 9005 (validation, 100) and 9999 (pilot, 5). Model calls use temperature 0 and seed 0. Bootstrap seed 0.

  - `python -m codenames.listen listen --seed S --boards A-B --k 20 --cap X --out OUT`, run in the job. It writes every reply and a file of rankings, one line per board and clue.
  - `python -m codenames.listen speak --seed 9004 --k K --cap X --out OUT` for S, run in the job, with `rerank.rerank_prompt` and `rerank.read_choice`.
  - `python -m codenames.listen select --runs DIR --out codenames/results/c10-val.json`, run here.
  - `python -m codenames.listen picks --runs DIR --out codenames/results/c10-picks-9004.txt`, run here, for the language-model guessers.
  - `python -m codenames.listen score --runs DIR --out codenames/results/c10.json`, run here.

- Tests:
  - the tail rule on replies made by hand, including one in the prompt's order and one that names no board word;
  - the listener's weights against a loop;
  - the fit of T recovering a known T on turns sampled from a Luce guesser;

## Stopping rules and a null result

- **Pilot.** It checks the providers and the reading of replies. It also checks the tail rule, the tokens and the cost. Nothing from it is scored. A fault it finds is fixed in an amendment before validation.
- **Validation.** If more than 5% of the listener's validation replies name no board word, the test does not start until an amendment fixes the cause.
- **Failed calls.** A listener call that fails five times, or returns an empty reply, is sent once more at the end of its job. If that also fails, the clue's listener gives every word the same rank, and the count is reported.
- **The test** is sent once, after K and w are committed.
- **Models.** If gpt-oss-120b is not served, the plan does not run, since T is fitted to it. If Qwen3.8-27B, Llama 3.3 or DeepSeek-V3.2 is not served, that arm or guesser is dropped and not replaced, and an amendment says so before the run.
- **A null result.** If H-listen-strangers fails, the README states that a language model used as a listener inside the giver did not beat the embedding giver's first choice with strangers. It gives the share of the gap closed, and L minus S. If H-add-strangers fails, the README states that the listener added nothing that these strangers could measure to a population of six embedding guessers. Each human-fitted result is reported the same way.

## Threats to validity

- **The scoring guessers are models.** The strangers are embedding models, and the human-fitted guesser is a linear map on one. A listener that guesses as people do could still lose with them. This is why the human-fitted guesser is a primary measure and the language-model guessers are reported, but no person guesses here.
- **The proposer limits the choice.** Every candidate is one of EmbeddingGemma 2's top K clues, as in C6, so the listener can only reorder clues that an embedding model already likes.
- **The listener's model is simple.** One temperature over ranks, fitted on first picks, with a prompt that always says count 1. A guesser told the real count might rank differently, and the second and later picks rest on the order of the informative prefix. The language-model guessers hear the real count, which tests part of this.
- **The fit's data.** C4 selected gpt-oss-120b on the same validation turns on which T is fitted. Neither use touches C1's test turns. Cultural Codes has been public since 2023 and may be in the model's training data.
- **Nondeterminism.** In C4, gpt-oss-120b's reply changed on 16 of 20 prompts sent three times at temperature 0. The repeat check measures how often that changes L's pick.
- **Selection.** Seven settings are compared on 100 validation boards. The test boards are fresh and are used once.
