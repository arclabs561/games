# C5. Codenames: reinforcement learning of a small language-model giver

## Question

Does training a small language model to give clues, with a reward from a population of guessers, produce clues that held-out guessers understand better than clues from the same model trained against a single guesser?

## When it runs

Only if C4's H-give holds: hosted language-model givers keep more value with strangers than the best embedding giver. Otherwise this plan is closed unrun, and the README says why. If C4 is null, a small model trained here would start from a giver that is no better than an embedding giver, and the question would be about RL alone, which C3 already asks more cheaply.

## Why it matters

C3 asks whether population training helps a policy over seven hand-made features. A language model's policy is far richer and can exploit its training partners in ways the feature policy cannot. Deshpande et al. (2026, arXiv:2605.27832) train Qwen3 models on Codenames with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards. Their judge is a single frozen model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and their game drops neutral words and the assassin. Their paper does not report how such a giver does with guessers other than its judge.

- If population reward beats single-guesser reward with held-out guessers, the other-play lesson (Hu et al., 2020) carries over to language-model givers, and single-judge rewards are a weakness of the RLVR setup.
- If the two are equal, a single judge is enough, at least among embedding guessers.

## Design

- **Model.** Qwen3-0.6B, thinking off. Full fine-tuning is not needed; LoRA rank 32 on attention and MLP projections.
- **Algorithm.** GRPO as implemented in TRL, with 8 samples per prompt, a KL weight of 0.02 and the TRL defaults otherwise. No hyperparameter search: these settings are fixed now, and the pilot (below) only checks that training runs.
- **Prompt.** `llm.giver_prompt` as in C4, so the untrained model is C4's prompt applied to a small model.
- **Reward.** The exact expected turn value (`luce.py`, the README's costs) of the parsed clue and count, averaged over the training guessers. An unparseable reply or illegal clue scores −1. A clue with no vector for a training guesser also scores −1. The policy can learn to stay inside the vocabulary, which is the 12,000 words of `words.clue_words()` and nothing else. This reward is computed, not judged, so it costs no model calls.
- **Arms**, one factor apart:
  - *single*: the training guesser is potion alone;
  - *population*: the training guessers are potion, wordllama, granite, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B and harrier-oss-v1-270m.
- **Held out from both arms:** EmbeddingGemma 2, bge-small, harrier-oss-v1-0.6b and the EAT association guesser. potion is in both training sets, so the arms differ only in the added partners.
- **Training boards** come from board seeds 5000 onward. **Test boards:** 200 boards from seed 5500, never seen in training or in the pilot.
- **Baselines** on the same test boards: the untrained Qwen3-0.6B, and the EmbeddingGemma 2 expected-value giver of C4.

## Primary metric and test

Mean expected turn value over the three held-out embedding guessers, population arm minus single arm. Paired by test board. Three training seeds per arm, with seed i of one arm paired with seed i of the other. 95% interval by bootstrap that resamples boards (shared) and seed pairs. 10,000 resamples, seed 0. Population reward is said to help if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- The same difference for the EAT guesser.
- Each arm's value with its own training guessers, the self-play side of the gap.
- Each arm against the untrained model and against the EmbeddingGemma 2 giver.
- The share of replies that score −1, and the mean count, over training.
- Distinct clues used on the test boards. A collapse to a few safe clues would show here.

## Sample size

200 boards, three held-out guessers, and paired arms give a board-level standard error near 0.03 for the difference, as in C4. Three seeds per arm add seed variance, which is unknown for this setup. The pilot gives one estimate of it, and the report states the per-seed differences so that a reader can see the spread. Three seeds is the minimum that METHOD.md allows for expensive runs. It can detect a difference of about 0.15 if seeds vary about as much as boards. It cannot detect a small effect, and the report will say so.

## Seeds

Pilot: seed 0, one run per arm, on 100 steps, exploratory. Confirmatory: training seeds 1, 2 and 3 for each arm. Test-board seed 5500; bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Jobs, `l4x1` ($0.80 an hour, 24 GB), which fits a 0.6B model with LoRA and 8 samples per prompt. The embedding guessers run on CPU inside the job from precomputed vectors, which the job copies from the branch `compute/codenames-embed`. Estimate per run: 600 steps of 16 prompts, about 1 to 1.5 hours. Pilot about $1.50, confirmatory six runs about $7, and evaluation runs on CPU. Hard cap $10, with a 2-hour timeout per job and no more than 8 jobs. This plan needs approval before the pilot and again before the confirmatory runs. The README listed an A100 for C5; a 0.6B model does not need one, and an L4 costs a third as much an hour.

## Entry points

To be written:

- `codenames/rlvr.py`: the reward function (tested against `luce.py` on fixed clues), the TRL GRPO loop, and `--arm single|population --seed S --steps N --out OUT`.
- A `codenames-gpu` dependency group (shared with C2) with `trl`, `peft`, `torch` and `transformers`.
- Launch: `hf jobs uv run --flavor l4x1 --timeout 2h -e COMMIT=<sha> -e GROUP=codenames-gpu -e EXPERIMENT=c5 -e RUN=population-s1 --secrets HF_TOKEN experiments/hfrun.py codenames.rlvr --arm population --seed 1 --steps 600 --out OUT`.
- The job ends by generating clues for the 200 test boards and writing them to `clues.json`. Scoring runs here: `python -m codenames.c5 --runs <folder> --out codenames/results/c5.json`.

## Outputs

- In git: `codenames/results/c5.json`, with per-board scores per arm and seed, the training curves (mean reward every 20 steps) and the costs, and a README section.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/c5/<run>/`: the LoRA adapters, the training log, and all generated clues.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The pilot must show that mean reward rises and the −1 share falls within 100 steps. If it does not, the confirmatory runs do not start, and the README reports that training did not take at this setting. No tuning on test is allowed to rescue it. If the primary interval includes 0, the README reports that population reward did not beat single-guesser reward for this model, with the per-seed results.

## Threats to validity

- **Small model.** A 0.6B model may not have room for the effect. A null result says nothing about 8B models.
- **Vocabulary pressure.** The −1 for clues without vectors pushes both arms toward common words. That is the same for both arms, but it is not part of the game.
- **Guessers are embedding models.** The held-out guessers share the training guessers' kind. Clues a person would take differently are not measured.
- **Few seeds.** Three seeds per arm is thin, and the plan says so in the result.
