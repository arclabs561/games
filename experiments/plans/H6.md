# H6. Hanabi: H4's gap between self-play and cross-play, compared at matched self-play

## Question

In H4's two-color game, the policies trained with entropy coefficient α = 0.05 played better with other seeds than those trained with α = 0.01, and they also played better with themselves. When the two arms are compared at checkpoints where their self-play scores are the same, is the gap between self-play and cross-play still smaller at α = 0.05?

## Why it matters

H4 compared the arms after a fixed number of steps, 2 × 10⁸ a seed. Its results (`hanabi/results/h4.md`): cross-play rose by 1.12 of 10 (0.83 to 1.41), self-play by 0.77 (0.53 to 1.01), and self-play minus cross-play was 0.69 (0.48 to 0.95) at α = 0.01 and 0.34 (0.22 to 0.45) at α = 0.05. A methods review of the folder notes that the α = 0.05 arm also learns faster and that the curves had not flattened. A smaller gap at a fixed step count can then come from two causes: higher entropy makes seeds compatible (Forkel et al.'s claim), or a better-trained policy in this game is more compatible whatever its α. Comparing the arms at equal self-play separates the two.

H4 saved only each seed's final parameters (`ippo.save_params` overwrites them after each chunk), so no earlier checkpoint exists. The comparison needs the arms trained again with checkpoints kept.

- If the gap at matched self-play is still smaller at α = 0.05, the entropy effect is not only speed.
- If the gaps agree at matched self-play, H4's gap difference is explained by learning speed in this game, and the README says so.
- Training longer also says whether the curves flatten, and whether cross-play at α = 0.01 catches up with time.

## Design

- **Everything as in H4** (plan, amendments and `ippo.py` at H4's commit `41f3ba7`): JaxMARL 0.2.0, the two-color game, LSTM, λ = 0.9, the masked entropy bonus, the baseline's other settings, one network shared by both seats, greedy play for evaluation.
- **Arms:** α = 0.01 and α = 0.05, training seeds 1 to 8 each, which start from the same random keys as H4's seeds. The checkpoint at 2 × 10⁸ steps is a reproduction check against H4's final policies (JAX on a GPU is not promised to be bitwise repeatable, so agreement is checked within intervals, not exactly).
- **Length:** 5 × 10⁸ steps a seed (3,815 updates of 1,024 games of 128 steps), 2.5 times H4.
- **Checkpoints:** every 191 updates (about 2.5 × 10⁷ steps), 20 per seed, kept as `params-seed{s}-u{update}.msgpack`.
- **Validation deals** (to place the checkpoints): seeds 402,000 to 402,499 of `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=2, colors=2), seed)`. For every checkpoint, all 8 self-play pairings and 56 ordered cross-play pairings of its arm play each deal once, greedily.
- **Test deals** (scored once): seeds 403,000 to 404,999. No earlier run used either range (H4 used 400,000 to 401,999 and its pilot 500,000 to 501,999). Open-game ceilings computed as in H4, with Solver A and Solver B.

## The matched point, fixed now

- **S\* = 8.40 points of 10,** the level a little below H4's final greedy self-play at α = 0.01 (8.43), so that both arms are expected to reach it.
- For each arm, the matched checkpoint is the first one whose mean greedy self-play over the 8 seeds on the validation deals is at least S\*. The arm's checkpoint index is the same for all its seeds, so cross-play pairs policies trained for the same number of steps.
- If an arm never reaches S\*, the matched point for both arms is the highest validation self-play that both reach, rounded down to 0.05, and the report says so.

## Primary metric and test

- **H-matched.** At the matched checkpoints, on the test deals: (self-play − cross-play) at α = 0.01 minus (self-play − cross-play) at α = 0.05. Self-play is the mean over the 8 seeds with themselves, cross-play the mean over the 56 ordered pairs, both paired by deal. 95% interval by a bootstrap that resamples deals (shared by both arms) and, within each arm, its 8 seeds, rebuilding the pairs, as in H4 (10,000 resamples, seed 0; a pair of two draws of the same seed is left out of cross-play).
- **Decision.** If the interval lies above 0, the higher bonus narrows the gap at matched self-play. If it holds 0, the gap difference is not separated from learning speed at this resolution, reported with the detectable effect. If it lies below 0, the reverse.

## Secondary metrics

- Self-play at the two matched checkpoints on the test deals, and their difference: the matching is good if it is within 0.1 points.
- Curves on the validation deals, per arm: self-play, cross-play and their gap at every checkpoint, plotted against steps and against self-play.
- **Plateau.** An arm has flattened if its mean training score rose by less than 0.05 points over the last 20% of training. Reported per seed and arm.
- At 5 × 10⁸ steps, on the test deals: H4's primary metric (cross-play, α = 0.05 minus α = 0.01) and each arm's gap, to see whether α = 0.01 catches up.
- **Reproduction:** at 2 × 10⁸ steps, each arm's self-play and cross-play on the test deals, beside H4's numbers on its deals.
- The 16 × 16 cross-play matrix at the matched checkpoints and at the end; the behavior counts of H4 (color hints, plays of the newest card, discards by slot) at the matched checkpoints.
- Sampled (non-greedy) play at the matched checkpoints.

## Minimum detectable effect

From H4's intervals, the gap's standard error was about (0.95 − 0.48) / 3.92 ≈ 0.12 at α = 0.01 and (0.45 − 0.22) / 3.92 ≈ 0.059 at α = 0.05, so the difference has a standard error near 0.134 with 8 seeds an arm. The detectable effect is 2.80 × 0.134 ≈ 0.37 points. H4's own difference of gaps was 0.35. **The plan is slightly underpowered for an effect of H4's size**: if the true effect at matched self-play is 0.35, the test detects it about three times in four (0.35 / 0.134 = 2.6 standard errors), and a smaller effect, which a matched comparison may well give, less often. Twelve seeds an arm would bring the detectable effect to about 0.31 at 1.5 times the training cost ($3.50 an arm instead of $2.30). The plan keeps 8 seeds, to stay with H4's seeds and its reproduction check, and states the detectable effect with any null.

## Seeds

Training seeds 1 to 8 per arm. Validation deals 402,000 to 402,499; test deals 403,000 to 404,999. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Jobs, `a10g-small` ($1.00 an hour; the L4 queue held H4's pilot for 70 minutes), through `experiments/hfrun.py`, group `hanabi-jax`.

- **Training.** H4 trained 8 seeds together at about 481,000 steps a second. 8 × 5 × 10⁸ steps take about 8,300 seconds, 2.3 hours an arm, plus about 3 minutes of setup and compilation (H4's measured overhead). Writing 20 checkpoints of 8 seeds is small.
- **Checkpoint evaluation.** 20 checkpoints × 64 pairings × 500 deals = 640,000 games an arm. H4's evaluation played 1,024,000 games in 43 seconds and replayed them in `rules.py` in a few minutes, so both arms' checkpoints take well under an hour.
- **Test evaluation** of the matched, 2 × 10⁸ and final checkpoints on 2,000 deals: about 1.2 million games, minutes.

| Job | Timeout | Expected cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pilot: `--keep-every` on 2 × 10⁷ steps, checkpoint loading, the agreement test | 20 min | $0.15 |
| Arm α = 0.01, 5 × 10⁸ steps | 3 h (training stops and saves at 2 h 45 min) | $2.35 |
| Arm α = 0.05, 5 × 10⁸ steps | 3 h (same) | $2.35 |
| Validation of all checkpoints, then the test evaluation | 1 h | $0.40 |

**Expected about $5.25. Hard cap $7.50**: the timeouts add up to 7 h 20 min, $7.33. If the pilot's speed would make 5 × 10⁸ steps pass the training deadline, the step count is cut to what fits by an amendment before the arms run, and the plateau question is reported as unsettled.

## Outputs

- In the private dataset, under `runs/h6/`: all checkpoints and per-game records.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- The agreement test of H4 must pass on the GPU again before training.
- Each arm runs once; a job that fails for reasons outside the code is rerun once.
- The test deals are scored once, after the matched checkpoints are chosen on the validation deals.
- **A null result.** If H-matched's interval holds 0, the README says that at matched self-play the two arms' gaps were not distinguishable, with the interval and the detectable effect of 0.37, and that H4's gap difference may come from learning speed.

## Threats to validity

- **Matching on one number.** Equal self-play does not mean equal skill in every respect.
- **Arm-level matching.** All seeds of an arm use the same checkpoint index, so seeds that learn at different speeds are not each matched.
- **The two-color game** has two color hints and a group of color permutations of size two, so it tests a weak form of Forkel et al.'s mechanism (a point the methods review made).
- **Still far fewer steps** than Forkel et al.'s 10¹⁰.
