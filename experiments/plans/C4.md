# C4. Codenames: hosted language models as guessers and givers

## Questions

1. **Guessing.** Do hosted language models pick the same word as a person more often than the best embedding model?
2. **Giving.** When a language model gives clues, do guessers it has never met find more of its words than they find of the best embedding giver's?

## Why it matters

Embedding givers lose 0.5 to 0.9 words per clue when the guesser is a different model (`codenames/README.md`, section 2). A language model is trained on how people use words in context, so its clues might suit strangers better. Or it might share the embedding models' blind spots.

- If language models predict human guesses better, the human-guess model in sections 1 and 4 should be a language model, and C2's tuned encoder is the wrong baseline to stop at.
- If language-model clues keep more of their value with strangers, C5 is worth running. If not, C5 is not run (see `C5.md`).
- A null on both is still a measured result. The README notes that the two benchmarks it cites do not measure this gap on fixed boards with partners that are not language models.

## Models

Four open-weight models from four families, served through Hugging Face Inference Providers. Ordered list, fixed now:

1. Llama 3.3 70B Instruct
2. Qwen3 32B (thinking off)
3. gpt-oss-120b (low reasoning effort)
4. DeepSeek-V3 (the newest V3 release served)

Substitutes, in order, if a model is not served when the run starts: Gemma 3 27B, Mistral Small 3. The model id, provider, date and every request parameter are recorded per call. Temperature 0 and a fixed seed where the provider accepts one. Providers do not promise determinism, so 20 prompts per model are sent three times to measure how often the reply changes.

## Design

### Part 1: guessing

- **Data.** C1's game split, test turns only. One call per turn per model: `llm.guesser_prompt` with count 1, and the first word of `llm.parse_ranking` is the guess.
- **Baselines.** EmbeddingGemma 2 (best untrained in section 1) and potion with the linear map trained on C1's train games (C1's arm). Their per-turn hits come from C1.

### Part 2: giving

- **Boards.** 200 fresh boards, board seed 4004. The seed-0 boards of section 2 are scored too, for comparison with the README, but they are not the test.
- **Givers.** Each of the four models through `llm.llm_giver`, with the README's clue rule. The baseline is the EmbeddingGemma 2 expected-value giver (`players.expected_giver`), chosen now because it kept the largest share of its value with other models in section 2. Its clue pool is the whole `words.clue_words()` list, not only EAT cues, so the baseline is not handicapped against the language models.
- **Guessers.** The seven calibrated embedding Luce guessers other than EmbeddingGemma 2. Each is a stranger to every giver in this comparison. Each clue is scored by the exact expected turn value (`luce.py`), so there is no sampling noise from the guesser.
- **Clues outside the vocabulary.** A legal clue for which a guesser has no vector is embedded afterwards: the list of new words goes through `embed.py` on the GitHub runner workflow `compute/codenames-embed`, for all eight models. Only a reply that cannot be parsed or breaks the clue rule gets the fallback (count 1). Its rate is reported per model.

## Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, tested with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05:

- **H-guess.** For the best of the four models by validation top-1 (chosen on C1's val turns before test is scored): top-1 on test minus EmbeddingGemma 2's top-1, paired by turn, bootstrap over test games.
- **H-give.** For the best of the four models by mean expected value on 50 validation boards (seed 4005): mean expected turn value over the seven stranger guessers, language-model giver minus EmbeddingGemma 2 giver, paired by board, bootstrap over the 200 test boards.

10,000 resamples, seed 0. The Holm step uses the bootstrap p-value: the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0, doubled.

## Secondary metrics

- All four models on both parts, with intervals, and without significance claims.
- Expected words found, without the costs of wrong picks.
- The EAT association guesser as a further stranger, with the caveat that it answers only EAT cues.
- Language-model guessers hearing clues from givers of other families: for each language-model giver, the three other models, and for the EmbeddingGemma 2 giver, all four. One sampled ranking per clue, so these are noisier than the exact embedding scores.
- Each giver's mean count, the fallback rate and the share of clues outside the vocabulary.
- Log-likelihood of the person's word is not available for language models without token probabilities, and is not reported.

## Sample size

- **Guessing.** About 750 test turns (C1). The half-width for a paired difference is 0.04 to 0.05, so a difference of 5 points is resolved and a smaller one is reported as unresolved.
- **Giving.** In section 2 a single giver's value with one other model has a standard error over 200 boards of 0.02 to 0.05. Averaging seven guessers and pairing by board leaves a standard error near 0.03 for the difference, a half-width near 0.06. The gaps between givers in section 2 are 0.1 to 0.4 words, so a gap of that size is detected.

## Compute and cost

Hugging Face Inference Providers, called from a CPU job (`cpu-basic`) through `hfrun.py`, so that the token stays in the job's secrets. Calls:

| Part | Calls per model | All four models |
|---|---:|---:|
| Guessing, val and test turns | about 1,600 | 6,400 |
| Giving, 250 boards | 250 | 1,000 |
| Language-model guessers on other givers' clues | about 1,000 | 4,000 |
| Repeat check | 60 | 240 |

Prompts are 150 to 300 tokens. Replies are capped at 64 tokens for givers and 256 for guessers, with reasoning off or set low. That is about 5 million tokens in all, or $1 to $3 at the providers' listed prices for these models. Hard cap $3, enforced in code: the job adds up the reported token usage times the listed price and stops at the cap. If it stops, it reports the parts completed in the fixed order above. The scoring of clues by embedding guessers runs on CPU here and on the GitHub runner. This plan needs approval before its jobs.

## Stopping rules and a null result

Validation turns and boards select the model for each primary hypothesis. Test is scored once, after selection. If a hypothesis fails, the README reports the difference and interval and states that hosted models did not outperform EmbeddingGemma 2 on that task. C5 runs only if H-give holds; see `C5.md`.

## Threats to validity

- **Model drift.** Hosted models change without notice. The plan records ids and dates, and the repeat check measures nondeterminism, but a rerun next year may differ.
- **Contamination.** Cultural Codes has been public since 2023 and could be in a model's training data, which would inflate Part 1. The prompts carry no dataset name. The risk is noted, not removed.
- **Prompt.** One prompt per role, as written in `llm.py`, with no tuning on test. A better prompt might do better, so the claim is about this prompt.
- **The guessers are models.** Part 2's strangers are embedding models, not people. Part 1 is the only contact with human behavior.

## Amendment, 2026-11-19

Written after two pilot runs (`runs/c4/pilot-1` and `pilot-2` in the private dataset: 50 boards with seed 4999, which no confirmatory run uses) and before any confirmatory run. The pilots are exploratory and are used only for cost, parse failures and request settings.

- **Models.** All four planned models were served, so no substitute is used: `meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct` (provider novita), `Qwen/Qwen3-32B` (deepinfra, `chat_template_kwargs: {enable_thinking: false}`), `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (deepinfra, `reasoning_effort: low`) and `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2` (deepinfra), the newest V3 release on the Hub. Each is pinned to one provider, recorded per call.
- **Token caps.** gpt-oss counts its reasoning against `max_tokens`. With 64 tokens, all 50 of its pilot clues were cut off before the answer. Its cap is 1,024 tokens for both roles. The other models keep 64 and 256.
- **Rate limits.** deepinfra refused about half of the Qwen3-32B pilot calls with HTTP 429 at 8 calls in flight. Qwen3-32B gets 2 calls in flight. Every model gets 5 attempts per call, with waits of 2 to 32 seconds. A call that fails 5 times counts as a reply that cannot be parsed.
- **Cost.** No provider returned a cost in the usage object. The meter charges the reported tokens at each provider's price listed by the router (`router.huggingface.co/v1/models`), read at the start of each job, as the plan says.
- **Fallback clue.** The plan gives a failed reply "the fallback (count 1)" without naming the clue. It is the clue of the EmbeddingGemma 2 expected-value giver limited to count 1 (`ExpectedGiver(..., nmax=1)`). In the pilots, 17 to 23 of 50 clues from Llama 3.3 and DeepSeek-V3.2 were board words or contained one, so this choice matters. Two sensitivity checks are reported beside H-give, without significance claims: a failed reply valued 0, and the boards where the reply was legal.
- **Guessers' calibration.** The strangers use the templates and temperatures of `results/calibrate-game.json`, fitted on C1's game split. They are the same as those in `calibrate.json`.
- **Part 1 data and intervals.** C1's game split has 717 val turns in 79 games and 551 test turns in 68 games. Intervals and the H-guess p-value use C1's game bootstrap (`codenames/stats.py`: resample games, pool their turns), 10,000 draws, seed 0, paired by turn. The EmbeddingGemma 2 baseline is recomputed here on the same turns: 0.548 (0.501 to 0.595) on test, as in C1.
- **Repeat check.** The first 20 boards of seed 4005 are sent two more times to each model, as part of the giving job.

## Amendment, 2026-11-20: the giving part rescored under the rulebook's clue rule (exploratory)

Written after C4's results were read, so everything here is exploratory and no number from it replaces a primary result. It makes no model call.

- **Why.** C4 judged a clue with `words.legal`: not a board word, not containing one, not contained in one. The rulebook forbids "any form of a visible word" and "part of a compound word on the table" (Czech Games Edition, English rules of July 2015), and it does not forbid every substring. `results/diagnostics/legality.md` checked only the clues that passed `words.legal`. The replies that C4 rejected, about half of two models' clues, were never checked under the rulebook, and some may be legal (a clue contained in a board word by accident of spelling), while some accepted clues are forms of board words (19.0% of DeepSeek-V3.2's and 18.5% of Llama 3.3's).
- **What is computed.** Every raw giver reply on the 200 test boards (seed 4004) and the 50 validation boards (seed 4005), read from the private dataset (`runs/c4/`), is parsed as C4 parses it and classified by `legality.worst` into legal, borderline and illegal. Reported per model: the share of replies at each level, and the cross-table of `words.legal` against the rulebook levels. H-give's difference is then recomputed with a clue accepted when `legality.strict_legal` allows it and the fallback otherwise, the fallback being EmbeddingGemma 2's best strictly legal clue for one word, and the embedding giver itself restricted to strictly legal clues. Intervals by bootstrap over boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0).
- **Where it goes.** `codenames/results/c4-strict.json` and a paragraph in `c4.md`, labeled exploratory.
- **Cost.** One `cpu-basic` job to read the private dataset, minutes; no paid calls. Entry point to be written: `python -m codenames.c4 strict --runs DIR --out codenames/results/c4-strict.json`, with a test on three hand-made replies (a substring that the rulebook allows, a plural, a compound part).
