# C15. Codenames: has gpt-oss-120b memorized Cultural Codes?

## Question

gpt-oss-120b picks the person's word on 65.0% of C1's test turns, 10.2 points more than EmbeddingGemma 2 (C4). Cultural Codes has been public since 2023. When the board of a human turn is changed in ways a person's choice would not depend on, does the model's agreement with the person drop more than the embedding guessers' agreement does?

## Why it matters

The README's guessing headline and C8's student "level with its teacher" both rest on gpt-oss-120b's agreement with people. `c4.md` says contamination "is not measured". A methods review notes that equal agreement on training and test turns does not test memorization, since both splits are public, and proposes this probe.

- If the model's agreement falls more than the embeddings' under the changes, part of its lead may be memory of the dataset, and the README says so beside the 65%.
- If not, the probe finds no sign of memorization at its detectable effect, and the README says that.

## Design

- **Turns:** 2,000 turns drawn from C1's training games (`codenames/splits.json`), by seed 15001, whole games at a time. Training turns, not test turns: the test split has four confirmatory uses already, and memorization, if any, is as likely on either split. This is not a use of the test set.
- **Arms**, the same turns in each, one factor apart:
  - **O, original:** C4's guesser prompt with count 1 and the board in the dataset's order.
  - **S, shuffled:** the board words in a random order (seed 15002 per turn).
  - **W, swapped:** S, and each board word other than the person's pick replaced by a noun from `words.txt`'s source list (WordNet nouns of 3 to 9 letters) with Zipf frequency within 0.1 of it, not on the board and not equal to or a form of the clue (`legality.strict_legal` against the clue). Seed 15003. The clue and the person's pick are unchanged.
- **Guessers:** gpt-oss-120b pinned to DeepInfra (reasoning effort low, 1,024 tokens), the primary; Llama 3.3 70B pinned to Novita (256 tokens), secondary, on the first 1,000 turns. EmbeddingGemma 2 and potion with C1's linear map as the embedding controls, computed exactly here. Temperature 0, seed 0.
- **Agreement:** top-1, whether the guesser's first board word is the person's pick.

## Primary metric and test

- **H-memory.** Difference in differences, paired by turn: [gpt-oss-120b's agreement on O − on W] − [EmbeddingGemma 2's agreement on O − on W]. 95% interval by bootstrap over games (10,000 resamples, seed 0). The swap changes how hard a turn is for every guesser; the embedding control removes that part. An interval above 0 is evidence that the model's lead depends on the exact board as published.

## Secondary metrics

- The same with S in place of W (order alone), and with potion's map as the control.
- Llama 3.3's difference in differences on its 1,000 turns.
- Each guesser's agreement on O, S and W with Wilson intervals.
- The share of turns where the model names a word not on the changed board but on the original (a direct sign of memory), counted exactly.
- Repeat check: 20 prompts per arm sent twice more.

## Minimum detectable effect

The per-turn difference in differences takes values from −2 to 2; with agreement near 0.6 and correlated arms, its standard deviation is near 0.7 (planning value). Turns come in games of about 9 turns; with a design effect of 1.3, 2,000 turns give 2.80 × 0.7 × √1.3 / √2000 ≈ 0.050, five points of agreement. The effect worth finding is half of the 10.2-point lead, 5 points. Powered for that. Equivalence margin δ = 0.03: an interval inside −0.03 to 0.03 is reported as no memorization effect larger than 3 points.

## Data

Cultural Codes (Shaikh et al., 2023), github.com/SALT-NLP/codenames. The repository states no license (read 2026-11-20); the folder's data table says the paper plans CC BY-SA 4.0. Nothing from it is committed: `fetch.py` downloads it inside the job, and only aggregates go into git. Perturbed boards contain only WordNet nouns and the dataset's own words, and stay in the private dataset.

## Entry points

`c4.guess`, `c4.read_ranking`, `splits.json` and the embedding guessers exist. To be written: `codenames/contamination.py` (the swap, with tests that the pick and clue are unchanged, that no swapped word is on the original board, and that frequencies are within 0.1), `python -m codenames.c15 guess --arm O|S|W ...`, and `c15 score --out codenames/results/c15.json`.

## Outputs

In git: `codenames/results/c15.json`, `c15-runs.json`, and a sentence in the README's guessing section and in `c4.md`'s contamination bullet. In the private dataset, under `runs/c15/`: prompts, replies and the perturbed boards.

## Stopping rules and a null result

The turns are sent once per arm; a failed job is rerun once from the start. A null is stated with the detectable effect of 5 points, and the README keeps "not measured" replaced by "not detected at 5 points".

## Threats to validity

- A model can memorize in ways the swap does not disturb (the clue and pick are kept).
- The swap can make a turn easier or harder for a language model in ways an embedding does not share; Llama 3.3 is a second family for this reason.
- Only top-1 agreement; the full ranking is not scored.

## Amendment, 2026-11-20: the swap pool, the turns and the pilot (before any model call)

Written after the code was run here on the turns and the swapped boards, and before any model call or any guesser's agreement on these turns was computed.

- **The swap pool.** The plan draws replacements from WordNet nouns of 3 to 9 letters. The embedding controls need vectors for every word on a swapped board, so the pool is those nouns that have vectors here: one-word lowercase WordNet nouns of 3 to 9 letters among `evaluate.all_words()`, which holds the clue words, the board words and the words of Cultural Codes. With the clue and board words alone the least common candidate has Zipf frequency 3.37, and 1,459 of the 42,810 replacements (rare words such as leprechaun and centaur) had no candidate within 0.1. With Cultural Codes' own words added, as the plan's data section allows, every replacement is within 0.1. A replacement is a uniform draw among the allowed candidates in the window.
- **The turns.** Whole games in the order of seed 15001 until there are at least 2,000 turns: 2,009 turns in 263 games. Llama 3.3 takes the first games holding at least 1,000 turns: 1,004 turns.
- **The pilot** uses the 20 turns that follow the scored ones in the same order of games. Nothing from it is scored.
- **Unanswered turns.** A reply that is empty, failed or came from another provider is sent once more at the end of its job. A reply that names no board word after that is not read as a guess. H-memory is computed on the turns a model answered in both arms, and the number dropped is reported.
- **The controls.** EmbeddingGemma 2 uses C1's calibrated templates (`calibrate-game.json`). Potion's linear map is trained, as in C1, on C1's training games, which are the games these turns come from, so that control has seen these turns; it stays secondary.
