# C11. Codenames: does the population giver's lead hold with guessers of other families, and with people?

## Question

C10's population pick (P) beats the embedding giver's first choice (F) by 0.24 words per turn with embedding guessers it never met. Does it still beat F (a) with hosted language-model guessers of two other families, and (b) with people guessing on a phone?

## Why it matters

Every giver result in this folder is scored by guessers of one kind. Some are embedding models. One is a linear map on an embedding model, fitted to Cultural Codes (the human-fitted guesser). One answers from the Edinburgh Associative Thesaurus (EAT). Simulated partners can make a method look better than it is with people, and can change which method ranks first. Carroll et al. (2019) found this in Overcooked: agents trained by self-play scored well with each other and worse with people than agents trained with a model of human play. The one study in which people played with Codenames agents (Archibald et al., 2025) found that an adaptive agent did better on three of four metrics used in earlier work, and that people preferred the basic agent all the same.

The headline giver result here is C10's comparison of P with F, on 300 test boards (seed 9004):

| Scored by | P − F, words per turn | 95% interval | How known |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Seven embedding strangers (held out in turn) | 0.239 | 0.198 to 0.282 | exact values, bootstrap over boards |
| Human-fitted guesser (potion maps of C1) | 0.140 | 0.054 to 0.229 | exact values, bootstrap over boards |
| EAT association guesser | 0.003 | −0.081 to 0.087 | exact values, bootstrap over boards |
| Llama 3.3 70B and DeepSeek-V3.2, turns played out | 0.025 | −0.095 to 0.153 | one reply per clue, bootstrap over boards |

All four rows are in `codenames/results/c10.json`. The last two were secondary in C10, and here they are exploratory. They suggest that the lead shrinks with guessers that are not embedding models. C3 is a related result: a giver trained against a population beat one trained against its own model by 0.31 (0.26 to 0.36) with held-out embedding guessers. Its arms are trained policies from a different candidate list, so C11 does not test it (see "Arms").

- If P beats F with the language-model guessers, the population result reaches beyond the embedding family, and the human pilot is worth scaling up.
- If the language-model lead is near 0 and below the embedding lead, the 0.24 is mostly agreement within one family of models. The README then says so in its giver sections.
- The human pilot cannot settle either case (see "Part B, power"). It measures how much people's turns vary, tests the page, and gives a direction with an interval for a powered study.

## Arms

Both arms are C10's, computed by C10's code unchanged (`codenames/actions.py`, `codenames/listen.py`, `codenames/c10score.py`). Nothing is trained.

- **Candidates.** EmbeddingGemma 2 proposes 30 legal clues on each board, each with counts 1 to 4: 120 actions (`actions.shortlist`). The prefilter keeps the K = 10 clues with the highest proposer value (`listen.prefilter`), K as C10 chose it on its validation boards.
- **F, first choice:** the action of the 120 that the proposer's Luce guesser values most.
- **P, population:** among the 40 kept actions, the one with the highest mean exact value over six embedding guessers. These are the seven strangers less potion. This is C10's task that holds out potion, the task whose pick C10 sent to the human-fitted and language-model guessers. In C11 every scoring guesser is outside the population, so one task is enough, and P is a single clue per board.
- **Oracle (reference, part A only):** the kept action best for the mean of all seven strangers, as in C10. It is the best that the embedding family can find in the list, not the best for the scoring guessers.

`codenames/human_pilot.py` (`giver_picks`) computes the three picks. Its `build` step first checks that they equal C10's committed F, P and oracle on the first three test boards of seed 9004. A separate check matched all three picks on C10's first 12 test boards.

The five-seed rule of the method applies where training is involved. No arm here is trained. C3's trained population giver is left out because it proposes from potion's or Qwen3's own shortlist, so it would differ from F in two factors at once.

## Part A: guessers of other families

### Design

- **Boards.** 1,000 test boards of seed 11004 and 5 pilot boards of seed 11999, dealt by `c4.boards` from `words.txt`. No earlier plan uses a seed from 11000 to 11999.
- **Guessers**, both from families other than the embedding models and from each other, each pinned to one provider (`c4.MODELS`):
  - gpt-oss-120b (OpenAI) through deepinfra, reasoning effort low, replies capped at 1,024 tokens. It matched the person's word on 0.650 of C1's test turns in C4, the highest of any guesser here.
  - Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (Meta) through novita, replies capped at 256 tokens. It matched on 0.606.
  - Both at temperature 0 and seed 0. A model that is not served by its pinned provider is not replaced by another provider during the run. The plan stops, and an amendment says what replaces it before any test call.
- **Prompt and play.** C4's guesser prompt with the action's own count (`llm.guesser_prompt(clue, count, words)`), read with `c4.read_ranking`. The turn is played out from the reply's order by `c4.play_turn`: up to the count, ending at the first word that is not the team's. A wrong pick costs 1 for an opponent word, 0 for a neutral word, and every team word still to find for the assassin, as everywhere in this folder. This is `rerank crossguess --distinct`, which sends each distinct (board, clue, count) once per guesser.
- **Each reply is one sample.** Providers are not deterministic at temperature 0. C10 reports that gpt-oss-120b's reply changed on 16 of 20 C4 prompts sent three times. A board's value is therefore one draw, and the repeat check below measures how much it moves.
- **Exact values**, at no cost, on the same boards: the seven embedding strangers (C10's stranger value of P, the mean over its seven tasks, and the potion task's pick under potion), the human-fitted guesser, and EAT.
- **Small World of Words (optional, secondary).** An association guesser on SWOW-EN18 counts (`norms.swow_counts`, `players.norm_weigher`), with its ε fitted on C1's training turns as EAT's is. SWOW is CC BY-NC-ND 3.0: it cannot be fetched by a script, and neither the data nor anything derived from it per cue is committed. That includes the fitted counts, per-board values and per-clue strings. It runs only on a machine where the file was downloaded by hand. Only these go into git: the fitted ε, the number of test clues it covers, and the means and intervals of P − F and oracle − F. If it is not run, the report says so.

### Primary metrics and tests

Two primary hypotheses, tested together with the Holm correction at a family level of 0.05. Both are paired by test board.

- **H-A1, the lead holds.** The mean over the two language-model guessers of the played-out turn value, P minus F.
- **H-A2, the lead shrinks.** H-A1's difference minus the same difference under potion, the embedding stranger held out of P's population. The picks are the same, so the two differ only in the scoring guesser's family.

95% intervals by bootstrap over the test boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0, `c4score.bootstrap`). The p-value for Holm is twice the share of resamples on the wrong side of 0. H-A1 holds if Holm rejects it and the difference is above 0. H-A2 holds if Holm rejects it and the difference is below 0.

A board enters the primary metrics only if both guessers answered the P and F prompts (the same prompt when P and F are the same action). The number of boards dropped is reported.

### Secondary metrics

Reported with intervals and without significance claims:

- P − F for each guesser on its own, and words found (not value) for each.
- Oracle − F and oracle − P under the language models. Also the order of F, P and the oracle under each kind of guesser (embedding, human-fitted, EAT, SWOW, each language model). This shows whether the ranking changes with the family.
- P − F under the strangers by C10's definition (the 0.24 of C10), on these boards.
- The share of boards where P and F are the same action, and where they share a clue word. Also the mean count of each arm (in C10, 1.93 for P against 2.49 for F) and the assassin rate of each arm under each guesser. A lead that comes from lower counts would show here.
- **Repeat check.** Every distinct P, F and oracle prompt of the first 100 test boards, sent twice more to each guesser. Reported: the share of prompts whose played-out value changed, and H-A1's difference recomputed on those boards from each repeat.
- **Replies.** For each guesser, the number that came back empty, that failed after the retries, that named no board word, and that were cut off at the token cap.
- **The part B items.** Both guessers play the 80 items of part B once each, so that people and models can be compared on the same items.

### Part A, power

The minimum detectable effect is the true difference that the test would detect 80% of the time. It is computed from C10's crossguess replies (`codenames/results/c10.json`). Each played-out P − F difference there has a standard deviation of 1.59 (Llama 3.3) and 1.46 (DeepSeek-V3.2). The two guessers' differences correlate at 0.04, so most of the variation is the draw of one reply, not the board. Their mean has a standard deviation of 1.10 across boards. The difference behind H-A2 (language models minus strangers) has 1.10 as well.

At Holm's stricter level for the first test (two-sided 0.025), the detectable effect is about 3.08 × 1.10 / √n:

| Test boards | Detectable P − F |
|---:|---:|
| 300 | 0.20 |
| 600 | 0.14 |
| 1,000 | 0.11 |

At 1,000 boards a lead of 0.11, under half of the embedding lead of 0.24, is detected with 80% power, and so is a drop of 0.11 from the embedding lead. Smaller ones are reported as unresolved. The standard deviation of gpt-oss-120b's replies is not known yet; the pilot and the repeat check measure it, and the report gives the detectable effect recomputed from the test data.

## Part B: a human pilot

### Design

- **Boards.** The first 40 boards of seed 11010 on which P and F name different clue words. On the others a person would see the same clue twice. 11 of the first 51 boards were skipped for this; C10's boards had the same clue word in both arms on 18% of boards. So the pilot estimates P − F on boards where the clues differ. Without the skip, the estimate would be about 0.8 times as large, if a board with a shared word contributes little.
- **Items.** 80 items, one per board and arm. Each shows the 25 words, the clue and its count, and nothing that names the arm. Item ids are random hex strings.
- **Order.** Two blocks of 40. Each block shows every board once; block 2 gives each board the clue it did not get in block 1. In block 1 of version A, 20 boards show P and 20 show F, chosen by the order seed 11011. Version B shows the same boards in the same order with the other clue at every position. The block orders are shuffled by the same seed, and block 2 never starts with the board that ends block 1. Volunteers are given versions A and B in turn.
- **Play.** A volunteer opens `codenames/results/human-pilot/play.html` on a phone. Each screen shows the 25 words in a 5 × 5 grid, with the clue and count above. They tap words in the order they would guess them, up to the count, and tap Done when they would stop. That can be with fewer picks or none. Undo removes the last pick. No feedback is given, so no team word is revealed before the end. The page asks a person to guess each board on its own in block 2.
- **Score.** Each item is scored by `c4.play_turn` on the person's picks, as every other guesser here is: up to the count, ending at the first word that is not the team's, with the same costs (`human_pilot.turn_value`, `human_pilot.score_response`).
- **Volunteers.** Adults who agree to play, about 4 to 8. There is no payment and no ethics review: no name, contact detail or other personal datum is asked, and the page says what is kept before the first screen.

### What is collected and where it is stored

- **Collected per item:** the item id, the picks in order, the milliseconds from the screen's start to each pick and to Done, and the number of undos. **Per session:** the version letter, a random session code made on the phone, the date (no time of day), and an optional free-text comment at the end, which asks for no names. Nothing else: no name, contact detail, device or location.
- **On the phone:** the page makes no network request. Answers are kept in the browser's local storage so that a session can be resumed, and at the end they are shown as one block of JSON text with Share, Copy and Download buttons.
- **Storage:** the volunteer sends that text to the coordinator. The coordinator saves it unchanged as `runs/c11/human-pilot/responses/<session>.json` in the private dataset `arclabs561/games-runs`, with its SHA-256. The text arrives by message, email or file from a person the coordinator knows, so the coordinator can tell who sent which file. The saved file holds no name, and the coordinator deletes the message it came in once the text is saved; the session code is what links a file to a volunteer who asks to withdraw. (Corrected on 2026-11-20; the first text said that the coordinator keeps no record of who sent which file, which the way the text is sent does not allow.)
- **In git, after the pilot:** `codenames/results/human-pilot/summary.json`, with every scored item (session numbered 1, 2, … in the order received; board, arm, block, picks, value, seconds) and the summary statistics. Comments are not committed. They stay in the private dataset, and the README reports only how many there were and, in a sentence, what they were about.

### Primary metric and test

The pilot is exploratory by the method's rule, since it is a pilot. Its analysis is fixed here all the same.

- **Primary.** For each board, the mean over the sessions that answered both its items of value(P item) − value(F item). Then the mean over boards. 95% interval by bootstrap over boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0).
- **Secondary:**
  - the same from block 1 alone, between versions A and B, which has no carryover from block 2;
  - block × arm, as a check on carryover;
  - a two-way bootstrap that resamples sessions and boards;
  - words found per arm, the share of items stopped before the count, the assassin rate, and seconds per item;
  - the spread of a person's turn values, for the power of a later study;
  - agreement of people with gpt-oss-120b and Llama 3.3 on the same items: the share of items with the same first pick, and P − F under each model on these 80 items;
  - the exact values of the 80 items under the embedding strangers, the human-fitted guesser and EAT.

A result whose interval holds 0 is reported as unresolved, not as no effect.

### Part B, power

From the same C10 replies: one played-out turn's P − F has a variance of about 2.34 (words per turn, squared), of which about 0.09 is shared by both guessers. So most of it is the draw of one play, and little is the board. With m people who answer both items of all 40 boards, the standard error of the primary metric is about √((0.09 + 2.25 / m) / 40), and the effect detectable with 80% power (two-sided 0.05) is 2.80 times that:

| People | Detectable P − F |
|---:|---:|
| 3 | 0.41 |
| 5 | 0.33 |
| 8 | 0.27 |
| 12 | 0.23 |

These figures assume that people vary about as much as the language models do; the pilot measures how much they do. Block 1 alone gives about half the pairs and a larger detectable effect. The embedding lead is 0.24 on all boards and roughly 0.29 on boards where the clues differ. **A handful of people therefore gives an underpowered pilot.** With five people it detects a lead only somewhat larger than the embedding lead, and it cannot tell a lead of 0.1 from none. About 11 people would be needed to detect a lead of 0.24, and about 7 for 0.29.

## Stopping rules and a null result

- **Pilot (part A).** It checks the providers, the reading of replies and the cost per call. Nothing from it is scored. A fault it finds is fixed in an amendment before the test.
- **Failed calls.** A call that fails five times or comes back empty is sent again once at the end of its job. If more than 5% of a guesser's test replies are still failed after that, its secondary results are reported with that share. The primary metrics then use the boards on which every needed reply was answered, as stated above, and are labeled with the number of boards.
- **The test** is sent once. A job that fails is rerun once, from the start, and only the rerun is scored. How many calls the failed job sent is recorded.
- **Part B** has no stopping rule on its result. It ends when the volunteers who agreed have played, or after four weeks. Partial sessions are kept and enter the block-1 analysis for the blocks they finished.
- **A null result.** If H-A1 fails, the README states that P's lead over F with embedding strangers (0.24 in C10) was not found with two hosted language-model guessers, and gives the difference and its interval. If H-A2 holds, it states that the lead is smaller with guessers outside the embedding family, by how much, and that the giver sections' claims hold for embedding guessers. If the pilot's interval holds 0, the README says that the pilot could not resolve the question, with the people's variance and the number of people a full study would need.

## Threats to validity

- **The language models are simulations too.** They are not people, and they may share habits with each other that people lack. Part B is there for this reason.
- **Models never stop early.** They pick up to the count, as `c4.play_turn` plays them. People can stop, and in part B they do so without feedback, unlike the real game. A count of 1, which P gives more often than F, is safer for any guesser.
- **One sample per prompt.** Each played-out value is one draw from a nondeterministic provider. The repeat check measures how much it varies.
- **Carryover in part B.** Each person sees every board twice, so block 2 is guessed with memory of block 1. The block-1 analysis has no carryover, and the block × arm check measures it.
- **The volunteers.** A few adults who agreed to play are not a sample of Codenames players. They play on a phone, alone, with no time limit.
- **The words** are this folder's 400 nouns, not the published game's cards, and the clues come from a 12,000-word list.
- **Pinned providers** can change the model behind a name without notice. Each call's log records the provider and the time.
- **Contamination.** Cultural Codes and Codenames strategy guides may be in the models' training data. That does not favor P or F.

### 2026-11-19, part B's clues: no form of a board word

This amendment was written before any volunteer was given the page. No human response exists, and no part A call has been sent.

- **The fault.** The rulebook (Czech Games Edition, English rules of July 2015) says: "You can't say any form of a visible word on the table" and "You can't say part of a compound word on the table", with snowshoe as a forbidden clue for HORSESHOE. The clue filter of every experiment here (`words.legal`) only forbids a board word and a clue that contains one or is contained in one. Of the 80 items first generated for part B, 14 broke the rule (residency with RESIDENCE on the board, twice; librarian and LIBRARY; winning and WINNER; owned and OWNER; governance and governing with GOVERNOR; teaching and TEACHER; actress and ACTOR; hiding and HIDE; countess and COUNTER; railway and RAILROAD, and railroad and RAILWAY; restroom and BATHROOM), and 4 more were borderline (telecom with TELEPHONE, twice; webcam with CAMERA; regiment with REGION). They were on 16 of the 40 boards. A person who sees residency with RESIDENCE on the board would rightly read the clue as pointing at that word, so these items measure the filter, not the giver.

Its check against C10's committed picks runs without the filter, since C10 ran without it, and still passes. Seeds, the skip rule, the orders and the page are unchanged. `key.json` records the filter under `giver.clue_filter`.
- **The new materials.** `human_pilot build` was run again. The first 40 boards of seed 11010 on which P and F name different clues are now these 40 of the first 53; 13 were skipped (11 before). 22 boards keep both clues, 16 keep the board with one clue or both changed, and 2 are new (seed indices 1 and 52). The old `key.json` is kept as `codenames/results/diagnostics/pilot-before-key.json`, and `codenames/results/diagnostics/legality.md` lists its flagged items.
- **What this does to the comparison with part A and C10.** Part A, as planned, uses C10's arms unchanged, so its P and F can name forms of board words. Under the strict check, C10's P (the potion task's pick) does so on 28% of test boards and F on 7% (`legality.md`). Part B's items now come from a different, stricter candidate list. Part B's P − F is therefore not the same quantity as part A's, and the report says so beside both. Whether part A adopts the filter is decided in a further amendment before its pilot.

The proposer shortlists its 30 clues from that pool, and the picks are made again from the exact values, as `diagnose refilter` and `human_pilot.giver_picks` do (`c11.board_lists(board, strict=True)`). `c11 lists` checks that its picks equal `human_pilot.giver_picks` on the first three boards of each seed, with and without the filter, and that without it they equal C10's committed picks. H-A1 and H-A2, their test, Holm's correction and the rule that drops a board are unchanged and apply to these arms.
- **The original arms, a preregistered secondary.** The plan's arms, with C10's filter (`words.legal` alone), on the same boards and sent to the same guessers. Reported: H-A1's and H-A2's differences for these arms with intervals and without a significance claim, and the per-board difference of H-A1's quantity between the strict and the original arms. This keeps the comparison with C10's 0.025 (−0.095 to 0.153) possible, with the caveat that C10's guessers were Llama 3.3 and DeepSeek-V3.2. A prompt that both lists share (same board, clue and count) is sent once and its reply is used for both. On the test boards, F is the same in both lists on 839 of 1,000 boards and P on 672. There are 2.05 distinct strict actions per board and 2.54 over all six arms.
- **Minimum detectable effect** (METHOD.md, amendment of 2026-11-20). The 20 pilot boards are too few to estimate the standard deviation of a board's difference, so the planning value is the plan's 1.10 for both H-A1 and H-A2, from C10's replies. With n = 1,000 boards it is 2.80 × 1.10 / √1000 = 0.097 at the two-sided 5% level, and 3.08 × 1.10 / √1000 = 0.107 at Holm's first level of 2.5%. The effect worth finding is half of the embedding lead. With strict clues, the strangers' lead on C10's test boards was 0.215 (`diagnose refilter`), so half is 0.11. The test is powered for that, at the Holm level, and not for smaller effects. Boards dropped for missing replies raise the detectable effect a little. The report gives the detectable effect recomputed from the test's standard deviation, and a null result is stated with it.
- **The guessing code.** The guessers run through `python -m codenames.c11 guess` and `c11 repeat`, not `rerank crossguess`. The prompt (`llm.guesser_prompt` with the action's own count), the reading (`c4.read_ranking`), the play (`c4.play_turn`), the models, providers and token caps (gpt-oss-120b at 1,024 with reasoning effort low, Llama 3.3 at 256), temperature 0, seed 0, and one send per distinct (board, clue, count) are those of the plan. What changes: `c4.crossguess` plays an empty or failed reply in the board's printed order, and its output cannot tell such a reply from an answer. The plan's drop rule needs to tell them apart. `c11` records the state of each reply: answered (ok, or cut off at the token cap after naming board words), empty, failed (five failed tries), unpinned (every try served by another provider) or naming no board word. Empty, failed and unpinned replies are sent once more at the end of the job, as the stopping rules say. A board enters the primary metrics when each guesser's final reply to the P and F prompts is answered. A reply that names no board word counts as not answered, so that no turn is played in an order the guesser did not give. Sends of the repeat check are not sent again. Calls pin their provider with the router's suffix (`codenames.hosted`), log the serving provider, model id and response id of each reply, and each job saves `quantizations.json` and `served.json`.
- **The pilot.** 20 boards of seed 11999 instead of 5, so that METHOD's repeat check at the start of validation has at least 20 prompts. That is 56 distinct prompts per guesser over the six arms, then the 41 strict prompts sent twice more. Before the test, the pilot is checked for four things. (a) No final reply came from another provider. (b) After the resend, replies that are empty, failed or name no board word are at most 5% per guesser. (c) gpt-oss-120b's replies cut off at its cap are at most 5%; if more are, the test uses a cap of 2,048 tokens, by this rule and without a further amendment. (d) The share of repeated prompts whose played-out value changed, and the median latency, are recorded. If (a) or (b) fails, part A stops, and a further amendment comes before any test call. Nothing from the pilot is scored.
- **The repeat check on the test.** The plan's check (every distinct strict prompt of the first 100 test boards, sent twice more to each guesser) runs in the job after the two test jobs. It is METHOD's repeat check for the test split. Reported per split (pilot, test, repeat): empty, failed, no-word and cut-off replies, median latency, and the providers that served. If these differ between the pilot and the test, the test result is also reported on the boards with no such reply; the primary metrics already use only those boards.
- **Part B's items.** Each test job also sends the 80 items of part B once (`--items`).
- **SWOW.** Not run: no machine that runs this plan holds a copy downloaded by hand. The report says so.
- **Jobs.** One at a time, on `cpu-basic`, through `experiments/hfrun.py` with the group `codenames`, at a commit of the branch `codenames/c11-a` that contains this amendment. Each is `python -m codenames.c11` with these arguments and `--out OUT`:

### 2026-11-20, part B: the page and the key, before anyone has played

No volunteer had played when this was written. The boards, items, clues, orders and the scoring are unchanged; `boards.json` is byte for byte the same.

- **What the page says.** Before the first screen it now says what the study is, that the text a volunteer sends is kept in a private Hugging Face dataset, that the scored answers (per screen: the number of picks, the team words found, the score and the time, under a session number, without words, session code, date or comment) will be published in this public repository, how to withdraw (send the session code shown at the end; if the answers were already published, a new version removes them, though earlier versions of the repository keep them), and that only people 18 or older should take part.
- **A shared phone.** On opening, a page that finds a stored session no longer shows it or continues it. It asks whether the session is the reader's own, and offers to continue it or to start fresh, which deletes it unseen. After the text is shared, copied or downloaded, it is deleted from the browser's storage and stays only on the screen. The result screen also has a Start fresh button.
- **Access.** Word buttons are made once per screen, so the keyboard focus stays on a word after it is picked; after Done, and on every new screen, the focus moves to the screen's heading (the clue). Picked words carry `aria-pressed` and a name such as "head, pick 1". The comment box and the result text have labels, the counter is a status region, and all text is at least 16 px (1rem), also at 320 px wide, where long words are hyphenated.
- **The key.** `key.json` (the arm of each item) is no longer committed beside the page. `human_pilot build` writes it to `codenames/.cache/human-pilot/key.json`, which git ignores. This only keeps a volunteer who opens the folder from reading the arms off a file. It is not a secret: the arm of each item id follows from the public order seed (`human_pilot.orders`), so `human_pilot.key` rebuilds the key from `boards.json` and checks it against the SHA-256 that `boards.json` holds, and the earlier key stays in the repository's history. A blinding that holds against someone who reads the code would need an order seed that is not published, and new materials.
