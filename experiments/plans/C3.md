# C3. Codenames: a giver trained against a population, across seeds and held-out partners

## Question

Does a clue giver trained by policy gradient against several guessers score more with a guesser it never trained with than a giver trained against its own model? The question is asked across five training seeds and with every other model in turn as the held-out guesser.

## Why it matters

Section 5 of `codenames/README.md` reports that population training raises the value with a held-out guesser from 0.75 to 0.92 (potion, bge held out) and from 0.43 to 0.73 (Qwen3, EmbeddingGemma 2 held out). Both numbers come from one seed and one held-out model, chosen when the code was written. The improvement could depend on that seed or on which model happened to be left out.

- If it holds across seeds and held-out models, section 5's claim becomes a measured effect with an interval, and the result supports C5's design: a population reward for a language-model giver.
- If it holds for some held-out models and not others, the README reports which, and the claim narrows.
- If the mean effect is within noise, section 5 is reported as a single-seed observation that did not replicate.

The headline is the score with held-out partners, not the gap. A gap can shrink because self-play gets worse, and section 5 shows that population training does cost 0.57 in self-play.

## Design

- **Givers.** potion and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, as in section 5.
- **Tasks.** For each giver, each of the other seven embedding models is held out in turn. The population is the remaining six. That makes 2 × 7 = 14 tasks. The EAT association guesser is held out in every task and stands in for people.
- **Policies per task and seed**, one factor apart: start (maximize expected words found, no training), self-play (population = the giver's own model), population, and population with the Deshpande et al. reward (an ablation of the reward).
- **Fixed settings**, the defaults of `rl.py`: shortlist 30 clues times counts 1 to 4, 300 training boards, 400 Adam steps of 16 boards, learning rate 0.05, the seven features.
- **Common random numbers.** The 200 test boards are the same for every task, seed and policy. They are dealt from a board seed that no earlier run used. The training seed changes the training boards, the minibatch order and the sampled guessers, and the four policies of one seed share them.
- **τ and templates** come from `results/calibrate.json` as committed (the published split). They describe the guessers, not the giver, and every arm uses the same ones, so C1's split does not change the comparison. C3 therefore does not wait for C1.

## Primary metric and test

For each task, seed and test board: the exact expected turn value (opponent word −1, assassin minus the team words left) under the held-out embedding guesser, population policy minus self-play policy. Paired by board and seed. The primary statistic is the mean over the 14 tasks of the mean over seeds and boards.

95% interval: a stratified bootstrap. Each resample draws 200 test boards with replacement, shared across all tasks to keep the pairing. Within each task it draws five seeds with replacement. 10,000 resamples, seed 0. Population training is said to help with held-out guessers if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- The same difference for the EAT guesser.
- The self-play cost: own-model value, population minus self-play.
- Per task: the mean difference and its interval, in a 2 × 7 table, so a reader sees which held-out models drive the result.
- The interquartile mean over the 70 runs and the probability of improvement (the share of task and seed pairs in which population beats self-play), as in Agarwal et al. (2021).
- The Deshpande et al. reward arm against the population arm, on held-out and EAT value.
- The learned θ per seed, to see whether the cautious policy (high weight on the margin feature) recurs.

## Sample size

In section 5 the two single-seed differences were 0.17 and 0.30, with standard errors over 200 boards of about 0.04 to 0.06 for each policy. The paired difference has a smaller standard error, since board difficulty cancels. Averaging 14 tasks and five seeds, the half-width of the primary interval should be a few hundredths if seeds vary as little as boards do, and about 0.1 if seeds vary as much as the policies. An effect of 0.1 or more is then detected. Five seeds per task is the number Henderson et al. (2018) showed can still mislead when compared run against run. Here they are pooled over 14 tasks, which Agarwal et al. show narrows the interval.

## Seeds

Training seeds 1 to 5 (seed 0 is the exploratory run in the README). Test-board seed 3003, and training-board seeds derived from the training seed as `rl.py` does now. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

CPU. One run of `rl.py` (four policies) takes 3 to 5 minutes here, so 70 runs take 4 to 6 hours on two cores. They run on GitHub Actions as a matrix over task and seed, on the branch `compute/codenames-c3`, with actions pinned to full SHAs as in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. The Qwen3 and other Hugging Face vectors come from `compute/codenames-embed`. Cost $0; cap 70 runner jobs of at most 20 minutes each, and the matrix is not rerun more than once.

## Entry points

`rl.py` exists. To be changed:

- `--test-seed` (default `seed + 10_000`, which keeps the README's numbers) so that test boards stop depending on the training seed.
- `--held-out` already takes one model; the population is every model except the giver, the held-out one and EAT, as now.
- `--per-board`: write each policy's value per test board and guesser, not only mean and standard error, so the bootstrap can pair.
- Launch: `uv run python -m codenames.rl --giver potion --held-out bge --seed 1 --test-seed 3003 --per-board --out OUT/potion-bge-1.json`, once per task and seed.
- `python -m codenames.c3 --runs <folder> --out codenames/results/c3.json` (to be written) computes the primary and secondary metrics with `codenames/stats.py` (written for C1).

## Outputs

- In git: `codenames/results/c3.json`, with per-board values for the held-out and EAT guessers, all 70 θ vectors and the summary, a few hundred kilobytes. Also the 2 × 7 table in the README.
- In the private dataset: nothing; everything is small and derived from public inputs.

## Stopping rules and a null result

All 70 runs are launched together, and the analysis runs only after all finish. A run that fails is rerun once. If it fails again, the task is reported with fewer seeds, and the bootstrap uses the seeds that exist. If the primary interval includes 0, the README replaces section 5's claim with the measured mean and interval and keeps the seed-0 numbers as the exploratory run they were.

## Threats to validity

- **Same family of partners.** Held-out guessers are other embedding models, and the EAT guesser answers only EAT cues. Neither is a person. The claim is about strangers of this kind.
- **Tuned on the defaults.** The learning rate and step count were chosen while writing section 5, by looking at the potion and bge task. That task is one of the 14 here and is not dropped.
- **Shared τ.** The guessers' temperatures were fitted on human data with the leaky split. A change in τ would change every arm alike, which is why the paired difference is the primary metric.
