# C8. Codenames: distilling a language model's guesses into a small embedding model

## Question

Does adding about 19,000 turns labeled by gpt-oss-120b to C2's human training turns raise the human-guess score of Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B with LoRA above C2's, on C1's test turns?

## Why it matters

Three guessers sit within noise of each other on C1's 551 test turns: gpt-oss-120b untrained at 0.650, the potion map at 0.632 and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B with LoRA at 0.626 (C2, C4). The two trained ones learned from the same 4,820 human turns. C2 left open whether more data would help, and Cultural Codes has no more. A hosted language model can label as many turns as the budget allows. It agrees with people 65% of the time, more often than any embedding model untrained. So its labels might carry what people do and are cheap to get.

- If the teacher's turns raise the student, the bottleneck for the embedding guessers was data, and a 0.6B encoder can run as a human model at a fraction of a language model's cost. C6 and C7 then gain a better human-fitted scoring guesser.
- If they do not, either the student cannot hold more than it already does or the teacher's errors cancel its signal. The secondary arms below separate the two.
- C9 asks the same question of a small language model. This plan is cheaper and runs first.

## Method, as of 2026-11-19

This is off-policy distillation through hard labels: the student never generates, so on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024; the 2026 survey of Song and Zheng, arXiv:2604.00626) does not apply to it. Inference Providers do not return the teacher's probabilities over the board words, so the label is the teacher's first-ranked word. The loss is C2's, unchanged: sentence-transformers' MultipleNegativesRankingLoss with 7 negatives from the same board.

## Design

- **Teacher.** gpt-oss-120b through deepinfra, reasoning effort low, replies capped at 1,024 tokens, temperature 0, seed 0. These are C4's settings and prompt (`llm.guesser_prompt` with count 1). The label is the first board word of `llm.parse_ranking`.
- **Teacher turns.** For each of C1's 4,820 training turns, four new boards. Each word on the turn's board is kept or, with probability one half, replaced by a word drawn uniformly from the words that appear on boards of C1's training games and not already on this board. The clue stays the person's. That gives 19,280 turns whose clues are human and whose boards look like the game's. No word list or turn from val or test games is used. The seed of this resampling is 8000.
- **Arms**, all with C2's settings (rank 16, α 32, dropout 0.05, batch 64, one epoch, the query template on the clue side):
  - *A, human only.* C2's arm 4, seeds 1 to 3, as committed. C8 pairs with its per-turn hits and does not train it again, as C2 did with C1's arms.
  - *B, human plus teacher* (the main arm): the 4,820 human rows and the 19,280 teacher rows.
  - *C, teacher only* (secondary): the 19,280 teacher rows.
  - *D, relabeled* (secondary): C1's 4,820 training turns with the teacher's label in place of the person's.
- **Learning rate.** C2 chose 3e-4, the largest value in its grid. For arm B, which has five times as many rows, one choice between 1e-4 and 3e-4 is made by log-likelihood on C1's val turns with seed 1. The choice holds for arms B, C and D.
- **Temperature and template** are fitted as in C2: τ on train, template on val.

## Primary metric and test

Top-1 match with the person's word on C1's 551 test turns, arm B minus arm A, paired by turn. Each arm's per-turn hit is averaged over its three seeds before pairing. The 95% interval is C1's bootstrap over test games (10,000 resamples, seed 0). Distillation is said to help if the interval lies above 0.

## Secondary metrics

- Arm B minus gpt-oss-120b (C4's per-turn hits): does the student pass its teacher?
- Arm C minus arm A, and arm D minus arm A.
- Each arm's log-likelihood of the person's word, and the teacher's agreement with the person on the 4,820 training turns. Only the teacher's labels on training turns are compared with people; no test turn is labeled.
- The teacher's agreement with itself: 100 teacher turns sent twice more.
- **As a giver**, as in C2: arm B against arm A on the 200 boards of seed 2002, scored by the seven other embedding guessers.
- **As a scoring guesser for C6 and C7** (exploratory): C6's test clues scored by arm B, reported beside the potion map.

## Sample size

C1's test set gives a half-width of about 0.035 for a paired difference between two trained guessers (C2's primary interval was −0.041 to 0.028). A gain of 4 points or more is resolved. Teacher and student disagree with people on different turns, so the gain could be smaller, and the report will say if it is unresolved.

## Seeds

LoRA seeds 1, 2 and 3 for arms B, C and D; arm A is C2's seeds 1 to 3. Board resampling seed 8000. Bootstrap seed 0.

## Compute and cost

| Part | Where | Estimate |
|---|---|---:|
| 24,100 teacher calls (19,280 new turns, 4,820 training turns) | Inference Providers, from `cpu-basic` | $1.00 to $2.00 |
| Learning rate, 2 jobs on validation | `a10g-small` | $0.30 |
| 9 test jobs (arms B, C, D at 3 seeds) | `a10g-small` | $1.20 |

C4 measured $0.00002 to $0.00006 per gpt-oss-120b call at these settings; the estimate allows up to $0.00008. C2's jobs took 2.7 to 6.9 minutes, the longest with 28,333 rows, and arm B has 24,100. Expected $2.50 to $3.50; hard cap $5.50. The teacher job stops at $2.50 by the cost meter and keeps the turns labeled so far, in the fixed order of training turns. Each GPU job has a timeout of 15 minutes, and there are at most 12, so the GPU part cannot pass $3. No Docker image: the `codenames-gpu` group already runs on `a10g-small`.

## Entry points

To be written:

- `codenames/distill.py`: `resample_boards(turns, vocab, k=4, seed=8000)`, and `python -m codenames.distill label --out OUT`, which runs in the job and writes `teacher.jsonl`.
- `--sources teacher|cultural+teacher|relabeled` and `--teacher FILE` on `codenames.lora`.
- `python -m codenames.c8 --runs <folder> --out codenames/results/c8.json`.
- Tests: `resample_boards` never uses a val or test word list and keeps every board at its original size; `lora.rows` with the new sources gives the expected counts.

## Outputs

- In git: `codenames/results/c8.json` and a README section. The teacher's labels are not committed: they are derived from Cultural Codes, whose license is not stated, and from provider outputs.
- In the private dataset, under `runs/c8/`: teacher prompts and replies, vectors, adapters and per-turn scores.

## Stopping rules and a null result

If the learning rate chosen for arm B gives a validation log-likelihood below C2's arm 4 at seed 1, the arm is reported as failed without a test run. If the primary interval includes 0, the README reports that about 19,000 teacher turns did not raise the student at this setting, with the arms C and D results to show whether the teacher's labels or the student's capacity is the limit.

## Threats to validity

- **The teacher may have seen the data.** Cultural Codes has been public since 2023. If gpt-oss-120b learned its turns, its labels on training turns carry a leak from people, not only from its own sense of words. The new boards reduce this but do not remove it.
- **Synthetic boards.** A person's clue was chosen for the original board, so on a resampled board the clue may point at nothing. The teacher still names a word. Those turns teach the student the teacher's guess, not a person's.
- **One student, one loss.** Only C2's configuration is tried.

## Amendments

### 2026-11-19, before any run

Written after the plan was approved with its cap of $5.50, before the pilot. The question, the arms, the primary metric and the test are unchanged.

- **Replacement words.** A replacement is drawn only from the training-board words against which the person's clue is legal under `words.legal` (not the clue, not containing it, not contained in it). Without this, a resampled board could hold the clue itself, and the teacher would name it. 140 of the 4,820 training turns already break that rule in their own words; those words are kept as the data has them.
- **Replies that name no board word.** Such a reply gives no label, and its turn is left out of the training rows. C4's rule, the first board word, would make the label the board's first word rather than the teacher's choice. The number of such turns is reported.
- **Order and time limit of the labeling job.** The calls go in this order: the 19,280 resampled turns in the order of the training turns, then the 4,820 training turns, then the repeat check (the first 100 resampled turns, twice more). The job makes no new call after 150 minutes, under a job timeout of 3 hours, so that what it has labeled is stored when it stops. Its cost cap is $2.50, as planned.
- **Repeat check.** It counts the turns whose label changed at least once, and separately those whose reply text changed.
- **Pilot.** 64 resampled turns at resampling seed 8999, not 8000, with a cap of $0.05, to measure the time per call with 32 calls in flight. Nothing from it is used.
- **Teacher file.** The GPU jobs download `teacher.jsonl` from the private dataset and check its SHA-256 against the labeling job's `label.json`.
- **Learning rate.** The two choice jobs are arm B at seed 1 with `--evaluate val`. The stopping rule compares the chosen rate's validation log-likelihood with C2's arm 4 at seed 1, −1.520 (`results/c2-lr.json`).
- **Scoring guesser for C6 and C7.** C6 has not run, so this exploratory secondary is not part of C8.
