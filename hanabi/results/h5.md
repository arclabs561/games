# Plan H5: three players, the reply cap fixed

gpt-oss-120b in three-player self-play on 30 seeded deals, against the information strategy on the same deals and each deal's open-game ceiling. Confirmatory, with one dated amendment (the test job's cap) written before any game. Every number below is in [`h5.json`](h5.json), which `python -m hanabi.h5 report` wrote from the run's records.

## What was found

The model scored 11.47 points below the information strategy (9.67 to 13.47), with the plan's official scoring. The strategy scored more on every one of the 30 deals. The interval runs below 10 and above 3, so by the plan's rule the result is reported as it is: 30 deals do not place the gap above 10 points, and the detectable effect is 2.75 points.

The cap fix worked. 2 of 1,941 replies reached the 16,384-token cap, and a game had 0.23 fallback moves on average, against 15.0 per game in H2's two-player arm at 4,096 tokens. Every life lost was lost on the model's own move, not on a fallback.

## The numbers

Three players, seeds 0 to 29 of `rules.seeded_deck(Rules(players=3), seed)`. Every deal has ceiling 25, by `upper_bound.verdict`. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals over deals (10,000 resamples, seed 0), and Wilson intervals for shares.

| | Mean | 95% interval |
|---|---:|---|
| **Gap to the information strategy (primary)** | 11.47 | 9.67 to 13.47 |
| Gap to the ceiling | 14.3 | 12.6 to 16.23 |
| Gap to the information strategy, scored at the moment of failure | 9.8 | 8.9 to 10.67 |
| Model's score, official (0 on the third life) | 10.7 | |
| Model's score at the moment of failure | 12.37 | |
| Information strategy on these deals | 22.17 | |

- The standard deviation of the per-deal gap is 5.38, near the plan's planning value of 6, so the detectable effect is 2.80 × 5.38 / √30 = 2.75 points.
- No game was perfect (0 of 30; Wilson 0% to 11.4%). The highest score was 16. Five games lost all three lives (Wilson 7.3% to 33.6%) and score 0 officially; at the moment of failure they had 7 to 12 points on the table. The 25 games that kept a life averaged 12.84.
- The information strategy scored 22.17 on these 30 deals, against 22.254 ± 0.032 on 10,000 seeded deals ([information.md](information.md)); its gap to the ceiling here is 2.83.

## Behavior

From replaying every game in `rules.py`:

- 1,941 moves: 866 hints, 652 discards and 423 plays, of which 52 were misplays. All 52 lives were lost on the model's own moves; fallbacks lost none.
- 38.0% of hints were followed by the hinted player's next move playing a card the hint touched.
- In three games one seat never played a card: seat 0 on deals 5 and 28, seat 1 on deal 18.
- Turns per game 64.7; completion tokens per call 3,269. The test cost $1.67 by the meter, $0.056 a game.

## Against H2, with care

H2's two-player primary arm, with the cap at 4,096, averaged 6.37 points with 15.0 fallback discards a game. Here, with three players and the cap at 16,384, the model averages 10.7 with 0.23 fallbacks. The player counts differ, so this does not measure the cap's effect alone. It is consistent with the plan's second reading: the fallbacks were a large part of H2's two-player losses, but with them gone the model still plays far below a fixed convention.

## The repeat check

20 prompts from the pilot and 20 from the first test games were sent twice more, pinned to Novita, at temperature 0 and seed 0. All 80 calls were served by Novita and none was cut off.

- Pilot prompts: the move changed on 16 of 20 ($0.026).
- Test prompts: the move changed on 12 of 20 ($0.024).

So a replay of these games would not reproduce them move for move. The per-deal interval already covers the variation between deals; it does not cover the variation of a deal replayed.

The first run of the pilot's repeat check (job 6b00c04a) stopped with an error when the meter refused a call at its $0.05 cap. Its spending is not known, and is at most $0.05. The check was rerun with fewer workers and the error caught per call.

## The pilot

Two games at medium effort on deals 1000 and 1001, exploratory and not scored: 12 and 14 points, 0 of 136 replies cut off at the cap, one fallback. By the plan's rule (at most 5% cut off), the test used medium effort and 16,384 tokens. The pilot cost $0.127.

## Cost

By the meter (Novita's listed $0.05 and $0.25 per million input and output tokens, times reported tokens): pilot $0.127, test $1.670, repeat checks $0.026 and $0.024, and the failed first repeat at most $0.05. Total $1.85 to $1.90, against the amended cap of $3.45. The test job ran for 1.7 hours of `cpu-basic`.

## Limits

- Self-play of one model, a proxy for play with others (METHOD.md).
- The information strategy at three players is a generalization made here, not the published five-player version.
- One prompt, fixed and not tuned, as in H2.
- The repeat check shows that the provider does not return the same move twice for most prompts, even at temperature 0.

Per-call prompts and replies are in the private dataset under `runs/h5/`. Job ids and costs: [`h5-runs.json`](h5-runs.json).
