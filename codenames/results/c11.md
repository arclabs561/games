# The population pick with language-model guessers (plan C11, part A)

Detail behind the [Codenames README](../README.md). The original model results are in `c11.json`, written by `python -m codenames.c11 score` from `c11-lists.json` (the arms and their exact values) and `c11-guesses.txt` (every played-out turn). Jobs, commits and costs are in `c11-runs.json`.

## Did filling in missing guesses change the result?

The old scorer added board words in printed order when a model gave fewer guesses than the clue allowed. We replayed the original replies, checked every saved primary-arm score, then stopped each turn at the last word the model named. Both models answered both clues on 998 of 1,000 boards.

P is the clue and count chosen for a group of embedding guessers. F is the embedding giver’s own first choice. We scored both on the same boards. A positive difference favors P.

| Comparison | Corrected turn score difference | Paired 95% bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| P − F, averaged over the two language models (H-A1) | −0.02004 | −0.09569 to +0.05511 |
| That difference minus P − F for potion, the held-out embedding guesser (H-A2) | −0.24187 | −0.32380 to −0.15957 |

Either clue choice could have a small lead with the language models. Potion gained more from P. Both conclusions survive the scoring correction (10,000 paired board resamples, seed 0, Holm over both tests).

| Guesser | Pick | Answered | Turn reached an added word | Score changed |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gpt-oss-120b | P | 999 | 10 | 8 |
| gpt-oss-120b | F | 999 | 16 | 11 |
| Llama 3.3 70B | P | 1,000 | 34 | 22 |
| Llama 3.3 70B | F | 999 | 54 | 29 |

An added word can end a turn without changing its score. Counts are per pick. When P and F chose the same clue and count, one reply counted for both. The [replay summary](c11-replay.json) records the estimates, intervals and counts. The human pilot is still open.

## Findings with the historical scorer

- With hosted language-model guessers, C10's population pick P shows no detectable lead over the embedding giver's first choice F. On 998 of the 1,000 test boards, with every arm choosing among strictly legal clues, P − F is −0.023 (−0.101 to 0.054) words per turn under gpt-oss-120b and Llama 3.3 70B. The interval excludes leads above 0.054. The plan expected 80% power for a lead of 0.11 at Holm's level; with the standard deviation the test found, the detectable lead is 0.123, so the planned power was not quite reached.
- The lead shrinks under these language-model guessers. On the same boards and picks, P − F under potion, the embedding stranger held out of P's population, is 0.222 (0.177 to 0.266). The language models' difference minus potion's is −0.245 (−0.329 to −0.161), and Holm rejects 0.
- What changes is the count. P asks for 1.95 words on average, F for 2.56. Under the language models P finds about 0.21 fewer words per turn and ends on the assassin less often (0.5% against 1.5% to 1.8%). Under the exact guessers the safer count is worth more than the lost words; under the language models the two cancel.
- H-A2 changes more than the guesser's model family. The language models answer once at temperature 0, are close to deterministic and were scored with their rankings filled out to the clue count, while potion is scored as an exact Luce guesser whose τ was fitted to people's first picks. A lower count helps a noisier guesser more, so H-A2 is a difference between this language-model protocol and the fitted Luce guesser. Rescoring potion's P − F with a sharper τ, or as a greedy guesser, would separate the two; that was not done.
- C10's original arms, which allow forms of board words, give the same answer: −0.007 (−0.088 to 0.075).
- These are proxy results, as METHOD.md says of model partners. Part B, the pilot with people, has not been played.

## Design

As in `experiments/plans/C11.md` and its amendment of 2026-11-20, written and merged before any model call:

- **Boards.** 1,000 test boards of seed 11004 and 20 pilot boards of seed 11999, dealt by `c4.boards`.
- **Arms.** EmbeddingGemma 2 shortlists 30 clues, each with counts 1 to 4. F is the action its own Luce guesser values most. P is the action, among the 40 of the 10 kept clues, with the highest mean exact value over the six embedding strangers other than potion. The oracle is the kept action best for the mean of all seven. In the primary arms the shortlist comes from the clue words that `legality.strict_legal` allows. The original arms use C10's filter (`words.legal`) and are a preregistered secondary. `c11 lists` checked its picks against `human_pilot.giver_picks` and C10's committed picks on the first three boards of each seed.
- **Guessers.** gpt-oss-120b pinned to DeepInfra (reasoning effort low, 1,024 tokens) and Llama 3.3 70B Instruct pinned to Novita (256 tokens), temperature 0, seed 0. Each hears C4's guesser prompt with the action's count, once per distinct (board, clue, count), and the turn is played out from its order by `c4.play_turn`. An empty or failed reply is sent once more at the end of the job. A board enters the primary tests only if both models answered both the P and the F prompt.
- **Tests.** H-A1 is the mean over the two models of P − F. H-A2 is H-A1's difference minus potion's exact P − F on the same board. Both use a bootstrap over boards (10,000 resamples, seed 0) and Holm's correction at 0.05.

## What can be recovered from short replies?

On test board 896 the clue was **trooper, 2**. SOLDIER was a team word. ROOM, printed first on the board, was the assassin. Suppose the model answered “SOLDIER.” The old scorer filled in ROOM as the second guess and scored the turn −8. If it answered “SOLDIER, ROOM,” it also scored −8. The saved score fits both replies. Scoring only the words named gives +1 for the first reply and −8 for the second. The published turn score alone cannot distinguish the two replies.

The public scores bound the effect on the 998 paired test boards. Run `uv run --group codenames python -m codenames.c11_bounds` to reproduce these sample means from `c11-lists.json`, `c11-guesses.txt` and the original deal (seed 11004).

| Paired sample mean | Lowest possible | Historical | Highest possible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| H-A1, mean of the two models’ P − F | −0.241483 | −0.023046 | +0.210922 |
| H-A2, H-A1 minus potion’s P − F on the same boards | −0.463312 | −0.244876 | −0.010908 |

The named-word rule can change the P − F difference on 379 boards. On 138 of the 998, P and F sent the same clue and count and shared each model’s reply, so their difference stays zero. Either arm could lead under the two models on this sample. Even at the upper endpoint, their mean P − F falls short of potion’s on these boards. The bounds give no bootstrap interval or Holm decision for the corrected scores.

**How the bounds work.** For each answered prompt we allow every ordering of one or more distinct board words that reproduces its saved pair (team words found, turn value) under the old rule. The parser counts the first occurrence of each complete board word, ignoring case, repeats and unrelated tokens. A token-cap flag gives no count of named words. A reply naming enough words to finish the turn preserves the old value. The only other possible values come from replies that named exactly *k* team words, with 1 ≤ *k* < clue count, and stopped. For each such subset, we append the other words in printed order and keep *k* if the old score matches. The order of those first *k* team words cannot affect either score.

To reach the lower endpoint, take the lowest feasible P score and highest feasible F score for each distinct prompt. Reverse these choices for the upper endpoint. Shared prompts use one response. Both endpoints are attainable across all cells under this response class. For H-A2 we subtract potion’s fixed P − F on each retained board before taking the mean. No assumption about how often models gave short replies enters these bounds.

**Exact replay.** The committed turn scores omit the named words. If you have the two original test-reply logs, use their local paths in model order, GPT then Llama:

```sh
uv run --group codenames python -m codenames.c11 replay --test-logs GPT_LOG LLAMA_LOG
```

The replay checks the original scores before reporting the corrected bootstrap intervals and counts of turns that reached an appended word. It makes no model calls and prints only aggregates.

## Primary tests with the historical scorer

998 of 1,000 boards; on each of the other two, one model's reply named no board word.

| Hypothesis | Difference (95% interval) | Bootstrap p | Holm | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-A1: P − F, language models | −0.023 (−0.101 to 0.054) | 0.56 | does not reject | no |
| H-A2: H-A1 minus P − F under potion | −0.245 (−0.329 to −0.161) | < 0.0002 | rejects 0 | yes, the lead shrinks |

Minimum detectable effect, from the test's own standard deviation: H-A1's per-board difference has a standard deviation of 1.26 (the plan assumed 1.10), so the detectable lead is 0.112 at the two-sided 5% level and 0.123 at Holm's 2.5%. For H-A2 the standard deviation is 1.36, giving 0.121 and 0.133.

## Repeat check

The 210 distinct strict prompts of test boards 0 to 99, sent twice more to each model at temperature 0. gpt-oss-120b's played-out value changed in at least one repeat on 20 of 210 prompts (9.5%), Llama 3.3's on 6 (2.9%). In the pilot it was 3 of 40 and 0 of 41. H-A1's difference on those 100 boards is −0.180 (−0.430 to 0.100) from the test replies, −0.135 (−0.390 to 0.125) from the first repeat, and −0.130 (−0.350 to 0.100) from the second.

## Part B's items

Both models also guessed the 80 items of the human pilot once each, so that people and models can be compared on the same items once people have played. P − F over the 40 boards: gpt-oss-120b −0.225 (−0.450 to 0.000), Llama 3.3 −0.200 (−0.476 to 0.075). On those same 40 boards, potion's exact difference was +0.182 (−0.017 to +0.396). The two language models averaged −0.213 with the historical scorer. These are the comparisons to use when people play those boards. All 40 were chosen because P and F name different clues. The per-board differences and intervals are in [benchmark.json](human-pilot/benchmark.json).

## Not run

The optional SWOW guesser was not run: no machine that ran this plan holds a copy downloaded by hand, and its license forbids fetching it by script.

## Limits

- Each played-out value is one reply. The repeat check shows that a tenth of gpt-oss-120b's prompts can change value when sent again.
- The models never stop early; they pick up to the count. People can stop, and a lower count is safer for any guesser.
- The words are this folder's 400 nouns, not the published game's cards.
